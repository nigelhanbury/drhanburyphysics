Consider the below circuit, showing a capacitor and resistor connected in series. We can make use of phasors to determine the combined impedance of the circuit. Here, impedance is analogous to total resistance.

As the resistor and capacitor are connected in series, the current at any instant must be the same in both components.

It is important to remember that the current is in phase with the voltage across the resistor VR. Remembering that the current leads the capacitor voltage VC , by  π _ 2  radians, results in VR leading VC by  π _ 2  radians.

Vector Addition of Voltages

In order to determine the peak of the supply voltage, we must do a vector addition of the voltage phasors as they do not oscillate in phase. Using Pythagoras, V  0  2 = V  R0  2  + V  C0  2  .

We can also use V  0 = √ _ 2  × V  rms , V  R0 = √ _ 2  × V  Rrms and V  C0 = √ _ 2  × V  Crms to obtain V  rms  2  = V  Rrms  2  + V  Crms  2  .

The angle θ, can be calculated using tanθ =   V  C0  ___  V  R0   or tanθ =   V  Crms  ___  V  Rrms   .

In this particular case, we can see that VR leads V by θ radians or I leads V by θ radians.
