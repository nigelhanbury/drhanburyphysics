An LC circuit can also demonstrate simple harmonic motion. The circuit on the right, shows a fully charged capacitor that is isolated via an open switch.

When the switch is closed, the circuit will generate an alternating current with a natural frequency, f =  1 ____ 2π √ _ LC    .

Derivation of The Natural Frequency

We already know from Faraday, that a varying current throught an inductor will produce a varying magnetic flux linkage through the coil. This will in turn, induce an emf in such a direction, as to oppose the changing current.

Therefore, the induced emf can be written as ε = − L  dI _ dt  . This is commonly referred to as the back emf and is a consequence of Lenz’s Law.

Therefore, we can write − L  dI _ dt  = V , where V, represents the voltage across the capacitor.

Since V =  Q _ C  , we can write − L  dI _ dt  =  Q _ C  . Substituting for I =  dQ _ dt  , gives − L   d  2 Q ___ d t  2   =  Q _ C  . After rearranging,   d  2 Q ___ d t  2   = − (  1 _ LC  ) Q .

This has the exact same form as the equation that describes SHM. Therefore, we can compare to   d  2 Q ___ d t  2   = − ω  2 Q .

Therefore, we can write ω  2 =  1 _ LC  , and hence, ω =  1 __  √ _ LC    . Using ω = 2πf , leads to f =  1 ____ 2π √ _ LC    . This is the natural frequency of the circuit. Here, there is a constant periodic exchange of energy between the capacitor and inductor, where energy is stored within the electric field of the capacitor and the magnetic field of the inductor.
