Alternating Voltage Across an Inductor

Another common component used in AC circuits is the inductor. An inductor is simply an insulated coil of wire wound onto an iron core. Below, the diagram on the left, represents an inductor and its symbol. The diagram on the right, represents an AC source connected to an inductor. The unit for inductance L, is the Henry or H.

Referring back to unit 4.5 ‘Electromagnetic Induction’, we can use Lenz’s Law to explain how an inductor works. Consider the above circuit with an AC current I = I  0 sin (ωt) , passing through the inductor. Since the current through the inductor produces a magnetic field, there will exist a magnetic flux linkage with the coil of wire making up the inductor. As the current is continuously varying, the flux linkage will continuously vary, and hence, an emf will be induced across the inductor. The direction of the induced emf is such, as to oppose the changing current. Therefore, work will need to be done in order to move a unit charge through the inductor.

The voltage across the inductor can be shown to be V = L  dI _ dt  .
