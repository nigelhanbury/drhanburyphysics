Option A: AC Theory

Determining Resistance Using AC Voltage and Current

The resistance at any given instant in time is determined using R =  V _ I  . Therefore, R =   V  0 sin (ωt)  _______  I  0 sin (ωt)   =   V  0  __  I  0   .

We can also make use of the rms current and voltage using V  0 = √ _ 2  × V  rms and I  0 = √ _ 2  × I  rms .

Therefore, R =   √ _ 2  × V  rms   _   √ _ 2  × I  rms   =   V  rms  ___  I  rms   .

This leads onto the important result that when dealing with AC, the resistance can be calculated using the peak voltage and current or the corresponding rms values.

Key Formulae

I   rms    =   I  0  __  √ _ 2   

V   rms    =   V  0  __  √ _ 2   

R =   V  0  __  I  0  

R =   V  rms  ___  I  rms  

Example Problem

The UK national grid delivers electrical energy through a distributed network throughout the UK. This is known as the mains supply, when delivered to residential areas, such as our homes. The mains supply rms pd is 230V and is at a frequency of 50Hz.

Calculate the period of oscillation of the voltage waveform.

Calculate the peak voltage.

If the mains supply is connected to a 50Ω heating element, calculate the rms current.

Calculate the mean power dissipated in the element, and hence, the total energy dissipated in 10s.

Why would it not be valid to use the same method as above to calculate the energy dissipated in 5ms.

Solution

The period is given by T =  1 _ f  , which leads to T =  1 _ 50  = 20ms .

We can use V  0 = √ _ 2  × V  rms , which gives V  0 = √ _ 2  × 230 = 325V .

Using I  rms =   V  rms  __ R  , gives I  rms =  230 _ 50  = 4.6A .

The mean power is given by <P> = I  rms  2  R = 4.6  2  × 50 = 1.1 ×  10  3 W       .

Therefore, the total energy dissipated is given by E = 1.1 × 10  3 × 10 = 1.1 × 10  4 J , or 11KJ.

The mean power given by <P> = I  rms  2  R is based on the mean power over one cycle. 5ms is a fraction of the cycle time of 20ms, and therefore, is not a valid method.
