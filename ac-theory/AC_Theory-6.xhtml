Increasing the frequency of the AC supply will decrease the reactance XC, and hence, increase Irms and I0.

We can qualitatively explain the increase in current by inspecting the graphs on the right. The graph in blue shows how the charge varies with time when the frequency of the supply is increased. Point A, represents the gradient of the graph at a higher frequency. We can clearly see that this gradient is larger than that at point B. Since the gradient of the graph at these points represents the maximum rate of change of charge, and hence, current, it follows that the maximum current I0, increases for increasing frequency.

Increasing the capacitance C, of the capacitor will decrease the reactance XC, and hence, increase Irms and I0.

Again, we can qualitatively explain the increase in current by inspecting the graphs on the right. Since the maximum charge on the capacitor plates Q0, is equal to Q0 = CV0, it follows that the larger capacitance C, will hold a greater maximum charge. The larger capacitance is represented by the blue graph. The gradient at the time intercept can be seen to be greater for the larger capacitance, and hence, larger maximum current I0.

If the frequency of the AC supply is increased, and the capacitance remains unchanged, then the capacitor will fully charge and discharge in a shorter amount of time, and hence, the rms current must increase.

If the frequency of the AC supply is unchanged, and the capacitance increased, then the capacitor will reach a greater full charge in the same amount of time. Hence, the rms current must increase.
