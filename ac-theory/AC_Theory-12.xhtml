The mean power taken from the supply by the inductor is zero.

The graphs on the right should help clarify. We know that the power at any instant being transferred to the inductor is given by P = IV. By inspecting the graphs, we can see that there is a phase difference of  π _ 2  radians between the voltage V, and current I.

Let us look at the first quarter of a cycle, which is represented by the shaded area A. We can see that both V and I are positive, and so, the power must be positive and transferred to the inductor. In the next quarter cycle, represented by the shaded area B, V is positive and I is negative. This results in a negative power, which leads to power being transferred from the inductor back to the supply. Therefore, during each half cycle, and hence, a full cycle, there is no net power transfer to the inductor.

It is not obvious how power can be transferred to and from an inductor, as it was with a capacitor. By inspecting the graph, we can see that current increases from zero up to a maximum value, and hence, the magnetic field strength increases from zero up to a maximum value during the first quarter of the cycle. Here, the energy is stored in the magnetic field itself. During the second quarter of the cycle, the current decreases to zero, and hence, so does the magnetic field strength. Here, the energy stored in the magnetic field decreases, and is returned to the supply.

The mean power transferred to an inductor over one cycle is zero.
