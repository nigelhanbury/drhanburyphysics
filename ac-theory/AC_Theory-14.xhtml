Consider the below circuit, showing an inductor and resistor connected in series. As before, we can make use of phasors to determine the combined impedance of the circuit. Here, impedance is analogous to total resistance.

As the resistor and inductor are connected in series, the current at any instant must be the same in both components.

It is important to remember that the current is in phase with the voltage across the resistor VR. Remembering that the inductor voltage VL , leads the current by  π _ 2  radians, results in VL leading VR by  π _ 2  radians.

Vector Addition of Voltages

As before, we can use Pythagoras on the phasor diagram to determine the peak and rms supply voltages, V  0  2 = V  R0  2  + V  L0  2  and V  rms  2  = V  Rrms  2  + V  Lrms  2  .

The phase angle θ, can be determined using tanθ =   V  L0  ___  V  R0   or tanθ =   V  Lrms  ___  V  Rrms   .

In this particular case, we can see that V leads VR by θ radians or V leads I by θ radians.

For an RL network, the applied voltage leads the current.
