The quality factor Q, of an LCR circuit is a measure of the sharpness of the resonance peak. The sharper or narrower the peak, the greater the value of Q.

The sharpness of the resonance curve depends on the values of the components. The graphs below, show two resonance curves that correspond to two different values of the resistance R.

The quality factor Q, is determined using the following formulae evaluated at resonance, Q =   V  Crms  ____  V  Rrms   =   V  Lrms  ____  V  Rrms   or Q =   V  C0  ____  V  R0   =   V  L0  ____  V  R0   .

Remember, that at resonance, X  L = X  C , and so, the peak and rms voltages of VC and VL are equal at resonance.

Additionally, we can use the following formulae evaluated at resonance, Q =   X  C  __ R  =   X  L  __ R  .

Substituting X  L = 2πfL at resonance, Q =  2π f  0 L ___ R  . After substituting f  0 =  1 ____ 2π √ _ LC    , gives Q =  2πL _ R  ×  1 ____ 2π √ _ LC    .

This reduces to Q =  1 _ R  √ __   L _ C    .

Analysis of The Quality Factor Q

We can make use of Q =  1 _ R  √ __   L _ C    and f  0 =  1 ____ 2π √ _ LC    to fully describe changes to the resonance curve and the quality factor.

If the resistance R, increases, then the peak rms current will decrease as I  rms =   V  rms  __ R  , since the impedance, Z = R. We can also see from the graph and the equation, that Q decreases, since Q ∝  1 _ R  . This means that the peak becomes lower and broader. The resonant frequency remains unchanged as f0 is independent of R.

At the resonant frequency, the impedance is purely resistive. In other words, Z = R .

The equation Q =  1 _ R  √ __   L _ C    is excellent for analysing the quality factor but must be derived.
