Capacitance, C =  Q _ V  and is a measure of the capacitors ability to store charge.

The capacitance of a capacitor is the charge stored per unit pd across it.

Definition of Capacitance

The capacitance of a capacitor is the charge stored per unit pd across it.

Capacitance =  Charge on either plate  ___________  PD across plates 

Units: C V   − 1 or Farad (F).

The larger the capacitance, the greater the quantity of charge that a capacitor can hold for the same applied pd. Therefore, it can be used to measure the size of a capacitor.

Example Problem

A 2.0µF capacitor has a pd of 10V across it. Find the charge stored on the capacitor.

Solution

Using Q = CV = (2.0 ×  10   − 6 ) × 10 = 20 × 10   − 6 C , or 20µC.

Example Problem

Find the capacitance of a capacitor that stores 0.0010C of charge when the pd across it is 500V. Give your answer in F, µF or pF.

Solution

Using C =  Q _ V  =  0.0010 _ 500  = 2.0 × 10   − 6 C , or 2.0µC.

Example Problem

Find the average current needed to charge a 50µF capacitor so that its pd across it reaches 10V in 0.0010s.

Solution

Using Q = CV = (50 ×  10   − 6 ) × 10 = 5.0 × 10   − 4 C . Therefore, it takes 0.0010s to transfer 5.0 × 10   − 4 C to the capacitor.

The mean current is then given by I =  Q _ t  =  5.0 ×  10   − 4  _____ 0.0010  = 0.50A .
