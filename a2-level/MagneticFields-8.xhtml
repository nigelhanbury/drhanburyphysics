A charged particle moving within a magnetic field will experience a force.

The magnitude of this force F, is given by F = BQv ,

where B represents the magnetic field strength (or flux density), Q, the charge and v, the velocity of the charged particle.

In this situation, the velocity of the charged particle is perpendicular to the magnetic field.

The direction of the force F, is perpendicular to both the magnetic field B, and the velocity v. Since the force is perpendicular to the velocity, the charged particle must follow a circular trajectory, where the centripetal force is given by F = BQv .

Fleming’s Left Hand Rule can be used to predict the direction of the force. The conventional current, in this case, is represented by the direction of the velocity of a positive charge Q, as shown above. Note, since the force F, is perpendicular to the velocity v, no work is done on the charge, and therefore, no change in kinetic energy or speed. In other words, the charged particle will be deflected with no change in speed.

Radius of Curvature of Deflected Charge

Since the charged particle will follow a circular path and the centripetal force is given by F = BQv , the radius of the path can be determined.

We can write BQv =  m v  2  ___ r  , where m represents the mass of the charged particle, and r, the radius of curvature.

This results in r =  mv _ BQ  .

Using the above equation, it can be seen that if the same charged particle enters the same magnetic field with a greater velocity, the radius of the circular path will increase as r ∝ v .

Key points:

Magnetic force acting on a moving charged particle, F = BQv

Force perpendicular to velocity and magnetic field

Motion of particle is circular

No work done as force perpendicular to velocity
