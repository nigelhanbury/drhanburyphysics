The Gas Laws

Boyle’s Law

If we keep the temperature T, and the number of moles n, constant, then the equation of state reduces to

PV = constant

After rearranging, we can see that P ∝  1 _ V  .

In words, for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume. This is known as Boyle’s Law.

In the above example, pressure is applied via a foot pump, which compresses the air column, decreasing its volume. The volume of the air can be taken straight off the scale, and the pressure taken off the pressure gauge. These values can then be plotted as shown. Since P ∝  1 _ V  , doubling the pressure will half the volume of the air. By ensuring the pressure is changed slowly, and allowing the oil column to settle down in-between readings, allows the temperature to remain constant. This is because any surplus thermal energy in the column of air, due to the air being compressed too quickly, can be conducted away through the vessel walls.

P = nRT (  1 _ V  )

The graph to the right, represents a plot of pressure P, against  1 _ V  . In this case, we would expect a straight line through the origin as P ∝  1 _ V  .

Re-arranging the state equation, gives P = nRT (  1 _ V  ) . This shows that the gradient of the line is equal to nRT. As can be seen, increasing the temperature T, increases the gradient.

This is a useful plot, as information regarding the number of moles n, and temperature T, can be obtained.

Additionally, we can write

P  1 V  1 = P  2 V  2

Using PV = nRT , we can deduce the 3 gas laws. Note, that WJEC do not require knowledge of the gas laws, but to be aware of them.
