EMF induced in a Wire Moving Through a Magnetic Field using Faraday’s Law

Consider a wire of length L, moving with a velocity v, perpendicular to a magnetic field as shown to the right. Let Δt represent the time taken for the wire to pass through the field.

The shaded area A, represents the magnetic flux that the wire passes through in that time. The flux ɸ, is given by ɸ = BA .

The magnetic flux linkage is given by Nɸ = BAN . Since we are only dealing with a single wire, then N = 1 . Therefore, Nɸ = BA .

Since H = vΔt and A = HL , then A = vLΔt . We can now write Nɸ = BA = BLvΔt .

Using Faraday’s Law, the magnitude of the induced EMF ε, is given by ε =  Nɸ _ Δt  =  BLvΔt _ Δt  = BLv .

The induced EMF in a wire is given by ε = BLv .

EMF induced in a Wire Moving Through a Magnetic Field using Force on Moving Electrons

The diagram on the right shows a wire moving with a velocity, v through a magnetic field, B.

Therefore, each free electron within the wire is moving through the field with a velocity, v, and hence, will experience a magnetic force, F = evB . The direction of this force is acting vertically downwards using FLHR.

Since the magnetic force acting on a free electron moves through the length, L of the wire, work must be done.

Therefore, the work done is given by W = FL = (evB) L .

Since EMF is the work done per unit charge, it follows that ε =  W _ e  =  evBL _ e  = BLv .
