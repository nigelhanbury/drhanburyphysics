Relationship between Electric Field Strength and Potential

Consider a small area surrounding a small test charge q, as shown in the diagram to the right.

The electric field strength E =  F _ q  , is the force acting per unit positive charge. Note, that at this small scale, the equipotential lines can be considered parallel, indicating that the electric field strength E, can be considered uniform (constant) throughout this small area.

The work required to move this test charge a small distance ∆r, in the direction of increasing radius r, is equal to F  applied × ∆r , where, the applied force F  appled = − qE , since it must act in the opposite direction to E.

Here, the applied force, F  applied must act in the opposite direction to the electric force of attraction qE, but equal in magnitude.

The work done ∆W, will therefore be ΔW = − qEΔr .

The work done must then equal the increase in electrical potential energy Δ E  P , as the test charge has moved a small distance away from the centre of the charge.

Therefore, qΔ V  E = − qEΔr , which leads to the important result: E = −  Δ V  E  __ Δr  .

In other words, the electric field strength at a point is equal to the negative potential gradient at that point.

Using the above result E = −  Δ V  E  __ Δr  , we can determine the electric field strength E, at a point by making use of the graphs shown on the right.

For example, we can determine E at a distance of 2R from the centre of the charge by finding the gradient of the tangent at the corresponding point on the graph of VE against r.

In this case, the gradient (potential gradient) is positive for a negative charge and negative for a positive charge.

E is then determined by taking the negative gradient.
