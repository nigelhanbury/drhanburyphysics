If the current carrying wire is not perpendicular to the field, and at an angle θ, to the magnetic field as shown to the right, we can still make use of F = BIL .

Here, we must replace B with the component of B perpendicular to the wire B  P .

We can write B  P = Bsinθ , and use F = B  P IL , which results in F = (Bsinθ) IL .

This leads to the general equation, F = BILsinθ .

The above equation can be used to define magnetic flux density and the Tesla.

Magnetic Flux Density is the force exerted on 1m of conductor, carrying a current of 1A, at right angles to the magnetic field.

One Tesla is the magnetic flux density of a field, when a force of 1N acts on a 1m length of conductor, carrying a current of 1A, at right angles to the magnetic field

The force is maximum when the wire is perpendicular to the field (θ = 90°)

The force is zero when the wire is parallel to the field (θ = 0°)

Definition of Magnetic Flux Density

The force exerted on 1m of conductor, carrying a current of 1A, at right angles to the magnetic field.

Definition of One Tesla

The magnetic flux density of a field, when a force of 1N acts on a 1m length of conductor, carrying a current of 1A, at right angles to the magnetic field

Example Problem

The diagram shows a rectangular block of copper with a current of 6.0A running through it suspended in a magnetic field. Density of copper is 8.96 × 10  3 kg m   − 3 .

Draw the direction of the magnetic field lines so that the magnetic force acting on the wire opposes the weight.

Calculate the required magnetic flux density.

Solution

Magnetic flux density, B

Weight, mg

Magnetic force, F = BIL

Using Fleming’s left hand rule (FLHR), the direction of the magnetic flux density is perpendicular to the wire and acting out of the page as shown.

We can write BIL = mg , where m = 8.96 × 10  3 × (20 × 20 × L) × 10   − 9 .

Therefore, B × 6.0 × L = 8.96 × 10  3 × (20 × 20 × L) × 10   − 9 × 9.81

This gives B = 5.86 × 10   − 3 T = 5.86mT .
