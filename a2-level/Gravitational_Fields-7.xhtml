Example Problem

Charon is one of the moons of Pluto. The radius of Charon is 606km, and that of Pluto is 1190km. A 20kg mass is projected towards Charon from the surface of Pluto at a velocity of 1400ms − 1.

Determine:

The gravitational potential energy of the mass at the surface of Pluto

The gravitational potential energy of the mass at the surface of Charon

The speed at which the mass impacts the surface of Charon

Solution

The potential is given by −   (6.67 ×  10   − 11 )  ×  (1.31 ×  10  22 )    ____________________  1190 ×  10  3   = − 7.34 × 10  5 J kg   − 1 , and so, the gravitational potential energy is given by − 7.34 × 10  5 × 20 = − 1.5 × 10  7 J .

The potential is given by −   (6.67 ×  10   − 11 )  ×  (1.59 ×  10  21 )    ____________________  606 ×  10  3   = − 1.75 × 10  5 J kg   − 1 , and so, the gravitational potential energy is given by − 1.75 × 10  5 × 20 = − 3.5 × 10  6 J .

The kinetic energy at the surface of Pluto is given by  1 _ 2  × 20 × 1400  2 = 2.0 × 10  7 J .

The total energy at the surface of Pluto is then given by − 1.5 × 10  7 + 2.0 × 10  7 = 5.0 × 10  6 J .

Using the conservation of energy, the total energy at the surface of Charon must also be 5.0 × 10  6 J . Therefore, − 3.5 × 10  6 + E  K = 5.0 × 10  6 , where E  K is the kinetic energy on impact.

We can now write E  K = 8.5 × 10  6 =  1 _ 2  × 20 × v  2 . This leads to v = 920m s   − 1 .

Determine the minimum launch velocity, v of a mass, m from the surface of Pluto, such that it is just able to reach Charon.

Solution

As we have done in a previous example, we can write  1.59 ×  10  21  _________  x  2   =  1.31 ×  10  22  ________   (1.96 ×  10  7  − x)  2   . This leads to x = 5.06 × 10  6 m as only valid solution.

Just beyond point A, Charon’s gravitational field takes over, and so, we only need to determine the launch velocity at which the mass will just reach A.

Therefore, the kinetic energy of the mass at point A must be zero, and so, the total energy at point A must be purely the gravitational potential energy due to Charon and Pluto.

The total gravitational potential energy of a mass, m at point A is then given by − m × 6.67 × 10   − 11 × (   1.59 ×  10  21  ________ 5.06 ×  10  6    +   1.31 ×  10  22   ________________  1.96 ×  10  7  − 5.06 ×  10  6   ) = − m × 8.11 × 10  4 , which must also be the total energy.

The total energy at the surface of Pluto must also be − m × 8.11 × 10  4 .

The gravitational potential energy at the surface of Pluto is given by − m ×   (6.67 ×  10   − 11 )  ×  (1.31 ×  10  22 )    _____________________  1190 ×  10  3   = − m × 7.34 × 10  5 .

Therefore, − m × 8.11 × 10  4 = E  K − m × 7.34 × 10  5 , where E  K is the launch kinetic energy. This results in E  K = m × 6.53 × 10  5 .

We can now write m × 6.53 × 10  5 =  1 _ 2  × m × v  2 . This leads to v = 1140m s   − 1 .
