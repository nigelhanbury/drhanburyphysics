Previously, we have looked at how to calculate the energy released in a nuclear reaction by calculating the mass defect. Here, we will look at how to use the binding energy to calculate the energy released. Fission is a process in which, heavy nuclei split into two smaller nuclei with a large amount of energy released. The splitting of the nucleus produces 2 to 3 further neutrons. The diagram below, represents one such reaction, where a Uranium - 235 nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits into Caesium - 144 and Rubidium - 90 with a release of 2 neutrons (known as induced fission). The reaction is represented by  92  235  U +  0  1  n →  55  144  Cs +   37  90  Rb  + 2  0  1  n .

The graph above, represents the binding energy per nucleon curve, where the nuclei involved are labelled. For convenience, their binding energy per nucleon values are shown in brackets. Notice, that the products of the fission reaction are higher up the curve. In other words, the products have a larger binding energy per nucleon. This indicates that the nuclei of the products are more tightly bound, and hence, more stable. They are in fact, not stable, and fission products themselves are highly radioactive. As a consequence, the total binding energy of the products is greater than that of Uranium - 235. This means that energy must have been released. This can be difficult for students to appreciate. If the total binding energy has increased, then since they are more tightly bound, the potential energy must have decreased. Therefore, if the potential energy has decreased, then energy must have been released. Note, that it is the increase in binding energy, which is equal to the energy released.

Example Problem

Using the above graph, calculate the increase in total binding energy due to the fission event,  92  235  U +  0  1  n →  55  144  Cs +   37  90  Rb  + 2  0  1  n and hence, calculate the energy released.

Solution

Initial binding energy is given by BE  Initial = 235 × 7.59 = 1780MeV , and the final binding energy, BE  Final = (144 × 8.21) + (90 × 8.63) = 1960MeV .

The increase in binding energy, and hence, energy released is given by E = BE  Final − BE  Initial = 1960 − 1780 = 180MeV .

Remember:

The binding energy of a single nucleon is zero.
