The diagram on the right, represents an ideal gas being compressed at constant temperature.

As stated previously, for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume, P ∝  1 _ V  .

In other words, as the piston is moved downwards slowly, the pressure exerted by the molecules on the cylinder walls increases.

Owing to Newton’s Third Law, there must be a greater mean force acting back on the vessel wall. Since the mean force is spread over an area of the wall, and pressure is defined as the force per unit area, there must be an increase in pressure.

The Mean Kinetic Energy of a Particle

Using the following 2 ideal gas equations, we can derive the formula to calculate the mean translational kinetic energy of a particle. This is assuming a monotonic gas (particles made up of only 1 atom), where the particles have no rotational kinetic energy.

PV =  1 _ 3  Nm _  c  2   

PV = NkT

Remembering that k represents the Boltzmann constant, given by k =  R __  N  A   .

Equating both equations, gives  1 _ 3  Nm _  c  2 = NkT , which simplifies to  1 _ 3  m _  c  2 = kT .

The mean kinetic energy of a particle, _  E  K is given by _  E  K =  1 _ 2  m _  c  2 . Multiplying both sides of  1 _ 3  m _  c  2 = kT by  3 _ 2  , gives  1 _ 2  m _  c  2 =  3 _ 2  kT .

Therefore, the mean kinetic energy of a particle is given by _  E  K =  3 _ 2  kT .

This provides a very important result, that the mean kinetic energy of the particles is directly proportional to the Kelvin temperature, _  E  K ∝ T .

Additionally, the mean square speed is directly proportional to the Kelvin temperature _  c  2 ∝ T . This makes intuitive sense, as you would expect the speed of the particles to increase if the temperature increases.

A monotonic gas is made up of particles made of only 1 atom, where the particles have no rotational kinetic energy.

Key equations and relationships:

_  E  K =  3 _ 2  kT

 1 _ 2  m _  c  2 =  3 _ 2  kT

_  E  K ∝ T

_  c  2 ∝ T
