Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

Consider the diagram of a Hall probe on the right. It consists of a semiconductor element (wafer) in a magnetic field, which has a current passing through it. In this case, the free electrons are the mobile charge carriers. When a magnetic field is applied in the direction shown, the voltmeter VH, registers a small potential difference, called the Hall Voltage.

If you apply FLHR to the diagram, the magnetic force FB, acting on the free electrons is towards the right (remember, conventional current is in the opposite direction to the flow of electrons). This means that there will be a build up of negative charge on the right hand side. The flow of electrons towards this side will eventually stop due to electrostatic repulsion. An equilibrium will be reached when the magnetic force FB, is balanced by the electric repulsion force FE.

An electric field E, is setup between the charged faces, and exerts a force FE, on the electron given by FE = eE .

Remember that the direction of this force acts in the opposite direction to the electric field.

The magnetic force FB, acting on the electron is given by FB = evd B , where vd, represents the mean drift velocity.

As the charge builds up, the electric field strength increases until FE = FB , and no further charge accumulates, as the resultant force on the electron is now zero, and so, no further electrons are deflected towards the right face.
