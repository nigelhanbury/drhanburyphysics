Period of Motion

We can use a similar equation to that previously, by making use of the centripetal force formula F = mr ω  2 , where ω represents the angular velocity.

We can write BQv = mr 𝜔  2 . Knowing that v = r𝜔 , gives BQ (r𝜔) = mr 𝜔  2 . This results in ω =  BQ _ m  .

Since ω =  2π _ T  , gives  2π _ T  =  BQ _ m  , and so, T =  2πm _ BQ  .

This is an interesting and important result, as this shows that the period of the particles motion in a magnetic field is independent of its velocity. This fact is essential for the working of the Cyclotron, which will be examined later.

If the velocity v, of the charged particle is at an angle θ, to the magnetic field B, the force F, acting on the charge is given by F = BQvsinθ . The direction of the force is perpendicular to both the velocity and magnetic field as before. With reference to the diagram, the force would be acting out of the page.

On inspection of the above formula, maximum force occurs when the velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic field and zero when parallel. In other words, the charged particle must cut the lines of flux in order to experience a force.

Helical Path Followed by Charged Particles

When a charged particle enters a magnetic field at an angle, the particle will trace out a helical path as shown in the diagram.

Using FLHR, we can see that the direction of the magnetic force is as shown in the diagram. The direction of the force must be perpendicular to both the magnetic field and velocity.

As a result, the particle will perform circular motion in the horizontal plane (perpendicular to the field lines).

Since there is a component of the velocity upwards (parallel to the field), a helical path is then traced out. We can visualise this by imagining the particle performing circular motion in the horizontal plane, while also travelling upwards at a constant velocity.
