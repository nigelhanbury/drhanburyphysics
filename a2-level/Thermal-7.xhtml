Gas Cycles

Thermodynamics can be used to extract energy from a fuel, through a series of expansions and compressions of a gas, the gas can be made to do work. This process is used to transform chemical energy into mechanical energy. An example of this, is the combustion engine. The diagram below does not represent the cycles involved within a combustion engine, as these are complex. The diagram is to simply demonstrate how a gas can be made to do work.

Enclosed area equals net work done by gas

1 – 2: Isothermal (constant temperature) expansion. Work is done by the gas, and is equal to the area under the curve between points 1 & 2. Heat supplied equals work done by the gas. ΔU = 0 .

2 – 3: Heat removed from the gas at constant volume. This results in a decrease in pressure and temperature. No work is done by the gas as ΔV = 0 .

3 – 4: Isothermal compression (constant temperature). Work is done on the gas, and is equal to the area under the curve between points 3 & 4. Heat extracted.

4 – 1: Heat is absorbed by the gas at constant volume. Pressure and temperature increase. No work is done by the gas.

Net work done by the gas = Work done by gas (1 – 2) – Work done on the gas (3 – 4).

Going through the above cycle in a clockwise direction, results in work being done by the gas. Since, after 1 complete cycle, the final state has the same pressure and volume as the initial state, the final temperature must be the same as the initial temperature. Using U ∝ T , there is no net change in the internal energy, and so, ΔU = 0 . Therefore, the net heat supplied Q, must equal the net work done by the gas W.

The enclosed area of a gas cycle is equal to the net work done by the gas.
