Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

The Synchrotron

The diagram to the right describes how a synchrotron operates. The physics is very similar to that of the cyclotron.

Particles are injected into the ring from a linear accelerator and are bent into the ring by a series of electromagnets, which produce a magnetic field as shown. Every orbit they pass through multiple accelerating gaps (cavities), and their energy is increased.

In contrast to the cyclotron, however, the radius remains constant, so the magnetic field must increase in strength as the particles velocity increases. Therefore, the frequency of the AC must also increase as the particles’ velocity increases.

In the example shown, an alternating potential difference is applied across each quarter ring. The sign of the potential is shown on each quarter. As can be seen, the positive ion is given an acceleration boost within the cavity that is labelled.

Relationship between Magnetic Field Strength, and Velocity

As mentioned previously, as the velocity of the particle increases, the magnetic field strength B, must also increase as the radius of the circular path needs to be maintained.

As derived previously, B =  mv _ rQ  . Since r, m & Q are constant, B ∝ v . The applied magnetic field strength B, is directly proportional to the velocity v, of the charged particle.

In other words, if the velocity of the particle doubles, then the applied magnetic field strength must also double in order for the radius of the circular path to remain constant.

Modern synchrotrons can produce magnetic fields up to 10 Tesla, which results in the need to use superconducting electromagnets. In order for the electromagnets to generate such a field, the number of turns of wire per unit length and current need to be extremely large. If the number of turns of wire per unit length is high, then the wire needs to be thin, and therefore, cannot carry the required large currents due to overheating. Superconductors are ideal as they can carry extremely large currents with no heating problems.
