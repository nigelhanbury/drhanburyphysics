V = (  nR _ P  ) T

The apparatus to the right, demonstrates how the temperature of an ideal gas can be increased while keeping the pressure constant.

Again, dry air is used as it is a good approximation of an ideal gas. As the temperature is increased, the plug of sulfuric acid moves upwards, allowing the volume of air to increase, while maintaining a constant pressure. The sulfuric acid absorbs surplus water vapour and keeps the air dry.

As can be seen from the graph, the volume is directly proportional to the temperature, V ∝ T .

Re-arranging the state equation, gives V = (  nR _ P  ) T .

The gradient of the graph is given by  nR _ P  .

Since  V _ T  = constant, we can write

  V  1  __  T  1   =   V  2  __  T  2  

Summary of equations:

P  1 V  1 = P  2 V  2 constant T

  P  1  __  T  1   =   P  2  __  T  2      constant V

  V  1  __  T  1   =   V  2  __  T  2   constant P
