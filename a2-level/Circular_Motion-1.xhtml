3.1 Circular Motion

Angular Measurements (The Radian)

When dealing with circular motion in physics, angular measurements in degrees are not always useful. Therefore, an alternative unit is used, and this unit is known as radians.

Arc length, l

With reference to the diagram on the left, the angle in radians is calculated as

θ  R =  l _ r 

Where l is arc length in m and r is the radius in m.

For 1 complete rotation (or cycle), the angle traced out corresponds to 360°.

In this case, the arc length is equal to l = 2πr , and hence, in radians, θ  R =  2πr _ r  = 2π . Therefore, 360° = 2π radians.

Definition of The Radian

The radian is defined as a unit of measurement of angles equal to about 57.3°, equivalent to the angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc length equal to the radius.

UNIT: rad.

Conversion Between Radians and Degrees

The above fact allows us to convert degrees to radians and radians to degrees.

Using ratios, we can state   θ  R  __ 2π  =   θ  D  __ 360  .

This simplifies to θ  R = θ  D × (  π _ 180  ) , where θ  D represents the angle in degrees.

Similarly, θ  D = θ  R × (  180 _ π  ) .

Key Equations:

θ  R =  l _ r 

θ  R = θ  D × (  π _ 180  )

θ  D = θ  R × (  180 ___ π  )
