Proof that a Mass-Spring System Oscillates with SHM

Restoring force, F  s

From Hook’s Law, we know that the extension is directly proportional to the applied force, and so, F = kx , where F, represents the applied force, and x, the extension with k, being the spring stiffness.

The diagram on the right represents a mass displaced, where here, the restoring force, F  s is shown. Notice that the restoring force acts towards the equilibrium position and in the opposite direction to the displacement.

Therefore, we can write F  s = − kx .

Using F  s = ma , we can write a = −  kx _ m  . This demonstrates that the mass-spring system oscillates with SHM as a ∝ − x .

Force and Acceleration

As discussed previously, the resultant force acting on the mass towards the equilibrium position is directly proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position. Therefore, maximum force, and hence, maximum acceleration will occur at x = + A or x = − A . The mass experiences zero force as it passes through the equilibrium position, and hence, zero acceleration, as the displacement is zero.

The velocity of the mass at any instant, is given by the gradient of the displacement/time graph. It is important to note the sign of the gradient, as this indicates the direction of the velocity. By inspection of the graph, the magnitude of the velocity (speed) is largest as the mass passes through the equilibrium position, and is reflected in the diagram. The gradient, and so, the velocity is zero when the mass is at the position of maximum displacement, e.g., x = + A or x = − A .
