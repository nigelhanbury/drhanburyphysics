The example on the right, represents a gas expanding at constant temperature (isothermal expansion).

The area under the graph, represented by the shaded region, represents the work done by the gas W, in moving from state 1 to state 2.

The arrow on the graph, represents the direction taken by the gas when changing state.

In other words, state 1, represents the initial state with pressure and volume, P1, V1, and state 2, is the final pressure and volume, P2, V2.

Since the temperature T, is constant and U ∝ T , the internal energy U, does not change. This leads to ΔU = 0 . Referring to the First Law of Thermodynamics, ΔU = Q − W , leads to 0 = Q − W , and so, Q = W . Note, that when the volume increases, the work is done by the gas, so W is positive.

This is an important result. For a gas that expands at constant temperature, the heat supplied is equal to the work done by the gas.

The direction of heat flow is into the gas. This is possible because of the conducting wall of the cylinder, as heat is able to be conducted quickly from the surroundings.

The First Law and a Gas Slowly Compressed

The graph to the right, represents a gas being compressed at constant temperature. Therefore, ΔU = 0 as the internal energy is constant.

Notice that the direction of the state change has reversed compared to the previous example. When the volume decreases, work is done on the gas, so W is negative.

This results in heat flow out of the gas, Q is negative.

The area under the graph going from state 1 to state 2, in this situation, represents the work done on the gas.
