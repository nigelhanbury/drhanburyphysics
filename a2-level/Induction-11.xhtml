Lenz’s Law is defined as the direction of any current resulting from an induced emf, is such, as to oppose the change in flux linkage that is causing the current.

Consider a magnet moving towards a coil as shown in the diagram on the right. The magnetic flux of the magnet cuts through the coil and a magnetic flux linkage within the coil is set up. As the distance between the magnet and coil decreases, the flux linkage increases, and hence, the coil experiences a rate of change of flux linkage. Owing to Faraday’s Law, an EMF is induced across the coil, and current flows.

When current flows in the coil, a magnetic field is set up, similar to that of a bar magnet. A north pole is induced on the left side of the coil in order to repel the approaching north pole of the magnet. This repulsion is set up in an attempt to prevent the flux linkage changing. The Right Hand Grip Rule can be used to predict the direction of the current.

As the north pole set up in the coil repels the north pole of the magnet, a force must be applied to the magnet to overcome this. Work must be done to move the magnet. This mechanical energy is converted to electrical energy in the form of the induced EMF.

Lenz’s Law accounts for the ‘–‘ sign in the equation for Faraday’s Law. The induced EMF opposes the change causing it. Lenz’s Law, is therefore, a consequence of the conservation of energy

Lenz’s Law and Changing Field Strength

The diagram on the right shows a conducting ring placed within a magnetic field, which is acting into the page and increasing. Previously, we looked at predicting the direction of the induced current using FRHR. However, we can use Lenz’s Law.

As the field strength is increasing, the magnetic flux linkage through the ring is also increasing. Remember, that we can visualise magnetic flux as the number of lines of flux cutting through an area. Here, as the flux is increasing (and hence, flux linkage), the number of lines of flux cutting the area is also increasing.

The direction of the induced current will be such, as to try and prevent this flux linkage from increasing. Therefore, the induced current will generate a magnetic field out of the page in order to keep the number of lines of flux constant. The RHGR can be used here.
