Consider a rotating drum within a spin dryer carrying some clothes.

Diagram A shows the clothing at the bottom of the drum. Since the drum is rotating at a constant angular velocity, the resultant force acting on the clothing must be acting vertically upwards, towards the centre of the drum. Here, N, represents the normal contact force, and mg, the weight of the clothing.

Let F = N − mg , where F, represents the resultant force, and m, the mass of the clothing. Note, that N > mg , since F must act vertically upwards.

Now, we are in a position to form an equation of motion in order to determine the normal contact force. Using the formula for centripetal force, F = mr ω  2 , we can write mr ω  2 = N − mg , and therefore, N = m (g + r ω  2 ) . This represents the normal contact force between the drum and the clothing at the base of the drum.

Diagram B shows the clothing at the top of the drum half a cycle later. Here, the resultant force acting on the clothing must act vertically downwards towards the centre. Let F = N + mg , where F, represents the resultant force. As before, we can write mr ω  2 = N + mg . Therefore, N = m (r ω  2  − g) . This represents the normal contact force between the drum and clothing at the top of the drum.

Notice, that there is a particular angular velocity where the normal contact force is zero. At this point, the clothing loses contact with the drum. This value of angular velocity is therefore, the minimum angular velocity in order to keep the clothing attached to the inside of the drum.

Remember, that it is the resultant of N and mg that acts towards the centre of rotation and this is known as the centripetal force.
