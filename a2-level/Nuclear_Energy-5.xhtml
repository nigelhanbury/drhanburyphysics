Binding Energy Per Nucleon

Nuclei have varying sizes, and therefore, it is useful to discuss the binding energy per nucleon. This simply represents the mean work or energy required to remove one nucleon from the nucleus. It is a measure of how tightly, the individual nucleons are bound to the nucleus.

Binding Energy Per Nucleon Curve

One of the most important curves in nuclear physics is the binding energy per nucleon curve depicted on the right. It is the characteristic shape of this curve, which allows the release of energy via the splitting or joining of nuclei. Later, we will examine this curve in the context of fission and fusion.

We can see from the graph, that the binding energy per nucleon for U - 238 is approximately 7.5MeV.

In fact, by inspecting the graph, we can see that typical values are between 7 – 9 MeV for the majority of nuclei.

We can use this graph to calculate the binding energy of any nuclei by multiplying the binding energy per nucleon and the number of nucleons.

The greater the binding energy per nucleon, the more tightly bound the nuclei. Therefore, the most stable nuclei exist within the shaded region above.

The binding energy per nucleon represents the mean energy required to remove a single nucleon from the nucleus.

Therefore, the greater the binding energy per nucleon, the more stable the isotope.

Example Problem

Use the above graph to estimate the binding energy of:

U - 238

Ge - 72

He - 4

Solution

For U - 238, BE = 7.5 × 238 = 1790MeV .

For Ge - 72, BE = 8.8 × 72 = 634MeV .

For He - 4, BE = 7.0 × 4 = 28MeV .

The binding energy of a single nucleon is zero.
