Consider a plate attached to a mass-spring system, as shown, submerged in oil. This introduces a frictional force known as drag. This will affect the dynamics of the system, and the system is said to be damped.

The mass will oscillate with SHM, but the amplitude will decrease every cycle as shown in the graph below. Mathematically, the amplitude decays exponentially. The amplitude will decrease by the same proportion every cycle. This is known as damping. Note, that the period T, is not affected by damping.

If a mass on a spring or a pendulum is pulled back and released, both will oscillate with SHM and eventually come to rest. The initial total energy of the oscillating system will be dissipated as thermal energy (heat) to the surroundings.

If the amount of damping is increased, the oscillations will decay away more rapidly until the point at which, there are no oscillations. Critical damping is the case when the resistive forces on the system are just large enough to prevent oscillations occurring at all when the system is displaced and released.

Referring to the graphs above, critical damping shows how the displacement varies with time. As can be seen, there are no oscillations. The important point, is that the system returns to equilibrium in the shortest amount of time.

If the amount of damping is increased beyond critical, the system still does not oscillate, but takes longer to reach equilibrium. This is known as over damped.
