The Hubble time (age of Universe) was calculated based on the recessional speeds of all the galaxies remaining constant during the time of the expansion up to now. This, of course, does not take into account, the gravitational attraction, and hence, the mean density of the universe.

The red dotted line represents a Universe with no matter and extrapolates back approximately 13.8 billion years – calculated using t ≈  1 __  H  0   . The different expansion rates will alter the estimations of the age of the Universe.

Notice that the gradient of all the above curves are equal at present day but start at different points in the past depending on how the Universe is expanding. For example, the flat Universe began approximately 9 billon years ago as in the past, the recession speed of the galaxies were greater than today.

Example Problem

The current estimate of the Hubble constant is approximately 70km s   − 1 Mpc   − 1 . 1pc = 3.086 × 1016m

Determine:

The current age of the Universe.

The critical density of the Universe in terms of number of protons per unit volume, where m  p = 1.67 × 10   − 27 kg .

Solution

Firstly, we need to convert the Hubble constant into SI units in order to estimate the age in seconds.

We can write H  0 =  70 ×  10  3  m s   − 1   _______________   10  6  ×  (3.086 × 1016m)    = 2.3 × 10   − 18 s   − 1 .

Then, t =  1 __  H  0   =  1 ___________  2.3 × 10   − 18 s   − 1    = 4.35 × 10  17 s .

Converting to years,  4.35 × 10  17   ___________  365 × 24 × 3600  = 13.8 × 10  9 years .

Using 𝜌  c   =   3 H  0  2  __ 8πG     , we obtain 𝜌  c  =  3 ×  (2.3 × 10   − 18  )  2   ______________  8π ×  (6.67 ×  10   − 11 )   = 9.47 × 10   − 27 kg m   − 3 .

Therefore, the number of protons per unit volume is given by  9.47 × 10   − 27   _ 1.67 × 10   − 27    = 6 .
