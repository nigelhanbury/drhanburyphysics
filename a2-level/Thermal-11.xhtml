Unit 3, Section 4: Thermal Physics

Example Problem

Find the heat needed to raise the temperature of 3.0kg of water from 20°C to 50°C. Specific heat capacity of water is 4190Jkg−1K−1.

Solution

Using Q = mcΔθ = 3.0 × 4190 × (50 − 20) = 3.8 × 10  5 J .

Example Problem

1MJ of heat is supplied to a length of aluminium, with dimensions 80mm x 80mm x 1.0m. By how much does its temperature rise?

Density of aluminium is 2700kgm−3 and its specific heat capacity is 900Jkg−1K−1

Solution

Using Q = mcΔθ , gives 1.0 × 10  6 = (80 × 10  −3  × 80 ×  10  −3  ×1.0) × 2700 ×900 ×Δθ ⇒ Δθ = 64°C .

The temperature increases by 64°C or 64K.

Example Problem

Water of mass 1.5kg is heated in a pan and reaches a temperature of 95°C. Vegetables of mass 1.2kg at a temperature of 25°C are then dropped into the water.

Specific heat capacity of water is 4200Jkg−1K−1

Specific heat capacity of vegetables is 3200Jkg−1K−1.

Determine the final temperature of the water and vegetables.

Solution

Heat lost by water = Heat gained by vegetables. From this, we can now form an equation.

1.5 × 4200 ×  (95 −  θ  F )  = 1.2 × 3200 ×  ( θ  F  − 25)   

This results in θ  F = 68°C .
