Example Problem

With reference to the below circuit, one of the 200µF capacitors is charged when the switch is in position A. At this point, the second capacitor is uncharged. The switch is then moved to position B, so that one capacitor charges the second.

Determine the final reading on the voltmeter and explain why it is less than 100V.

Solution

When the switch is in position A, the total charge is given by Q = (200 ×  10   − 6 ) × 100 = 0.0200C .

When the switch is moved to position B, this charge is redistributed between the two capacitors.

The new total capacitance is given by 400µF. This is simply the sum of the two capacitors as they are connected in parallel.

Using V =  Q _ C  =  0.0200 _________ 400 ×  10   − 6   = 50.0V .

The final reading on the voltmeter is 50.0V. Since the charge had to redistribute, there must have been a momentary current. This resulted in energy being dissipated in the connecting wires, resulting in a decrease in the total energy stored in the capacitor combination. Therefore, less energy stored per unit charge, and hence, less pd.
