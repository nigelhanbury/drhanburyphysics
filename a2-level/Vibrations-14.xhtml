Unit 3, Section 2: Vibrations

Free Oscillations

Free oscillations occur when an oscillatory system (such as a mass on a spring, or a pendulum) is displaced and released. The frequency of the free oscillations is called the system’s natural frequency.

Examples of a system with a natural frequency:

Guitar string

Stroked wine glass

Childs swing

Atoms within a crystal

Building during an earthquake

Column of air

Struck bell

These occur when a sinusoidally varying ‘driving’ force is applied to an oscillatory system, causing it to oscillate with the frequency of the applied force.

The example to the right, shows a mass-spring system placed on an oscillating platform. The platform oscillates with a frequency f. This causes a sinusoidally varying force to be applied to the mass as shown. This is known as the driving force, and the frequency, the driving frequency. The mass-spring system is forced to oscillate at the driving frequency.

The above graph demonstrates how the amplitude of the forced oscillations varies for increasing driving frequency. As can be seen, the amplitude is a maximum when the driving frequency is equal to the natural frequency f  0 , of the system, and this is known as resonance.
