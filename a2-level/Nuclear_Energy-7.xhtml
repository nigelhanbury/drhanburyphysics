Most stable nuclei

Fusion is the process given to the combining (fusing) of light nuclei to form heavier nuclei. This process releases energy.

A typical reaction is given by 1  2  H + 1  3  H → 2  4  He + 0  1  n .

Nuclear fusion is the process that occurs at the centre of stars. This particular example, shows the fusing of 2 Hydrogen isotopes, Deuterium and Tritium, resulting in the formation of Helium - 4.

Again, by inspection of the graph on the right, we can see that the binding energy per nucleon of the product Helium - 4, has increased, therefore, energy must be released in this fusion process.

Example Problem

Using the above graph, calculate the energy released due to the fusion event, 1  2  H + 1  3  H → 2  4  He + 0  1  n .

Solution

Initial binding energy is given by BE  Initial = (2 × 1.1) + (3 × 2.8) = 10.6MeV , and the final binding energy, BE  Final = 4 × 7.1 = 28.4MeV .

The energy released is given by E = BE  Final − BE  Initial = 28.4 − 10.6 = 17.8MeV .

It is important to recognise the regions of the graph where fission and fusion will release energy. With both processes, the product/products must have a greater binding energy per nucleon. Fusion has the ability to release tremendous amounts of energy compared to fission. Modern day nuclear weapons use thermonuclear warheads, which is essentially a fusion device. Typical energy released is 1 – 50 Megatons of TNT equivalent.
