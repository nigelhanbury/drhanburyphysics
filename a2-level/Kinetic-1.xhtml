3.3 Kinetic Theory

Ideal Gases

The physics of an ideal gas represents an important aspect of thermodynamics, which is relevant across a wide range of disciplines, such as chemical engineering, chemistry, and meteorology. The term ideal gas, represents the basic model of a gas, and is considered an idealised scenario. The definition and conditions, which make an ideal gas will be looked at in detail later, when we look at the particle model.

In thermodynamics, there are two types of quantities: macroscopic and microscopic. The term microscopic, refers to quantities that relate to atoms and molecules, such kinetic energy, molecular mass, momentum etc. These are related to the very small scale. The term macroscopic, refers to the quantities we can directly measure, such as pressure, temperature, and volume. These relate to the large scale of everyday life.

It was found through empirical evidence, that pressure P, volume V and Temperature T, are related by the following formula.

 PV _ T  = constant

Here, P is measured in Nm-2 or Pascal (Pa), V in m3, and T in Kelvin (K).

The Kelvin temperature scale is similar to the Celsius scale, where zero K is defined as that temperature at which the internal energy is minimum, and all particle motion can be considered stationary (absolute zero). This occurs at -273.16°C.

This will be examined in detail later, but for now, T(K) = T(°C) + 273 . Note, when referring to this temperature scale, you must state either ‘Kelvin temperature’/’absolute temperature’ or ‘thermodynamic temperature’.
