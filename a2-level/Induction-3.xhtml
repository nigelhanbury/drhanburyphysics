When the flux linking an electrical circuit is changing, an emf is induced in the circuit of magnitude equal to the rate of change of flux linkage.

In equation form, ε = −  Δ (Nɸ)  ___ Δt  or ε = −  d (Nɸ)  ___ dt  The negative sign relates to Lenz’s law, which is a statement of the conservation of energy. This will be looked at later.

Consider a rectangular wire coil with N turn, as shown below, entering a uniform magnetic field B, at constant velocity. As the coil enters the field, the magnetic flux linkage increases at a constant rate, and so, has a constant rate of change of flux linkage, and hence, a constant EMF is induced across the coil. This in turn will cause a current to flow in the coil. When fully inside the field, there is no change in flux linkage, and so, no EMF is induced. As the coil exits the field, the flux linkage decreases at a constant rate, and so, a constant EMF is induced. Note, that on exit, the rate of change of flux linkage is negative compared to when the coil was entering the field, therefore, the EMF will be induced in the opposite direction, and hence, the current.

Referring to the above graph, Δt represents the time taken to fully enter the magnetic field, and BAN represents the change in the magnetic flux linkage.

From Faraday’s Law, the induced EMF is ε = −  BAN _ Δt  . In other words, this is the negative gradient of the flux linkage graph.

Since the gradient is constant, the induced EMF, ε is constant. It is important to note, that when sketching the EMF graph, the negative sign is important, and hence, while the coil is entering the field, the EMF is negative. Similarly, this is why the EMF is positive on exit.
