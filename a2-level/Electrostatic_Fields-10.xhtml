Equipotential lines are lines of constant electrical potential. Referring to the equation for electrical potential, V  E =  Q ____ 4𝜋 𝜀  0 r  , all points lying along a circular path centred on the charge have constant r, and hence, constant electrical potential.

The difference in potential ∆V, between each equipotential line must be constant. This results in a series of concentric circles where the distance between each one increases as we move away from the charge.

The work required to move a charge q, from one equipotential line to another, is simply the difference between the two potentials ∆V multiplied by the charge q. W = q∆V .

For point or spherical charges, the equipotential lines are made up of a series of concentric circles where the distance between each one increases as we move away from the charge.

The difference in potential ∆V, between each equipotential line must be constant.

The diagram on the right shows the equipotential lines around two equal and opposite charges. Notice that at each point, the equipotential lines are perpendicular to the electric field. Therefore, no work is required to move a charge along the same equipotential line as there is no change in electrical potential.
