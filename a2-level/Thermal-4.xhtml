Technologies such as the combustion engine, refrigeration units and jet engines etc, all follow the principles of thermodynamics. The combustion engine, for example, uses the compression and expansion of gases in order to extract energy from fuel and transfer it to mechanical energy.

The diagram to the right, represents a gas within a cylinder expanding and pushing the piston head upwards. In order to do this, the gas must do work.

Let us examine the motion of the piston head over a very small distance Δx . Since Δx is very small, we can consider the pressure of the gas P, constant.

The force F, acting on the piston head can be calculated using F = PA , where A, represents the cross sectional area of the piston head.

Since work done W, is equal to the product of the force and the distance moved in the direction of the force, we can write W = FΔx , and substituting for F = PA , gives W = PAΔx .

On inspection of the above diagram, the piston head has moved through a small volume ΔV , given by ΔV = AΔx . Substituting ΔV = AΔx into W = PAΔx , gives the important result W = PΔV .

The work done by an expanding gas at constant pressure is given by

W = PΔV

From AS, it was stated that energy transferred is equal to the work done.

Therefore, PΔV gives the energy transfer to the piston head, and what is ever connected to it.

Therefore, work can be considered energy in transit
