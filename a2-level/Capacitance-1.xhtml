A capacitor is a device that stores charge. A capacitor consists of two conductors, such as a pair of metal plates, separated by an insulator known as a dielectric material. This dielectric material can be air.

When a capacitor is connected to a cell or any other emf source, electrons are removed from the plate that is connected to the positive terminal of the cell. They travel through the cell and are deposited on the plate connected to the negative terminal as shown in the diagram. There will be a flow of charge in the circuit until the capacitor is fully charged. For every electron that leaves the positively charged plate, an equal number are deposited on the negatively charged plate. Hence, the plates are always equally but oppositely charged.

Electrons leave the plate and the plate is left with a net positive charge.

Definition of a Capacitor

A capacitor is a pair of conducting plates separated by an insulator. If a potential difference is placed across the plates, they acquire equal and opposite charges.

Technically speaking, capacitors do not store charge, they displace charge from one plate to the other.

The net charge on any capacitor is always zero.

Uses of Capacitors

Capacitors are used throughout our everyday lives, as they are able to not only store charge, but to also store electrical energy. Some examples are:

Camera flash: Sometimes, small capacitors are used to power a camera flash. When the capacitor discharges rapidly, the energy stored is transformed into heat and light in the bulb. The charged capacitor releases approximately 0.5J of energy in 1ms, giving a power output of approximately 1KW.

Backup power supply for computers: In the event of a power failure, data can be lost. Capacitors are used to provide a temporary power supply.

Signal generation: Capacitors are used to generate alternating current or pd, such as in a signal generator.
