Energy Lost During Charging

When the capacitor is fully charged, the potential difference V, will be equal to the supply emf E, and so, the energy stored will be U =  1 _ 2  QE , where V = E .

Since the emf of the supply is constant during the charging process, the emf source will supply a total quantity of energy equal to E  Supply = QE , since Q coulombs of charge will pass through the emf source.

Comparing the energy supplied to the energy stored, it can be seen that half the energy supplied has been dissipated as heat in the wires. In other words, U =  1 _ 2  × E  Supply .

Important:

When a capacitor is charged, the energy stored is equal to half the energy supplied by the emf source.

U =  1 _ 2  × E  Supply

Therefore, the energy dissipated as heat in the wires is equal to the energy stored in the capacitor.

Example Problem

A 20µF capacitor is connected to a 10V supply. Determine:

Charge stored by the capacitor

Energy stored by the capacitor

The energy dissipated as heat in the wires during charging

Solution

Using Q = CV = (20 ×  10   − 6 ) × 10 = 200 × 10   − 6 C , or 200µC.

Easiest formula to use is U =  1 _ 2  C V  2 =  1 _ 2  × (20 ×  10   − 6 ) × 10  2 = 1.0 × 10   − 3 J or 1.0mJ.

Energy dissipated is given by 1.0mJ.
