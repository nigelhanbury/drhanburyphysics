Unit 3, Section 4: Thermal Physics

The first law of thermodynamics is really a statement of the conservation of energy. In the following text, we will refer to the system. The system can refer to a cylinder of gas, cup of tea or a steel bar etc.

Q = Heat energy supplied to the system

W = Work done by the system

ΔU = Change in the internal energy of the system

The First Law of Thermodynamics

Applying the conservation of energy, gives ΔU = Q − W . It is important to get the signs correct.

Q(+) = heat supplied

Q(-) = heat leaving

W(+) = work done by system

W(-) = work done on system

∆U(+) = increase in internal energy

∆U(-) = decrease in internal energy

The term, system can refer to a cylinder of gas, cup of tea or a steel bar etc.

Example Problem

500J of heat flows into a system. The system does 300J of work.

Find the net change in internal energy of the system.

Solution

Let Q = 500J , W = 300J , we can use ΔU = Q − W . Thus, ΔU = 500 − 300 = 200J

The internal energy increases by 200J.

Example Problem

A mug of tea is stirred so that 5J of work is done on the tea. At the same time, 40J of heat escapes from the mug.

What is the change in internal energy?

Solution

Here, the mug of tea is the system. Let Q = − 40J , W = − 5J , we can use ΔU = Q − W .

Thus, ΔU = (− 40) − (− 5) = − 35J

The internal energy decreases by 35J.

Example Problem

A system has 500J of work done on it. Its internal energy falls by 800J.

Calculate the heat flow and state its direction

Solution

Let ΔU = − 800J , W = − 500J , we can use ΔU = Q − W .

Thus, − 800 = Q − (− 500) ⇒ Q = − 1300J

1300J of heat leaves the system.
