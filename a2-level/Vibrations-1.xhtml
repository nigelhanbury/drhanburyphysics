3.2 Vibrations

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

If a motion repeats itself regularly, it is called a periodic motion. Simple harmonic motion is the name given to one particular type of periodic motion. SHM occurs often in nature, the following are examples:

Simple pendulum (for small angles of swing)

Oscillating spring and mass

Vibrating cantilever

Oscillation of liquid in a U - tube

Molecules oscillating due to the passage of a sound wave

Thermal vibrations of atoms in a crystal lattice.

The motion of a body is defined as simple harmonic if it has an acceleration which is:

Directed towards a fixed point

Directly proportional to the displacement of the body from the fixed point

In equation form, a ∝ − x , where x, represents the displacement from a fixed point.

The example on the right, represents a mass-spring system that is oscillating.

The mass in position A is at equilibrium. Here, the spring is not extended, and so, no resultant force acts on the mass. The displacement x, is defined as the distance from the equilibrium position (fixed point) and positive in the upwards direction.

The mass in position B shows the spring under tension, and so, the resultant force on the mass is acting upwards towards the equilibrium position.

The mass in position C shows the spring under compression, and so, the resultant force on the mass is acting downwards towards the equilibrium position.

We can now see that at all times during the oscillation, the resultant force is always acting towards the equilibrium position, and hence, so is its acceleration. The first statement of the definition of SHM can be seen to be satisfied.
