The Mole

The mole (abbreviated to mol) is the S.I. unit of an ‘amount of substance’. It is the amount containing as many particles (e.g. molecules) as there are atoms in 12g of carbon-12. The standard symbol is n. The number of particles that make up 1 mol is known as Avogadro’s Constant NA, and is given by 6.02 × 10  23 . In other words, N  A = 6.02 × 10  23 mol  −1 .

The molar mass Mm, is the mass per mol. In the case of carbon-12, the mass of 1 mol is 12g by definition. It is easy to find the molar mass of a particular particle, as its mass in grams is equal to the relative molecular mass Mr, of the particle.

M  r =  mass of molecule   _____________     1 _ 12   × mass of carbon-12 atom 

In the case of carbon-12, Mr is 12, and so, the molar mass is 12g mol  −1 .

Some examples are:

Oxygen-16 has a molar mass of 16g mol  −1

CO2 (carbon dioxide molecule) has a relative molecular mass of 44 and a molar mass of 44g mol  −1 .

Equation of State

Now the mole is well understood,  PV _ T  = constant can now be re-expressed in more formal terms.

PV = nRT

The above equation is known as the equation of state. Any gas that obeys the state equation, is considered an ideal gas. Here, R is defined as the molar gas constant, and is given by

R = 8.31J mol  -1 K  -1 .

For real gases, the equation of state is a good approximation in some circumstances, such as gases at low pressure, and well above their boiling point.

The equation of state can also be expressed as

PV = NkT

Here, N represents the total number of particles, and k, the Boltzmann constant, defined as

k =  R __  N  A  

The two ideal gas equations are:

PV = nRT

PV = NkT
