Unit 3, Section 2: Vibrations

Examples of Resonance

Forced oscillation and resonance is a common occurrence in our everyday lives. Some are useful, but some are a nuisance. Below, are some examples:

Washing machine

Sky scraper in earthquake

The rotating drum of a washing machine acts as the driving force, and the casing, the system that is being driven. When the driving frequency of the drum is equal to the natural frequency of the casing, the casing will resonate, and oscillate with maximum amplitude.

The oscillation of the ground during an earthquake acts as the driving force, and the sky scraper, the system that is being driven. When the frequency of the ground oscillations is equal to the natural frequency of the sky scraper, resonance occurs and the sky scraper risks collapse.

Other examples include:

Microwave cooking

Pushing a child on a swing

Rubbing a wine glass

Wind causes bridge collapse

Rear view mirror within a vehicle

It is important, that when giving examples of resonance, you specify what is doing the driving, and what is being driven.

When designing buildings and bridges, it is important for the engineers to select materials and designs, where the natural frequency is as far away as possible from any source of forced vibrations.
