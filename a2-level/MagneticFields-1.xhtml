Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

A conductor carrying a current, placed in a magnetic field, experiences a force. This is due to the interaction of the magnetic fields produced by the magnet and the conductor.

The maximum force occurs when the current is perpendicular to the magnetic field, as shown in the diagram on the right. In this situation, the force F, can be calculated using the equation F = BIL .

Here,

B = Magnetic field strength (magnetic flux density)/Tesla

I = Current/A

L = Length of conductor within the field/m

4.4 Magnetic Fields

A magnet affects the space around it. When certain materials (or other magnets) are brought near a magnet, they experience forces. The space around a magnet, where forces are experienced, is called the magnetic field.

• Certain materials known as magnetic materials are attracted to either pole of a magnet. E.g. Iron, nickel and cobalt.

• Unlike poles (N and S) attract

• Like poles (2 N’s or 2S’s) repel

Magnetic Field Lines

A magnetic field is represented by lines of magnetic flux (or lines of force). This is a vector quantity. Its direction is that in which the North pole of a freely-pivoted magnet points. The stronger the field, the smaller the separation between the field lines.

Fleming’s Left Hand Rule can be used to predict the direction of the induced force on the conductor.

• First finger represents the direction of the magnetic field B, in the direction of N to S.

• Second finger represents the direction of conventional current I.

• Thumb represents the direction of the induced force F.
