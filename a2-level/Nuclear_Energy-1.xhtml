3.6 Nuclear Energy

Nuclear energy is one of the key discoveries of the early 20th century, which has led to nuclear power, medical physics, including nuclear weapons. There exists a tremendous amount of energy contained within the nucleus of an atom, and work in the early 20th century lead to the harnessing of this energy.

E = m c  2

The above equation is probably the most famous equation in the world, and is built on the work of Albert Einstein. Einstein was known for his work on special and general relativity, and through this, the link between mass and energy. Einstein proposed that energy and mass are simply different aspects of the same thing, known as mass-energy. We can think of mass-energy as a single concept that either has mass or energy, depending on our point of view. Here, E, represents the quantity of energy in Joules that is represented by a mass m, in kg. And of course, c, the speed of light in a vacuum, measured in ms-1.

The Energy Equivalence of a 1 kg Mass

Let us consider a 1kg mass and apply E = m c  2 .

Substituting for the corresponding numbers, gives E = 1.0 × (3.0 ×  10  8 )  2 = 9.0 × 10  16 J .

This is a tremendous quantity of energy that is represented by a 1kg mass. Energy can be converted to mass, and mass converted to energy. We know from AS, that a particle and anti-particle will annihilate and their masses will be converted to energy in the form of gamma photons.

If we had 0.50kg of matter and 0.50kg of anti-matter, then after coming together, would release 9.0 × 1016J of energy.

This interchange between mass and energy is a common occurrence on the nuclear scale, particularly in the formation of nuclei, the breaking up of nuclei and radioactivity.

The mass - energy equivalence can be determined using

E = m c  2

The energy equivalence of a 1.0kg mass is given by
