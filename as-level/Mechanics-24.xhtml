Projectiles

Projectile motion is really just the application of the xuvat equations of motion in two dimensional space. The diagram below, represents a cannon ball being fired from a cannon in a horizontal position at the exact same time a duplicate cannon ball is released from rest. The diagram replicates what would be seen if a camera was set to take photographs at equal time intervals, and the position of the ball superimposed at these times. Therefore, the path (or trajectory) of the ball can be seen.

Horizontal Motion

When dealing with projectile motion, it is important to analyse the motion in the vertical and horizontal plane separately. On close inspection of the diagram, the cannon ball covers the same horizontal distance in equal time intervals. Therefore, the horizontal component of the ball’s velocity must be constant. One way to imagine this, is to think of a light shining downwards on the ball. The shadow that is cast of the ball on the ground would travel at a constant velocity to the right. This must be the case as the weight of the ball, mg is acting vertically downwards. Therefore, there is no component of the weight acting in the horizontal direction, and therefore, no forces are acting in the horizontal plane as we’ve assumed no air resistance (drag). As a consequence, there can be no acceleration in the horizontal plane.

Vertical Motion

Again, on close inspection of the diagram, we can see that the cannon ball covers increasing vertical distance in equal time intervals, demonstrating that the ball must be accelerating in the vertical plane. The vertical component of the ball’s velocity is increasing at a constant rate. One way to imagine this, is to think of a light shining to the left on the ball. The shadow that is cast of the ball on the cliff would accelerate downwards as though it was in free fall. In fact, by comparing the cannon ball and the ball that was released from rest, both can be seen to accelerate vertically downwards at the exact same rate and reach the bottom of the cliff at the same time. This must be the case as the weight is acting vertically downwards, and therefore, both objects must accelerate downwards at the same rate. This clearly shows, that the vertical and horizontal motion are completely independent of each other.
