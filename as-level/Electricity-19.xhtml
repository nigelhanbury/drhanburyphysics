Potential Difference across Components Connected in Parallel

The circuit on the right, represents a parallel circuit with two closed loops highlighted. Consider the top loop in diagram 1. The current splits at the junction, passes through the single component and returns back to the cell. Each one coulomb of charge that leaves the cell has ε joules of electrical potential energy that must be converted to other forms after passing through the single component. Therefore, the p.d. across the component must be ε. Regarding the bottom loop in diagram 2, each one coulomb of charge that leaves the cell must convert ε joules of electrical potential energy to other forms after passing through both components. Therefore, the p.d. across both components is ε. This shows that the p.d. across components connected in parallel are equal as can be seen in the diagram. Note, all the electrical potential energy must be converted to other forms when one coulomb completes any closed loop back to the cell.

The pd across components connected in parallel are equal.

Combining Resistances

Series

The circuit on the right, shows two resistors connected in series. The second diagram represents the exact same circuit as the first, where R, represents the total or combined resistance of R1 and R2. In other words, we could replace R1 and R2 with a single resistor that would produce the same current, I, for the same p.d., V.

Knowing that V=V1+V2 along with V1=IR1 and V2=IR2, we can combine these to give V=IR1+ IR2, and therefore, V=I(R1+R2 ).
