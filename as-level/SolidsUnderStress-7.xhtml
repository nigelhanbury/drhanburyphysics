Stress-Strain Graph of a Ductile Metal

The linear range extends to the limit of proportionality, with its slope denoting the Young modulus.

Elasticity (elastic behaviour) only persists up to the elastic limit, where stress removal allows for shape restoration. Past this point, they are plastic and results in permanent deformation.

At the yield point, minimal stress growth yields significant increase in strain. The stress value at this point is termed yield stress.

The graph reveals a broad plastic zone, culminating at the breaking stress or ultimate tensile strength (UTS), beyond which, the material fractures at the breaking point.

Necking

Beyond the UTS, the graph bends downwards. Within this region, necking usually occurs. Here, the cross sectional area decreases and is the point of fracture.

Elastic Deformation of a Ductile Material

When stress remains below the elastic limit, ion separation may increase minimally, yet ions can restore their original placement due to the force of attraction between them. This is regarded as elastic deformation, as ion forces act to bring them back to their initial state when the stress is eliminated. In other words, the material will return to its initial length/shape once the load is removed.

The yield point is that point at which the material shows a large increase in strain for little or no increase in stress.

The maximum stress is known as the breaking stress or ultimate tensile strength (UTS).

Strains beyond the elastic limit are plastic and will be permanently deformed.
