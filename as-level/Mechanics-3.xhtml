Homogeneity

A valid equation in physics must be homogeneous. An equation is homogeneous if, when the base units of all the quantities are written, they are the same on both sides of the equation. However, a homogeneous equation may not be correct, for example, there may be missing unitless constants in the correct equation which do not show up in the analysis. If an equation is homogeneous, it may be correct, but if it is not homogeneous, then it is definitely wrong.
