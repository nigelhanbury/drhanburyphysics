An electron is left in the 4th energy level after absorbing an incoming photon. Determine the number of distinct transitions an electron can take back to the ground state.

The atoms within a cloud of Hydrogen are excited into the 4th energy level. The corresponding emitted radiation is passed through a diffraction grating. State the number of spectral lines present in the emission spectra.

Determine the number of spectral lines that will be within the visible spectrum, and state the corresponding transitions.

Solution

The minimum photon energy required is 13.59eV.

Therefore, E =  hc _ λ  , and so, 13.59 × 1.6 × 10  -19 =  6.63 ×  10  -34  × 3 ×  10  8   _____________ λ  .

This leads to λ = 0.91 × 10  -7 m or 91nm. This corresponds to UV.

There are 6 possible transitions back to the ground state.

n = 4 to 3, n = 3 to 2, n = 2 to 1 (3 transitions)

n = 4 to 2, n = 4 to 1, n = 3 to 1 (3 transitions)

6 distinct transitions in total.

Each distinct transition will emit a photon of a particular wavelength. Since there are a very large number of Hydrogen atoms, all 6 wavelengths will be emitted between them. Therefore, 6 spectral lines will be present.

For the mathematically minded, we can make use of the general formula,  n! ___ r! (n − r) !  . This determines the number of ways we can select r objects from n.

In the case of finding the number of transitions, we want to find the number of ways we can pick 2 energy levels out of n. In this case, we would use  n! ___ 2! (n − 2) !  =  n(n − 1) _ 2  .

Therefore, the number of transitions from the nth energy level back to the ground state is  n(n − 1) _ 2  .

Since all transitions down to the ground state result in wavelengths outside of the visible spectrum, 2 spectral lines will be within the visible spectrum.

These correspond to n = 3 to 2, n = 4 to 2.
