Determine the work function of the metal in eV and Joules.

Determine the threshold wavelength, and hence, determine which part of the EM spectrum this corresponds to.

Determine Planck’s constant.

Determine the number of photoelectrons incident on the anode per second when the current is maximum, and hence, determine the number of photoelectrons emitted from the cathode per second.

If the intensity of the incident radiation was doubled, while keeping the frequency constant, state and explain the value for the maximum current.

When the applied pd is zero, the current recorded is 35µA. Explain why the current increases up to a maximum value for increasing positive pd.

Solution

From the graph, the stopping voltage is − 1.25V. Therefore, max KE is 1.25eV.

Number of electrons per second is given by  41 ×  10  -6  _______ 1.6 ×  10  -19   = 2.6 × 10  14 s  -1 . This is also the number emitted per second from the cathode.

If the intensity is doubled, then the number of photons incident on the cathode per second doubles, and hence, the number of photoelectrons emitted per second doubles. Therefore, I = 82µA.

The photoelectrons are attracted to the positive anode, increasing the kinetic energy of the photoelectrons. The number of photoelectrons incident on the anode per second increases until it is equal to the number of photoelectrons emitted.
