Resolving Vectors

The term resolving vectors is really the reverse of vector addition. The diagram on the right, represents a general vector a. Sometimes, it is useful to express a single vector as the sum of two perpendicular vectors. This process is known as resolving. In this example, the vector a, has been expressed (resolved) as the sum of the two perpendicular vectors, aH and aV as shown. The magnitude of these components are expressed as

Resolving vectors is useful in many situations. The example on the right, represents a single vector force acting on a mass along with the replacement of this force with its perpendicular components. The second diagram will produce the exact same effect as the single force. In this instance, the motion of the mass can be determined in both those directions independently. More on this later.

At AS level, you are only expected to perform numerical calculations involving the addition or subtraction of perpendicular vectors, as shown below. In this case, simple trigonometry and Pythagoras’s Theorem would be required.
