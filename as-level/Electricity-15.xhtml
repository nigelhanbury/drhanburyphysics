Superconductivity

Superconductivity is a phenomenon observed in several (but not all) metals and some ceramic materials. When some metals are cooled, using liquid helium, to temperatures near absolute zero, (0 K ≈ -273°C), their electrical resistance drops with a jump down to zero because the free electrons no longer collide with the ions in the lattice. The temperature at which electrical resistance drops to zero is called the transition temperature, θC, and this temperature is a characteristic of the material.

Diagram 1 represents a non superconductive metal and diagram 2 shows how a superconductor makes the transition to zero resistance.

Some materials (ceramics) have transition temperatures above the boiling point of liquid nitrogen (77 K ≈ -196°C). Note, Liquid nitrogen is much cheaper than liquid helium.
