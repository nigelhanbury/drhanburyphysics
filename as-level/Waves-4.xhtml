Phasors

At this point, it is worth discussing the idea of the phasor to aid in the understanding of phase difference. The diagram on the right, represents a displacement-time graph of a single particle on a wave. To the left of the graph is a phasor representation.

The red arrow represents a rotating vector (phasor) of length, A. The green line shows the vertical component of the rotating vector, and this represents the displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position. As can be seen from simple trigonometry, the displacement, x, is calculated using x=Asin(ωt), where ω is a constant. By inspection, we can see that the amplitude is equal to the length of the phasor. When the phasor has rotated through a full circle (360º or 2π radians), the particle has gone through one cycle.

Phase Difference

The diagram on the right, represents the displacement-time graph for two particles on a wave, shown in blue and red. This wave is shown on the displacement-distance graph, where the two particles have a separation of 1/4 λ.

Since the displacement-distance graph represents a snapshot of the wave at time, t = 0, we can see that the blue particle has max. displacement at t = 0 and the red particle has zero. As the wave travels to the right, the individual particle displacements will change according to the two displacement-time graphs.

The two corresponding phasors are shown. As can be seen, the two particles do not oscillate in step with each other, they are out of sync.

A Note about Phase Difference:

The phase difference between the two particles is simply a measure of how out of step these oscillations are. This is where the use of phasors can help. By inspecting the phasor, and remembering that one complete circle represents one cycle, we can see that the blue particle is ahead of the red particle within a cycle. In fact, the blue particle leads the red particle by 90º. We say that there is a phase difference of 90º between the particles.

Definition of Phase Difference

Phase difference is the difference in position of 2 points within a cycle of oscillation. It is given as a fraction of the cycle or as an angle (where one whole cycle is 2π radians or 360°) together with a statement of which point is ahead in the cycle.
