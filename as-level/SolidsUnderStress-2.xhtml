Elastic Potential Energy (Strain Energy)

We have already covered this in section 1.4. The work done to stretch a material is equal to the area under the force - distance graph. We can use this fact to derive an equation to determine the elastic potential energy (or strain energy) stored in the spring. The elastic potential energy is equal to the work done in stretching a spring.

The shaded region on the graph to the right, represents the work done in extending the spring, and hence, the stored elastic potential energy.

Therefore, E  EPE =  1 _ 2  Fx

Since F = kx , we can write E  EPE =  1 _ 2  (kx) x =  1 _ 2  k x  2

This formula, of course, is only valid up to the limit of proportionality.

For extensions beyond the limit of proportionality, the area must be estimated.

Definition of Elastic Potential Energy

This is the energy possessed by an object when it has been deformed due to forces acting on it.

Unit: J

Note, elastic potential energy can only have positive values, since x  2 > 0 .

Therefore, even when the spring is compressed, the elastic potential energy is still positive.

Example Problem

A climber falls 2.3m before being stopped by his climbing rope that is secured above him. The weight of the climber is 840N. The diagram on the right, shows a force – extension graph for the rope being used.

Use the graph to find the stiffness of the rope when it is being used with forces up to 350N. Give the appropriate unit.

Use the graph to determine the energy stored in the rope when it is stretched by 0.25m

Solution

The spring stiffness, k, is given by the gradient of the slope up to 350N, which corresponds to an extension of 0.30m.

Gradient, k =  350 _ 0.30  = 1170N m  -1

Energy stored in the rope, E  EPE =  1 _ 2  × 0.25 × 290 = 36J
