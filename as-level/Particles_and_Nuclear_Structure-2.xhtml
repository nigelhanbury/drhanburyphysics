Particle – Antiparticle Annihilation

When a particle interacts with its respective antiparticle they annihilate each other. That is, they disappear and their mass-energy manifests itself as two photons (e-m radiation). These photons are given the symbol, γ because they are very high in energy. The total energy of the photons is equal to the sum of the mass-energy and kinetic energy of the annihilating particles. The concept of mass-energy is covered in A2 physics and makes use of the famous equation E = m c  2 . In other words, their mass is converted into energy.

The diagram on the right, represents a particle and its antiparticle annihilating to produce two gamma photons.

Remembering that momentum is a vector quantity, we can add the momenta of each particle to produce the total momentum as shown in red. As can be seen, the total momentum after the annihilation is equal to the total momentum before the annihilation.

This is why at least two gamma photons need to be produced, otherwise, momentum will not be conserved.
