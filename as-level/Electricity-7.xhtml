2.2 Resistance

In the previous section, we discussed current within a conductor as the flow of mobile charge, in this case, free electrons. However, when charge passes through a wire, bulb or resistor, energy is always transferred or transformed. With this in mind, it is useful to discuss the concept of potential difference (p.d.) or voltage as this allows us to calculate the quantity of energy transferred through a component, and indeed, define an important quantity known as resistance.

Potential Difference

Potential difference, V between two points is the energy converted from electrical potential energy to some other form per unit charge that passes between those points. With reference to the diagram to the right, when a current passes through the resistor, the resistor will become warm. In addition, when a current passes through the lamp, the lamp will become warm and emit visible light. Clearly, electrical energy is being transformed into other types. In this case, heat and light.

The potential difference across the resistor is measured using a voltmeter. The voltmeter must be connected in parallel across the component. The voltmeter must have a very high resistance.

It is important to connect the voltmeter the correct way around. The positive lead (red on diagram) needs to be connected to the positive side of the circuit, as shown in the diagram.

The positive side of the lamp is said to have a greater electrical potential energy compared to the negative. In other words, the decrease in electrical potential energy across the lamp is equal to the potential difference across the lamp.

Energy Transfer in a Conductor

Free electrons collide with the positive metal ions in the lattice. The kinetic energy of the free electrons is transferred to vibrational energy of the lattice, or kinetic energy of the free electrons is transferred to thermal energy in the metal. Therefore, this is the nature of potential difference as it is in the context of energy transfer.

We can link potential difference to work done. Alternatively, the potential difference between two points is the work done in moving a unit charge between those points.

Here, when one coulomb passes through the filament lamp, 12 joules of electrical potential energy is transformed into heat and light.
