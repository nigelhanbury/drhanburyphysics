Example Problem

The diagram below, shows 4 identical copper conductors connected in parallel with each conductor having a diameter of 1mm. This combination is connected in series with an identical conductor of diameter 2mm. A current of 3A flows through the network. The number density of copper is 8.4 × 10  28 m   -3 .

Determine:

The cross sectional area of the 2mm diameter conductor

The mean drift velocity of the free electrons travelling through the 2mm conductor

The cross sectional area of one of the 1mm conductors

The mean drift velocity of the free electrons travelling through one of the 1mm conductors

The current through one of the 1mm conductors and comment on the result.

1mm diameter

2mm diameter

3A

3A

Solution

Using A =   πd  2  __ 4  , gives A =  π ×  (2 ×  10  -3 )  2   ________ 4  = 3.14 × 10  -6 m  2

Substituting into I = nAve , gives 3 = (8.4 ×  10  28 ) × (3.14 ×  10  -6 ) × v × (1.6 ×  10  -19 ) . This gives v = 7.1 × 10  -5 m s  -1 .

Recognising that A ∝ d  2 , gives A =  1 _ 4  × 3.14 × 10  -6  = 7.9 ×  10  -7  m  2 .

Since the total cross sectional area of the parallel combination is equal to the cross sectional area of the 2mm conductor, and both have the same current, the mean drift velocity must be the same as previously calculated. Therefore, v = 7.1 × 10  -5 m s  -1 .

As the free electrons are passing through one quarter of the cross sectional area of the 2mm conductor and are travelling with the same mean drift velocity, then the current must be one quarter of the current passing through the 2mm conductor. Therefore, I =  1 _ 4  × 3 = 0.75A .

This shows that the 3A current must split equally at the junction.
