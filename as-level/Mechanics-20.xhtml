The diagram below, represents a velocity-time graph with its corresponding acceleration-time graph. Remembering that instantaneous acceleration is defined as the rate of change of velocity, it follows that the gradient of the tangent of a velocity-time graph will give the instantaneous acceleration. The linear (straight) regions of the graph represent a constant acceleration as the gradient is constant at all points along the linear portions. The linear regions with a negative gradient, B – C, D – E, will have a negative acceleration. Negative acceleration is commonly referred to as deceleration.

The area between the line (or curve) and the time axis of a acceleration-time graph represents the velocity. The first shaded area above, represents the increase in velocity up to point A on the graph. The second shaded region represents the decrease in velocity from point B to point C on the graph. The reason for this, is that the area under the time axis is negative, and hence, represents a negative velocity between these two points. Since the final velocity at point G is zero, it follows that the total area under the graph must also be zero.
