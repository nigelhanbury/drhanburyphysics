The Colour of Stars

The visible colour of a star corresponds to the wavelength where spectral intensity is greatest. If a star peaks in the middle of the visible spectrum, it is expected to be white (like the Sun).

If it peaks on the left of the visible, then it is more likely to appear blue, and if it peaks on the right of the visible, then it is more likely to appear red.

Power Radiated by a Star

While the peak of the black body curve defines the colour of a star, most of the power is radiated in longer wavelengths, i.e. to the right of the peak, because of the shape of the curve.

The area under the graph is a measure of the total power radiated from the star in all directions. The star is considered a black body as it radiates EM radiation equally in all directions.

Stefan-Boltzmann Law (or Stefan’s Law)

It is found that the power radiated from a surface per unit area is proportional to the fourth power of its temperature in Kelvin.

This lead to P = σA T  4

Where A represents the total surface area at temperature, T, and σ is known as Stefan’s constant, given by σ = 5.67 × 10  -8 W m  -2 K  -4 .

It is common to refer to the power radiated by a star as luminosity, L, and so, L = σA T  4 .

Definition of Stefan-Boltzmann Law

The total electromagnetic radiation energy emitted per unit time by a black body is given by

power = σA T  4

Where A is the body’s surface area and σ is a constant called the Stefan constant.
