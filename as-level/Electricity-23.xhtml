At time t = 0, the filament lamp is switched on. The graph shows how the output p.d., Vout varies with time. Describe and explain the features of the graph.

At “switch on” (t = 0) the filament lamp is at room temperature and as such, its resistance is low.

After “switch on” the current causes a heating effect, and so, the temperature of the lamp begins to rise. As the temperature rises, the resistance of the filament lamp increases and its share of the total p,d, increases. Since the total p.d. is constant, the p.d. across resistor R, and hence Vout, falls.

As the temperature rises, the rate at which the lamp loses heat to the surroundings increases. When the rate at which energy is dissipated in the lamp is equal to the rate at which heat is lost to the surroundings, the temperature of the lamp reaches a maximum value and remains constant. Therefore, the resistance of the lamp reaches a maximum value and remains constant. Hence, the p.d. across the lamp reaches a maximum constant value and Vout reaches a minimum value and remains constant – the gradient of the graph is zero.

When the switch is closed, the pd across R2 is given by

V = IR = 0.3 × 10 = 3.0V , and therefore, the pd across the parallel combination AB must be 3.0V.

Since the pd across the parallel combination is half the supply emf of 6.0V, it follows that the resistance of the parallel combination, RC must be half of the total resistance.

Therefore,   R  C  __  R  C  + 2.0   = 0.5      . This results in RC = 2.0Ω.
