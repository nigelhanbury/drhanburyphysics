1.4 Energy Concepts

Work Done

The work done by a force is the product of the magnitude of the force and the distance moved in the direction of the force.

Prior to AS physics, the direction of the force was always parallel to the distance moved. At AS, the force, F can be at an angle, θ to the distance moved, x as shown on the diagram to the right.

In this case, the work done is the product of the component of the force parallel to the distance moved and the distance, x or .

Every time work is done, energy is always transferred. Therefore, work and energy have the same unit. Work Done Against Friction

The diagram on the right, represents a block being pulled along a rough horizontal surface at a constant velocity.

Here, there is no change in kinetic energy, yet work is still being done by the force, F and there must still exist a transfer of energy. In this case, since the work is being done against the frictional force, FR the work done is transferred to thermal energy of the surroundings and to the block and surface. More precisely, the work done equals the gain in kinetic energy of the surrounding air molecules and the molecules of the block and surface.

At the point when the object comes to a complete stop, all of the initial kinetic energy is transferred to thermal energy in the surroundings.

Since frictional forces result in wasted energy, these forces are known as dissipative forces as they cause energy to be dissipated to the surroundings (wasted).
