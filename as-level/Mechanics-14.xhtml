Centre of Mass/Centre of Gravity

The centre of mass of a body is that point within a body where all the mass appears to act.

The centre of gravity of a body is that point within a body where all the weight appears to act. The weight vector force, therefore, is drawn vertically down from that point.

The centre of mass/gravity of a body of uniform density is located at the geometric centre as shown to the right.

Stability

Diagram A on the right, shows a square sheet in three different tilt positions. The weight of the cuboid acts through the centre of gravity and vertically downwards. In the first and third position, the reaction force, R is shown at the pivot point. If moments are taken about the pivot, it can be seen that there exists a resultant moment, which will cause the cuboid to rotate in the direction shown, and restore the sheet to stability. In the central position, the line of action of the weight passes straight through the pivot point, and hence, a zero resultant moment exists. Therefore, no rotation (or toppling) will occur. This is known as unstable equilibrium, as the cuboid is on the verge of toppling.

Diagram B, shows a square sheet of material suspended by a cord at one end. Similarly, the first and third positions will produce a resultant moment about the pivot and cause rotation in the direction shown. Again, in the central position, the line of action of the tension passes straight through the pivot point, and hence, a zero resultant moment exists, and no rotation will occur. This is known as stable equilibrium, as a nudge in either direction will cause a resultant moment which will restore the sheet to its equilibrium position

Solution
