Demonstrating Stationary Waves

The diagram on the right, represents a taught wire connected to an oscillator/vibrator. A progressive wave is initiated by the vibrator and travels towards the pulley. The wave reflects off the pulley and passes through the incident wave. The reflected and incident waves have equal or almost equal amplitudes and equal frequency. The waves superpose and produce a resultant wave made up of nodes and antinodes.

Since both ends of the wire are fixed, nodes must exist at the two end.

The fundamental frequency, f0, is the minimum frequency of the stationary wave. The fundamental frequency is also known as the first harmonic. As we increase the frequency of the vibrator, only standing waves with nodes at each end can form, as these ends are fixed. The diagram on the right, shows the modes of vibration as we increase the frequency. The second mode has double the frequency of the fundamental, so we refer to this as the 2nd harmonic. In general, harmonics are the integer number multiples of the fundamental frequency.

We can calculate the frequency of each mode by using c=fλ, and appreciating that the speed, c, of the progressive waves that make up the stationary wave is constant (provided tension is constant).
