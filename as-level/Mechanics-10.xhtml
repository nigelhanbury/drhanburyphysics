Moments of a Force

Forces can make an object rotate. The moment of a force is sometimes referred to as a turning force. The greater the turning force, the easier it is to make an object rotate. The diagrams below, represent two situations where a moment of a force causes rotation. Diagram B, represents the simple example of applying a force to a spanner (or wrench) in order to undo a bolt. Since the bolt is pivoted, the force produces a moment about the pivot (or bolt), causing the bolt and spanner to rotate in an anti-clockwise direction. We say that the force produces an anti-clockwise moment about the pivot. The longer the handle, the easier it is to turn the bolt, and assuming the same force is applied at the end, the greater the moment.

Diagram A, represents an applied force on the handle of a wheelbarrow. The weight of the wheelbarrow and contents acts vertically down as shown and wants to rotate the wheelbarrow in a clockwise direction, and so, creates a clockwise moment about the pivot. The applied force produces an anti-clockwise moment about the pivot. Since the distance of the applied force is much further from the pivot compared to that of the weight, it is relatively easy to lift the wheelbarrow. To lift the wheelbarrow there must be a resultant (or net) anti-clockwise moment about the pivot.

Definition of a Moment

The moment (or torque) of a force about a point is defined as the force × the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force, i.e.

moment = F × d 

S.I. Unit: Nm

Prior to A level, moments are taken about a pivot, however, moments can be taken about any point, and it does not have to be within the body itself.

Diagram A, represents the simplest situation. The dotted line represents the line of action of the force. This is an imaginary line that runs parallel to the force. d is the perpendicular distance between the stated point and this dotted line. Here, the moment about the stated point is determined by Fd.

Diagram B, represents a more general case, where the force is acting at an arbitrary angle. Here, we can see that d and the line of action of the force are perpendicular, and the moment is again, determined by Fd.

In both diagrams, the moment about the stated point is deemed an anti-clockwise moment.

When quoting a moment, you must always state the rotational sense of clockwise or anti-clockwise.

To determine the rotational sense, imagine the point you are taking moments as a pivot and imagine which way the body would rotate with the applied force.
