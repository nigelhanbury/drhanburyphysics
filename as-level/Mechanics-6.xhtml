Vector Addition

With regard to vector addition, we will introduce the concept via displacement vectors, and from this, all other vector quantities will follow the exact same rules.

The diagram on the right, represents a journey between two points. A walk of 100m in the direction shown is represented by vector a, and a further walk of 290m in a different direction represented by vector b. These vectors are known as displacement vectors. A displacement vector is defined as the distance covered in a particular direction.

The resultant displacement is represented by vector c (in red), and shows the equivalent journey that could have been made. In terms of vectors, the resultant is represented by . Notice, that the vector addition of these two vectors is represented arrow to tail as shown in the diagram. It is common to use a double arrow for the resultant vector.

Diagram A on the right, represents two forces, F1 and F2, acting on a spherical mass. We can apply the exact same rule, as above, in order to determine the vector sum of the force vectors.

Notice, that it does not matter in which order we apply the arrow to tail rule, as it produces the same resultant force.

Diagram B, shows that we can replace the two individual forces with a single force, known as the resultant force, that will produce the exact same effect.

Parallelogram Rule

So far, we have used the arrow to tail method for vector addition. Alternatively, and sometimes more intuitive, we can apply the parallelogram rule.

The diagram on the right, shows the parallelogram rule. Simply construct two dotted lines parallel to both forces. The intersection point, represents the end of the resultant force vector.

The parallelogram rule can only be applied to two vectors only, whereas, the arrow to tail method can be applied to any number of vectors.
