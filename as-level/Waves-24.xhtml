Generating Stationary Sound Waves

The diagram on the right, represents a loud speaker placed directly in front of a hard surface, such as a metal sheet. As can be seen, a stationary wave is set up. It is important to remember that sound is a longitudinal wave, and the stationary wave drawn, merely represents how the amplitude of the oscillating particles changes with position. The diagram shows that the particles direction of oscillation is parallel to the line joining the speaker and the solid surface

As before, there is a node at the hard surface, since no vibration can occur at that point, however, an antinode must exist at the loud speaker cone. This is because the particles are free to oscillate with maximum amplitude when the stationary wave is formed.

As before, the fundamental frequency, f0, is the minimum frequency of the stationary wave. Here, the stationary waves that can form are constrained by the need for a node at the fixed end, and an antinode at the position of the loud speaker cone. The diagram on the right, represent the first four modes for increasing sound frequency.
