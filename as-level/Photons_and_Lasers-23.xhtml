Semiconductor Lasers

Semiconductor lasers, also referred to as laser diodes, are the most commonly used lasers. These lasers achieve a high population inversion by using electric current to pump electrons to the upper energy levels. As such semiconductor lasers achieve a much higher efficiency compared to conventional lasers. This allows them to use mirrors that transmit up to 60% of the photons, and produce a relatively powerful laser beam with a low energy input.

While semiconductor lasers cannot produce the high power beams that conventional lasers do, they can be used in most common applications, due to their small size and low power input requirement. With the advancement of semiconductor technology, these lasers can be mass produced at very low cost per unit.

This low cost makes them very popular in applications such as:

CD/DVD

BluRay player

Optical fibres for communications

Supermarket barcode scanners

Laser printers and many other uses.

Binary data can be read from an optical disc via a laser scanning the surface. By the process of interference, the reflected laser light is modulated to encode digital information.

Bar code scanners use a laser that sweeps across a bar code. The reflected light is modulated to contain digital information regarding a particular product.

Laser pulses representing digital information are sent into a fibre optic cable. By the process of total internal reflection, the digital information is sent along the fibre and reconstructed at the other end.

Advantages Compared to Conventional Lasers:

High population inversion using electric current

Higher efficiency

Relatively high power with low energy input

Small size

Low cost
