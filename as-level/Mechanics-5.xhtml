Scalar and Vector quantities

Scalars

Scalars are quantities that have magnitude only. Examples of scalar quantities are: mass, temperature, pressure, energy etc. It would not make sense for mass, for example, to have a direction. Note, some scalar quantities can have negative values, such as temperature and potential energy. Many students are often confused regarding this. The negative sign does not indicate direction, merely where zero is defined.

Addition of scalars

The addition of scalars is simply the numerical sum of the individual scalars, eg, 3kg + 7kg = 10kg. Another example, this time involving the calculation of total energy. A satellite in orbit has a kinetic energy of 2000J and a gravitational potential energy of -3000J. Here, the total energy is the sum of kinetic and potential,

2000J + (-3000J) = -1000J. This will be examined in Unit4, and is used here as an example only.

Vectors

Vectors are quantities that have both magnitude and direction. Examples of vector quantities are: displacement, velocity, acceleration, momentum, force etc. Here, for example, velocity is the speed of an object in a given direction, and force is applied in a given direction.

Vectors are represented by arrows. The length of the arrow represents the magnitude or size of the quantity, and its direction represents the direction of the vector, eg, the direction of the applied force. The direction can be expressed as an angle as shown in the diagram.
