Wavefront View of Young’s Fringes

The diagram below, represents Young’s double slit experiment. Plane wavefronts are incident on the double slits and diffract when passing through. The diffracted wavefronts spread out and overlap (superpose) as shown.

The antinodal lines connect points of constructive interference where the wavefronts overlap. Since wavefronts are defined as lines of constant phase, the points where wavefronts meet must be in phase.

Intensity Distribution of Young’s Fringes

In reality, the central fringe is the brightest, with the surrounding fringes becoming dimmer as they move further from the centre.

The diagram below, shows how the intensity of the interference pattern changes across the screen.

The dotted curve shows the modulating envelope, which is responsible for the diminishing intensity.

In order to obtain a suitable number of maxima:

The slits must be narrow in order that the diffracted waves spread out and overlap

The slit separation must be small to further ensure the diffracted waves overlap

The modulating envelope is due to the diffraction of waves at a single slit. The reason for this is beyond the scope of A level physics. The narrower the slit, the wider the envelope becomes, and further distant maxima become more visible.
