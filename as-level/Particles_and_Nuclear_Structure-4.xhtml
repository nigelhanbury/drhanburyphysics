Although the ∆+ and ∆0 have the same quark composition as the proton and neutron respectively, ∆ baryons have a higher mass than their nucleon equivalents. This is due to them being in an excited state, and therefore, having greater energy. We can consider the ∆+ particle as an excited state of the proton and the ∆0 particle as an excited state of the neutron, and is therefore, defined as a different particle.

Since ∆ baryons have a higher mass than the neutron and proton, they are unstable and have a short mean lifetime of around 10  -23 s .

Baryon Number

All baryons have a property called a baryon number (B). All baryons have a baryon number of B = +1 and all anti-baryons have a baryon number of B = −1 . The baryon number is conserved in all particle interactions along with electrical charge, Q.

Since all baryons are composed of three quarks, it makes sense that the baryon number of any quark is B = +  1 _ 3  and all anti-quarks have a baryon number of B = −  1 _ 3  .

Mesons

Definition: A meson is a hadron consisting of a quark-antiquark pair ( q _ q ).

A meson has a baryon number, B = 0 as a meson is not a baryon. We can also show this using the baryon number of quarks. B = (+   1 _ 3  ) + (−   1 _ 3  ) = 0 .

There are many different types of mesons, however, specific knowledge of these is not required for WJEC. You may be asked questions involving specific mesons, however, either you will be provided additional information in the examination or general knowledge of meson quark composition will be sufficient.
