Conditions for Static Equilibrium

Translational Equilibrium

If the resultant (or net) force acting on an object is zero, then the object will either remain at rest, or continue to move at a constant speed in a straight line (i.e. at a constant velocity). This is Newton’s first law of motion. Such an object is said to be in translational equilibrium.

Rotational Equilibrium

If the resultant moment about any point on an object is zero, then the object will either rotate at a constant rate, or not rotate at all. Such an object is said to be in rotational equilibrium.

Equilibrium under the action of forces and moments

An object is said to be in equilibrium (or static equilibrium) if:

The resultant force acting on it is zero

The resultant moment (or torque) about any stated point is zero.

The diagram below, represents a cuboid in equilibrium resting on a slope. The weight, mg acts vertically downwards from the centre of gravity, the normal contact force, N perpendicular to the surface of the slope, and the frictional force, F acting up the slope and parallel to the slope.

The first condition is met as if we perform a vector force addition using the arrow to tail method to determine the resultant force, we can see the it forms a closed triangle. This demonstrates that the resultant force on the cuboid is zero.

The second condition is that the resultant (or net) moment about any point is zero. On close examination of the diagram, we can see that the line of action of all three forces meet at a single point. Therefore, if we took moments about this point, the resultant moment would be zero, and hence, the resultant moment about any point would be zero.

Special Case (Three Forces):

If three forces are in static equilibrium, then the line of action of all three forces must intersect. This only applies to three forces.
