Absorption Spectra

Excitation can also occur when an atom absorbs a photon. An atom can make a transition from a lower energy level to a higher energy level by absorbing a photon, provided that the photon energy is equal to the difference between two energy levels of the atom. Any photon whose energy is not equal to the difference between two energy levels will not be absorbed, unless it’s energy is great enough to cause ionisation.

The diagram above, shows the first five energy levels of Hydrogen. The incoming photon has an exact energy equal to 1.89eV, which corresponds to the exact difference in energy between n = 2 and n = 3. The photon will be absorbed, and the atom will be excited into n = 3. The corresponding wavelength of the absorbed photon is 658nm (Red). Note, the photon will only be absorbed if the atom is already in an excited state corresponding to n = 2.

If white light passes through a Hydrogen gas cloud, and the emerging light is passed through a diffraction grating, then a absorption spectra is produced. The diagram on the right, shows such a spectra. Since white light is made up of a continuous range of wavelengths, there must be a stream of photons with a continuous range of energies. Only those specific photons that have an energy equal to the energy difference between levels will be absorbed, otherwise, they will pass straight through. The continuous spectrum on the right, represents the continues range of wavelengths of white light, and the dark lines, the specific wavelengths absorbed.

The vast majority of the Hydrogen atoms making up the cloud will be in the ground state. However, there will be a statistical distribution of atomic energy levels occupied beyond the ground state. Therefore, there will be some atoms already present in the n = 2 excited state and higher.

Definition of Ionisation

Ionisation is the removal of one or more electrons from an atom. If the energy absorbed by an atom is sufficient to allow an electron to escape from the atom, the atom is said to be ionised.

Definition of Ionisation Energy

The ionisation energy of an atom is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the atom in its ground state.

Unit: J or eV. For hydrogen this is 13.6 eV or 2.18 × 10-18J.
