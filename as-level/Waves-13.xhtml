Two Source Interference

Diagram 1 represents waves from two point sources, S1and S2, arriving at point P, and constructively interfering and re-enforce each other. The wave path S1P=7λ and S2P=8λ. The difference in wave path is known as the path difference. In this case, the path difference, pd, is equal to pd=S2P - S1P=1λ. As a consequence, the waves arrive at P in phase. More generally, constructive interference will occur if the path difference is an integer number of wavelengths. In other words, when S2P - S1P=nλ, where n=0,1,2,3,… Waves arriving at a point are said to be in phase if they have the same frequency and are at the same point in their cycles at the same time.

Diagram 2 represents waves from two point sources, S1and S2, arriving at point P, and destructively interfering and cancel each other out. The wave path S1P=7λ and S2P=7½λ. In this case, the path difference, pd, is equal to pd=S2P - S1P=½λ. As a consequence, the waves arrive at P in anti-phase (180º phase difference). More generally, destructive interference will occur if the path difference is

S2P - S1P=(n+½)λ, where n=0,1,2,3,…

Diagram 1 below, represents the displacement-time graphs for a particle at point, P due to waves arriving from source S1 and S2 resulting in constructive interference. Since the two oscillations are in phase, the vector addition of the individual displacements at each point is doubled, and hence, the resulting wave has double the amplitude. Diagram 2 below, represents the displacement-time graphs for a particle at point, P due to waves arriving from source S1 and S2, resulting in destructive interference. Since the two oscillations are in anti-phase, the vector addition of the individual displacements is zero, and hence, there is no resulting oscillation.
