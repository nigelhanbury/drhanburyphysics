Recap of Atomic Structure

An atom is comprised of a positively charged nucleus made up of neutrons and protons. The nucleus has a positive charge since the protons have a positive charge.

Negatively charged electrons orbit around the nucleus. These orbit in what is known as orbital shells as shown in the diagram on the right.

For an electrically neutral atom, the number of electrons must equal the number of protons in the nucleus.

The number of protons within the nucleus dictates the elements within the periodic table. Nuclei (pleural for nucleus) with differing numbers of protons are different elements.

The above diagram represents the element Oxygen, with symbol  8  16  O . Here, the top number represents the total number of neutrons and protons within the nucleus, and the bottom number represents the total number of protons. In this case, there are 8 neutrons and 8 protons that make the Oxygen nucleus, resulting in a total of 16 nucleons.

Particle Interactions

Beta Minus Decay

A common particle interaction is the radioactive decay of Carbon into Nitrogen. The nuclear equation is shown below. We can see that the decay involves the emission of an electron and an anti-electron neutrino.

 6  14  C →  7  14  N +  e  −  +  _ ν  e

On inspection, we can see that since the total number of neutrons and protons does not change (14), and the number of protons has changed ( 6 → 7 ), then a neutron must have turned into a proton. The equation can be expressed in terms of baryons and leptons (or a particle equation), as shown below.

n → p + e  − + _ ν  e

The table shows the total quantity of charge, lepton number and baryon number before and after the decay. We can also write the equation in terms of quarks.

(d d u) → (u u d) + e  − + _ ν  e

d → u + e  − + _ ν  e
