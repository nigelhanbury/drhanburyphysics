Expressing Quantities in Terms of S.I. Base Units

Sometimes, it is a requirement to express derived units in terms of S.I. base units. The examples above seem pretty easy, but others are not obvious. An example of this, is the derived unit for energy, J. In order to do this, we must choose an equation that allows us to calculate energy in terms of variables that are already in S.I. base units. The example below, demonstrates this process for expressing energy in terms of S.I. base units.

The simplest energy formula is work done, or W=Fd, where F represents force, and d, distance.

Here, F is not a base unit, so our initial step is to quote F in S.I. base units.

Therefore, 1J=1kgm2 s-2. We have now expressed the unit of energy in terms of S.I. base units.
