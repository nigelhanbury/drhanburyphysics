Help — Moments
Three Modes
Single Pivot — one pivot; check if the beam balances by pressing Check Balance.
Two Supports — two supports; reaction forces R₁ and R₂ update live as you change weights and positions.
Angled Force — explore M = F × d with a single force applied at an angle. Switch between F × d_perp and F_y × d_x calculation methods.
Building a Scene
Click the beam to add a weight at that position.
Drag a weight along the beam to reposition it.
Drag the pivot triangle(s) underneath the beam to move them.
Use the sliders in the Weights panel for precise mass and position values.
Try the Examples panel for ready-made scenarios.
Deleting Weights
Right-click a weight on the beam (laptop).
Long-press a weight for ~½ second (iPad / touchscreen).
Or use the ✕ button on the weight's row in the side panel.
Revealing Force Values
Hover over a weight or reaction arrow to see its value briefly.
Click / tap a reaction arrow to lock the value display on; click again to hide.
Use the Lines of Action tickbox below the canvas to show extended dashed lines through each force.
Single Pivot — Balance Check
Press Check Balance to test whether moments balance about the pivot.
The beam tips clockwise or anticlockwise if unbalanced, or stays level if balanced.
A Challenge Mode (in the Game panel) generates unknown weights for students to find.
Two Supports — Reaction Forces
R₁ + R₂ = total downward force (vertical equilibrium).
Take moments about either support to find the other reaction.
If R₁ or R₂ goes negative the beam tips off that support — shown by the animation and the TIPPING! label.
Enable Use beam weight to add a uniform weight to the beam itself.
Angled Force — Two Methods
Line of Action method: M = F × d, where d is the perpendicular distance from pivot to the line of the force.
Components method: Resolve the force into F_x and F_y. Only F_y produces a moment about a horizontal beam.
Both methods give the same answer — the panel shows them side by side.
Use Hide d value or Hide component values for "predict, then check" questions.
Help — Stability
What This Tab Shows
A block sits on an inclined slope. Adjust the slope angle and the block's geometry to explore when the block topples.
The block topples when the vertical line of action of its weight falls outside the downhill base corner.
Friction is not modelled here — this tab focuses on toppling only. Friction and sliding are covered in Static Equilibrium → Block on Slope.
Controls
Slope Angle — tilt the surface.
Block Width / Height — change the block's footprint.
CoG Position — move the centre of gravity within the block. Higher CoG → tips more easily; narrower base → tips more easily.
Display Options
Dimensions — show width and height labels on the block.
Weight & Line of Action — show the weight vector and its vertical line of action from the CoG.
Critical Angle — overlay the critical-angle geometry so students can see why the block topples at that angle.
Direct Manipulation on Canvas
Drag the slope directly to change angle (the slider follows).
Drag the labels (mg, CoG, dimensions, angles) to reposition them for clarity.
Double-click (or double-tap on iPad) a moved label to return it to its default position.
What to Look For
The critical angle for toppling depends on the block's base width and the height of its CoG: θc = arctan(width / 2 ÷ CoG height).
When the slope reaches the critical angle, the weight vector passes through the downhill corner — beyond that the block topples.
Wider base or lower CoG → larger critical angle → harder to tip.
Help — Static Equilibrium
Four Scenarios
Block on Slope — single block; explores forces parallel and perpendicular to a slope.
Ladder — ladder against a wall with a person on it; moments and friction at the base.
Hinged Rod — horizontal rod hinged at one end, held by a strut; tension and hinge reaction.
Hinged Door — vertical door on two hinges; weight distribution between upper and lower hinges.
Common Gestures (All Scenarios)
Drag the slope, wall, ladder ends, person, hinges, or rod endpoints directly on the canvas.
Click / tap a force vector to lock its value display on; click again to hide.
Drag labels (W, R, F, T, etc.) to reposition them.
Drag the triangle-of-forces panel to move it where you want.
Double-click / double-tap a moved label or panel to return it to its default position.
Block on Slope
Use the calculation-method tickbox to switch between the two ways of resolving W: along the slope or vertically.
F is the friction force, drawn along the slope. R is the normal reaction, perpendicular to the slope.
Toggle Parallelogram to select up to two forces and see them combined head-to-tail.
The triangle of forces in the panel updates live as you change the slope angle.
Ladder
Move the person up and down the ladder by dragging.
Take moments about the base to find the wall reaction (no friction at the wall).
Vertical equilibrium gives the normal reaction at the base; horizontal gives the friction needed.
If required friction exceeds μR, the ladder slips — shown by the warning.
Hinged Rod
Drag the load along the rod, or drag the strut anchor (D) on the wall.
Tension T acts along the strut. Hinge reaction R at A balances the rest.
Toggle Show tension components to resolve T into T_x and T_y at the strut's upper end.
Toggle Show hinge components for the corresponding decomposition of R at A.
Hinged Door
Drag the door's edges to change width/height; drag the CoG marker to move the centre of gravity.
Drag either hinge to adjust its position along the door's vertical edge.
Top hinge typically pulls outwards; bottom hinge pushes inwards — together they provide a couple to balance the weight's moment.
Their vertical components share the weight; total vertical reaction = door weight.
