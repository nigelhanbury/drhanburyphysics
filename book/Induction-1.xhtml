Magnetic flux is a tremendously useful concept within electromagnetism, and is used extensively in the theory of AC generators and transformers etc. Firstly, we will define magnetic flux, and later, build upon this in order to determine induced EMFs in circuits due to changing magnetic fields.

Magnetic flux 𝛷, is measured in Wb (Weber)

Magnetic flux = magnetic flux density × area perpendicular to the field

Or in equation form, 𝛷 = BA .

B = Magnetic flux density (T), A = Area perpendicular to lines of flux (m2)

The diagram on the right shows magnetic field lines (lines of flux) passing through a cross sectional area, where the lines of flux are perpendicular to this area.

It is important to note, that the above equation is defined based on the fact that B and A are perpendicular.

Using the above equation, 1T = 1Wbm − 2 . The magnetic flux passing through a particular area can be thought of as representing the total number of lines of flux passing through that area. In the same sense, the magnetic field strength (magnetic flux density) B, can be thought of as representing the number of lines of flux per unit area.

If the lines of flux are not perpendicular to the area, the magnetic flux can still be calculated.

In this instance, we must use 𝛷 = B  P  A , where BP represents the component of the flux density B, perpendicular to the area as shown in the diagram on the right (remembering that B is a vector quantity), where B  P = Bcosθ .

Therefore, 𝛷 = BAcosθ .

Using the above equation, it can be seen that the flux is maximum when θ = 0° (perpendicular), and zero when θ = 90° (parallel).

In other words, when the lines of flux are parallel to the area, there are no lines of flux actually passing through the area, and so, the flux passing through the area must be zero.
