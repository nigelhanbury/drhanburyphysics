Intensity and the Inverse Square Law

Since stars behave as black bodies, the energy radiated from the surface spreads out in all directions. As such, the energy passing through a given area per unit time decreases with increasing distance from the star. In other words, the star appears less bright, the further we are from the star. The diagram on the right, represents a small area on the surface of a star at a distance r, from its centre. The area of this surface is given by A, and it emits a quantity of energy per unit time from its surface. As can be seen, if the distance from the star’s centre is doubled, the corresponding area that this emitted energy passes through increases by a factor of 4. Likewise, if the distance increases by a factor of 3, the corresponding area increases by a factor of 9.

Defining the intensity, I, as I =  power _ area  gives the energy radiated per unit time per unit area. The corresponding unit being W m  -2 .
