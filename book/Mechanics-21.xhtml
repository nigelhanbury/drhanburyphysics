Distance-time/Speed-time Graphs

It is very important to recognise the difference between displacement and distance, velocity and speed. The diagram on the right, shows how the distance and speed of the object on the previous page changes with time.

Remember, that distance is a scalar and only has magnitude. In other words, the distance can only continue to increase or stop increasing. It can never decrease. The gradient of the tangent represents the instantaneous speed.

Likewise, for speed. Compare these two graphs with those on the previous page. The total displacement was found to be zero, indicating that the object returned to its initial starting point. Here, the total distance is not zero, and represents the round-trip distance back to the start.
