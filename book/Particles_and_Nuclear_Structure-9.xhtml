The Four Fundamental Forces (interactions)

Physicists recognise that there are four fundamental interactions (forces), which govern the interactions between particles. The below table needs to be memorised.

Range

Comments

Gravitational

All matter

Infinite

Very weak - negligible except between large objects such as planets

Electromagnetic (EM)

All charged particles

Strong

Very short

( ∼ 10  -18 s )

Only significant when the e-m and strong interactions do not operate

The gravitational and electromagnetic forces are detectable and quantifiable at a large scale, while the strong and weak forces are only significant when dealing with particles at the subatomic level.

The stability of nuclei is brought about by the final three forces (gravitational is completely negligible at the subatomic scale):

The electromagnetic force is responsible for keeping electrons in orbit around the nucleus.

The strong force is what binds quarks to form hadrons, and also holds together protons and neutrons in the nucleus by counteracting the electromagnetic repulsion between the protons.

Beta decay, which is the process of decay of neutrons and protons, is regulated by the weak force.

It is important that you learn this table.
