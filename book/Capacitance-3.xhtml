The capacitance (amount of charge per unit pd) that a capacitor can store depends on three things:

The cross sectional area of one of the plates

The distance between the plates

The permittivity of the material between the plates

In WJEC, the dielectric material used between the plates is air, where the permittivity of air is considered very close to the permittivity of empty space ε0, which will be used in the section on electric fields.

The capacitance C is found to be:

Directly proportional to the cross sectional area of one plate, C ∝ A

Inversely proportional to the distance between the plates C ∝  1 _ d 

The equation to calculate the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by C =   𝜀  0 A __ d  .

Energy Stored in a Capacitor

ΔQ

Charge, Q

Potential difference, V

ΔW ≈ VΔQ

In order to charge the capacitor, work must be done in moving a charge Q, from one plate to the other so that they are left with an equal and opposite charge. As the charge Q, increases, so does the potential difference V, across the plates.

Referring to the graph, consider the area sliced up into many strips, where one is shown. Consider a very small charge ∆Q, that is transferred from one plate to the other. During this small transfer of charge, V can be considered approximately constant, and so, the work ∆W, in moving the charge from one plate to the other is ΔW ≈ VΔQ , which corresponds to the approximate area highlighted.

The approximate total work done in charging the capacitor is then the sum of all the strip areas. As the strip width ∆Q, tends to zero, the number of strips tends to infinity, and so, the exact work done in charging the capacitor is the area under the graph W =  1 _ 2  QV . The work done in charging the capacitor is known as the stored energy, where the standard symbol is U. By combining C =  Q _ V  and W =  1 _ 2  QV , the following three equations shown on the right can be used to calculate the stored energy in a capacitor.
