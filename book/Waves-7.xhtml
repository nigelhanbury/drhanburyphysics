Wavefronts

The diagram on the right represents a ripple tank. This is a standard piece of laboratory equipment that is used to observe wavefronts generated by an oscillator or vibrator. Up to now, we have looked at waves on a rope, which is a one dimensional view. Most waves are two or three dimensional.

The setup comprises a shallow tank of water with a bright light source directly above, and a viewing screen immediately below. A vibrator, which is attached to a paddle is connected to a power supply and positioned on the water surface. The vibrator can be held in place using a clamp attached to a retort stand. The frequency of the vibrator can be adjusted. The paddle will oscillate in the vertical plane, perpendicular to the water surface.

This will produce a series of wavefronts that will propagate away from the paddle. The wavefronts can be visualised as the crests of the wave. The distance between the wavefronts is the wavelength, λ. The wavefronts produced in the above example are straight, as they are produced with a rectangular paddle. These are known as plane wavefronts.

The diagrams on the right, represent plane and circular wavefronts. The circular wavefronts are produced by replacing the rectangular paddle with a circular paddle.

Definition of a Wavefront

A wavefront is a continuous line (or surface), which connects points of a wave which are oscillating in phase. The wave propagation directions (rays) are at right angles to the wavefronts.
