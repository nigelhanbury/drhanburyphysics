Leptons

Leptons are fundamental particles, therefore, they are not composed of particles.

The diagram on the right, represents the first generation leptons along with their corresponding anti-particle.

Lepton Number

All leptons have a property called a lepton number (L). All leptons have a lepton number of L = +1 and all anti-leptons have a lepton number of L = −1 . The lepton number is conserved in all particle interactions along with electrical charge, Q.

Summary of Conservation Laws

Experimentally it has been verified that all interactions will always adhere to four fundamental conservation laws:

Conservation of charge, (Q)

Conservation of lepton number, (L)

Conservation of baryon number, (B)

Conservation of quark number.

During particle collisions or when particles decay into other particles, the total quantity (of each above) before the interaction must equal the total quantity after the interaction.

Remember, that quark flavour is not always conserved.

Important, quark flavour is not always conserved during an interaction.
