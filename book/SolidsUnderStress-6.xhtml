Properties of Ductile Metals

Crystalline Materials

A lattice with a repetitive structure characterizes these substances. The particles within the lattice of metals are positive ions and are kept together by a sea of negatively charged electrons, which are also known as de-localised electrons. These electrons can move freely among the lattice ions. All metals are considered crystalline materials.

The image above displays a lattice consisting of a single layer, as well as a 3D lattice comprising two layers. Due to the spherical shape of metal ions, they adopt a hexagonal arrangement. This structure is lost when metals are melted and regained upon solidification. Metals are composed of multiple crystals that grow adjacent to each other, a state referred to as polycrystalline. As metals cool and solidify, each crystal increases in size by attracting additional ions. As crystals develop in various regions, they are limited by neighbouring crystals and are unable to expand beyond their boundaries. Thus, the appearance of a metal is typically similar to the image below, with large crystals known as grains that determine the behaviour of the metal sample.

The grain boundaries have a large component of impurity atoms which are forced out of the grains during crystallisation. Materials called super alloys have been developed that consist of a single crystal.

In ductile materials, an essential characteristic of their structure is the existence of lattice imperfections. These imperfections take the form of edge dislocations, where an extra ½ plane of ions exists, and point defects, either where an atom is absent from or a foreign atom is present within the lattice plane.
