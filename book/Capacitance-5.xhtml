Capacitors can be connected in series and parallel in the same way that resistors can be, and in a similar way, the total capacitance of the combination can be determined. The following two sections will derive the series and parallel combinations.

Capacitors C1 and C2 are connected in parallel, and so, share the same potential difference V. When the two capacitors are fully charged, C1 has a charge of Q1 and C2 a charge of Q2.

On inspection of the diagram on the right, it can be seen that the total charge Q, supplied by the emf source is Q = Q  1 + Q  2 .

The combined capacitance is represented by a single capacitor that will store the same total charge for the same applied potential difference V. The two circuits below can be considered identically equal, where C, represents the combined capacitance.

We can make use of the formula, Q = CV and Q = Q  1 + Q  2 to determine the combined capacitance. Since both C1 and C2 share the same potential difference V, we can write:

CV = C  1 V + C  2 V , where Q = CV , Q  1 = C  1 V and Q  2 = C  2 V .

This reduces to C = C  1 + C  2 .

Useful Ratio for Capacitors in Parallel

Capacitors connected in parallel share the same pd, V, and so, we can make use of V =  Q _ C  .

Recognising that V =   Q  1  __  C  1   and V =   Q  2  __  C  2   , we can write   Q  1  __  C  1   =   Q  2  __  C  2   .

Therefore,   Q  1  __  Q  2   =   C  1  __  C  2   .

This just states that the ratio of charges must equal the ratio of capacitances.

Formulae for parallel capacitors:

C = C  1 + C  2

  Q  1  __  Q  2   =   C  1  __  C  2  

For n capacitors in parallel:

C = C  1 + C  2 + ... + C  n
