Coherent Waves and Sources

Waves or wave sources, which have a constant phase difference between them (and therefore must have the same frequency) are said to be coherent.

Lasers produce a single source of waves that are coherent, meaning that they are monochromatic (single wavelength/frequency) with wavefronts that are continuous across the width of the beam.

Only coherent sources can produce interference fringes, therefore, laser light is a good source of coherent waves to demonstrate Young’s double slit experiment. Interference can only be observed if the coherent sources/waves oscillate in the same direction.

Incoherent Waves and Sources

Waves or wave sources, which have a varying phase difference between them (and therefore must have varying frequency) are said to be incoherent. Incoherent waves cannot form interference fringes.

The light from the illuminated slits is diffracted at each of the two slits, and these slits then act as coherent sources of light. Where the diffracted light waves overlap, they superpose and interfere forming bright and dark fringes. Bright fringes are seen on the screen where the waves arrive in phase, and so, constructively interfere. Constructive interference occurs at points where the path difference =nλ (n = integer). Dark fringes are seen on the screen where the waves arrive in anti-phase, and so, destructively interfere. Destructive interference occurs at points where the path difference =(n+½)λ (n = integer).
