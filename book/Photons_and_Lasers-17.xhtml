Definition of Stimulated Emission

Stimulated emission is the emission of a photon from an excited atom, triggered by a passing photon of energy equal to the energy gap between the excited state and a state of lower energy in the atom. The emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, direction of travel and polarisation direction as the passing photon.

Steps for Stimulated Emission

Atom already excited and at a higher energy level.

Passing photon with energy exactly equal to the difference in the energy between the higher and lower energy level.

Passing photon causes electron to transition down (drop) to lower energy level.

Electron emits photon. Two photons now in total.

Emitted photon has the same phase, energy, wavelength, direction and polarisation as the passing photon.

Incoming photons, however do not just stimulate de-excitation. Incoming photons can make a net positive or negative difference to the laser beam.

Photons have different effects on atoms depending on their energy and the state of the atom. They can either pass through an atom without interacting with it, be absorbed by the atom and cause excitation, or trigger stimulated emission if the atom is already excited.

A laser beam can also be affected by these interactions between photons and atoms. It can either remain unchanged, lose a photon due to absorption by an atom, or gain a photon due to stimulated emission by an excited atom.
