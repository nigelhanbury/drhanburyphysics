Force Between Current Carrying Conductors

A force exists between two long, straight and parallel conductors carrying a current as shown in the diagram on the right. This is because each conductor is within the magnetic field generated by the other. Consider the conductor on the right, carrying a current I2 in the direction shown. This conductor is in the field B1 (into page), which is generated by the conductor on the left, carrying a current I1. Using FLHR, a force must act on the right conductor acting to the left. Similarly, a force must be acting on the left conductor acting to the right. The two forces are equal and opposite owing to Newton’s Third Law.

Interaction Between the Field of a Current Carrying Conductor and External Field

The force acting on a current carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field, such as between the poles of a magnet, can be visualised as the interaction between the magnetic field produced by the conductor and magnetic field produced by the magnets.

Since magnetic field strength (magnetic flux density) is a vector quantity, the resultant field strength at the top half of the wire is greater than the field strength at the bottom half, and is represented by the separation between the lines of flux.
