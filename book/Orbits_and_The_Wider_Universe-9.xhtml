The diagram below, represents a star orbiting the common centre of mass of a planet - star system. The radial velocity, v  rad which is the component of the orbital velocity, v  o directed towards the observer is given by v  rad = v  o cos𝜔t . The variation of v  rad during two complete orbits of the star is shown below. As can be seen, the values that correspond to positions A and B corresponds to the orbital velocity of the star. Positive values of v  rad indicate that the star is moving away from the observer, and negative values, towards the observer. At positions C and D, v  rad is zero as the orbital velocity vector of the star is perpendicular to the direction of the distant observer.

We can use  Δλ _  λ  =   v  rad  __ c  to determine the doppler shift of the measured wavelength by the observer, given by Δλ = (   λ _ c  ) v  rad . The graph is shown above. Maximum blue shift occurs at position A, and maximum red shift at position B. There is no doppler shift at positions C and D as there is no component of the orbital velocity acting towards the observer.

The measured wavelength, λ`    by the observer is given by λ`    = λ + Δλ . The graph is shown above. The mean value gives the unchanged wavelength emitted by the star.
