Let us examine A = λN . As time t, increases, the number of parent nuclei N, must also decrease. Since A ∝ N , it follows that the activity A, decreases.

The equation that describes how the activity A, changes with time t, is given by A = A  0 e  − 𝜆t .

Notice that the decay constant λ, forms part of the decay equation. The graph on the right, represents how the activity changes with time, and is known as an exponential decay.

On inspection of the graph, A  0 represents the initial activity at time t = 0 . As can be seen, when t = T    1 _ 2  , the activity has decreased from A  0 to   A  0  __ 2  . This time interval is known as the half life.

The half life can be defined as the time taken for the activity A, to reduce to one half of the initial value. Unit: s or any unit of time.

As a consequence, the activity can never reach zero. It can only approach zero. The table below, represents how the activity decreases as a percentage of the initial activity.

When using A = A  0 e  − 𝜆t , we can make calculations much simpler by choosing the appropriate unit of time.

For example: If the decay constant λ, is in units of day−1, then it makes sense to use time in units of days. It is important to ensure that the units of λ and t are consistent.
