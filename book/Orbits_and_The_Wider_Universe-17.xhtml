Converting Hubble Constant into S.I. Units

In order to estimate the age of the Universe and make use of Hubble’s Law to calculate recessional velocities, we need to make sure that the approriate units are used for the Hubble constant.

The current measured value of the Hubble constant is approximately 70kms − 1Mpc − 1. We need to be able to express this in terms of S.I. base units. Remembering that a galaxy at a distance of 1Mpc will have a recessional speed (moving away from Earth) at 70kms − 1, we can write the following:

Hubble constant, H  0 =  70km s   − 1   _______   10  6  ×  (3.086 × 1016m)    , where 1pc = 3.086 × 1016m. This translates to H  0 =  70 ×  10  3  m s   − 1   _______________   10  6  ×  (3.086 × 1016m)    = 2.3 × 10   − 18 s   − 1 .

From the Hubble graph, we can estimate the Hubble constant by taking the gradient of the largest triange.

vA and vB represent the rotational velocity of the galaxy at positions A and B respectively, and v represents the recessional velocity of the galaxy.

The minimum radial velocity, vmin corresponds to position B, and the maximum, vmax corresponds to position A.

Firstly, we need to determine the recessional velocity of the galaxy,

v = H  0 D = 68.8 × 650 × 10   − 3 = 44.7km s   − 1 .

Now, v  max = v + v  A = 44.7 + 150 = 195km s   − 1 , and v  min = v − v  B = 44.7 − 150 = − 105km s   − 1 .

Δλ  max = (  195 _____ 3.0 ×  10  5   ) × 650nm = 0.42nm and Δλ  min = (  − 105 _____ 3.0 ×  10  5   ) × 650nm = − 0.23nm .

Therefore, λ  min = 650 − 0.23 = 649.8nm and λ  max = 650 + 0.42 = 650.4nm .
