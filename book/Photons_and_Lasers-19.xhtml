Achieving and Maintaining a Population Inversion

Two Energy Levels

Initially, the population of atoms in a lower excited state, N1 exceeds that in a higher excited state, N2 resulting in a higher likelihood of absorption of incoming photons than stimulated emission. The diagram illustrates a sequence of incoming photons created by optical pumping with the objective of exciting atoms until a population inversion is attained. As shown, the number of excited atoms (depicted in red) increases until the population of excited atoms reaches 50%. However, at this stage, the probability of absorption and stimulated emission becomes equal, resulting in the absorption rate being equal to the stimulated emission rate. This, in turn, prevents any further population growth, leading to no net amplification.

The Three Energy Level Laser System

The limit imposed by a two level system can be overcome with a three level system, in which three energy levels are involved. The lowest (L), highest (P), and between them an upper (U) level. In the diagram on the right, the lowest (L) level has energy, E1, the pumped level (P) has energy, E3, and the upper level (U) has energy, E2.

Pumping

Electrons can be excited from the ground state (L) to the pumped level (P) either by external light or electron collisions. Optical pumping, where the incoming photon is absorbed by the atom, is used in the above example. The electrons quickly drop to the upper level (U), also known as the metastable state, via spontaneous emission. To ensure that the population of atoms in (P) is negligible and to prevent stimulated emission back to (L) caused by pumping photons, (P) has a short lifetime in the order of nanoseconds. On the other hand, (U) has a long lifetime in the order of milliseconds, allowing for an increase in the population of atoms in (U) and progression towards a population inversion between (U) and (L). Note that to achieve a population inversion, more than half of the atoms in state (L) must be pumped to state (U).

A two level laser is not feasible as a population inversion cannot be achieved.

Since N1 = N2, the probability of absorption will equal the probability of stimulated emission.
