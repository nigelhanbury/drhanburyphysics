Electron Beams

The transfer of energy to a charged particle (an electron) is the principle behind the cathode ray tube.

In an electron gun, electrons are released by thermionic emission from a hot wire (the heater), and then accelerated by an electric field between the cathode and the positively charged grid (the anode).

This beam can then be deflected by passing it between other charged parallel plates. We can work out the energy, and therefore, the velocity of an electron accelerated in an electric field between the hot cathode and the anode.

The work done W, in accelerating an electron through a high potential difference V, between the cathode and anode is W = eV .

The work is equal to the increase of kinetic energy Ek, of the electron. Assuming the kinetic energy of the emitted electron at the cathode is negligible, we can write:

eV =  1 _ 2  m  e v  2

Here, v is the velocity of the electron at the anode, and me is the electron mass.

Motion of Charged Particles in a Uniform Electric Field

When the electron enters the electric field, the electron experiences a force FE, given by, F  E = q × (  V _ d  ) where V represents the potential difference across the plates and d is the plate separation.

This force acts in the opposite direction to the electric field E, towards the positively charged plate and vertically down. Therefore, there is no component of the force acting in the horizontal direction, and so, the horizontal component of the velocity 𝜈, remains constant. This results in the electron following a parabolic trajectory.

Outside of the plates, the electron travels at constant velocity as no force is acting. The path followed is exactly like that of a projectile fired horizontally from the top of a cliff.
