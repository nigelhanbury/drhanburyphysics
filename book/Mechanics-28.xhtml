As time increases, the velocity of the parcel increases, and so, the drag force, D increases. The resultant force is, therefore, less than the weight. and .

The drag will continue to increase as the velocity increases. At some point, the drag force will exactly equal the weight and the resultant force will be zero. D = mg and F = 0. The parcel then travels at a constant velocity known as terminal velocity since a = 0.

At the point the chute opens, the drag force, D increases massively, producing a very large resultant force, F acting upwards. This causes a large deceleration, which causes the velocity to decrease. As time increases, the velocity of the parcel decreases, and so, the drag force, D decreases. The resultant force, F is smaller but still acting upwards. The drag will continue to decrease as the velocity decreases. At some point, the drag force will exactly equal the weight and the resultant force will be zero. D = mg and F = 0.
