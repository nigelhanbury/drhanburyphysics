The diagram on the right, represents 2 bodies at different temperatures connected by a conducting rod. Initially, body A, is at a higher temperature than body B.

There exists a temperature gradient between the 2 bodies, and since they are connected via a conducting rod, thermal energy will be conducted away from body A, and transferred to body B. Therefore, we say there is heat flow between the 2 bodies.

The rate of heat flow depends on the temperature difference, and hence, the temperature gradient. The greater the temperature difference between the 2 bodies, the greater the rate of heat flow.

The term heat has a specific meaning in physics. This is energy flow from a region at higher temperature to a region at lower temperature, due to the temperature difference. In thermodynamics, we deal with heat going into or out of a system. It makes no sense to speak of heat in a system. Heat can be considered as energy in transit. Here, the system, refers to the 2 bodies. These 2 bodies could be 2 solid metal spheres, 2 gas filled syringes or any other body.

If a body has a higher temperature than the surroundings, heat is transferred from the body to the surroundings. Likewise, if a body has a temperature less than that of the surroundings, heat is transferred from the surroundings to the body.

Let’s look at the above scenario in more detail. As heat flows from body A, and into body B, the temperature of body A decreases, and the temperature of body B increases. The temperature difference decreases, and so, the rate of flow of heat decreases. As this continues, there will reach a point when the temperature of the 2 bodies are equal. At this instant, the temperature difference is zero, and there will be no net heat flow. The temperature of the 2 bodies remains constant. We say that the 2 bodies are in thermal equilibrium. We use this to define the concept of temperature. When 2 systems are in thermal equilibrium with each other, they are said to be at the same temperature.

Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics (Not required by WJEC)

The concept of thermal equilibrium and temperature can be extended to more than 2 bodies. If body A is in thermal equilibrium with body C, and body B is in thermal equilibrium with body C, then body A and body B are in thermal equilibrium with each other. In this situation, body A and body B have the same temperature.

The law states that, if body A and body B are in separate thermal equilibrium with body C, then body A and body B are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

If the Zeroth Law did not exist, then the idea of temperature would not exist. This law allows thermometers to measure temperature. Bodies A and B would represent 2 thermometers, and body C, represents the object whose temperature is to be measured. Once the 2 thermometers are in equilibrium with C, the 2 thermometers must be at the same temperature as body C, and hence, agree.
