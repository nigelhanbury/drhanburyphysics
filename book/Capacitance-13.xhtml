The time constant RC, can now be defined in terms of a charging capacitor. Setting t = RC .

Therefore, Q = Q  0 (1 −  e   − 1 ) = Q  0 (1 −   1 _ e  ) ≈ 63% Q  0 .

This leads us to state that the time constant is the amount of time required for the charge on a charging capacitor to rise to approx. 63% of its final value.

Definition of Time Constant

For a charging capacitor, it is the amount of time required for the charge on a charging capacitor to rise to approx. 63% of its final value.

Practically, we can consider a capacitor fully charged after 5 time constants, as after 5 time constants, the charge would be 99.3% of its final value Q0.

By inspecting the charge/time graph, it can be seen that the gradient decreases with time, and hence, the current must decrease with time.

The current while a capacitor is charging decreases exponentially and is given by

I = I  0 e   −   t _ CR  , where I0 represents the maximum current.

Considering the pd VC, across the capacitor, V  C ∝ Q , and so, VC must increase as the capacitor is charging.

The equation is represented by V  C = V  0 (1 −  e   −   t _ RC  ) , where V0 is the maximum pd across the capacitor.
