Unit 3, Section 3: Kinetic Theory

Qualitative Explanation of Pressure

Vessel containing gas molecules/atom

Molecules/atoms have a distribution of speeds and directions

It is important to be able to explain, in terms of kinetic theory, how pressure arises in an ideal gas.

The molecules that make up a gas are in a constant state of movement as well as travelling in random directions. There is a random distribution of speeds amongst these molecules, and they continuously collide with each other and the container walls.

Each molecule experiences a change in momentum with each collision with the container wall, and therefore, must experience a force from the container wall.

As there are a large number of molecules, there exists a large number of collisions per unit time between the molecules and the container wall.

The molecules then experience a change in momentum per unit time (or rate of change of momentum), and owing to Newton’s Second Law, must experience a mean force acting on them. Owing to Newton’s Third Law, there must be an equal but opposite mean force acting back on the container walls from the molecules. As this mean force is acting over an area of the wall, and pressure is defined as the force per unit area, there must be a pressure acting the container walls due to the gas.

Main points to cover when explaining pressure:

Constant state of movement of molecules with random directions

Collision between molecules and container wall

Refer to change of momentum of molecule and link with force via Newton’s Second Law

Use Newton’s Third Law to explain that force acts on container wall from molecules

• Link force on wall and area to justify pressure

It is important when asked to explain pressure or changes in pressure using kinetic theory, to include all of the points listed.

The points need to be made in the correct time sequence, using key words spelled correctly.
