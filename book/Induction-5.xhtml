Unit 4, Section 5: Electromagnetic Induction

Fleming’s Right Hand Rule

If the wire passing through the magnetic field is connected to a circuit, then a current will flow.

The induce EMF is given by ε = BLv .

The above equation only applies if the wire is moving through the magnetic field perpendicular to that field.

Referring to the above, the factors that will increase the induced EMF, and hence, current are:

Increasing magnetic field strength (flux density)

Increasing the velocity

Increasing the length of the wire within the field

If the wire is at an angle θ, to the field, then ε = BLvsinθ . Therefore, the maximum EMF is induced when θ = 90° and zero when θ = 0°. In other words, no EMF will be induced if the wire is parallel with the field.

Fleming’s Right Hand Rule can be used to predict the direction of the induced current in a conductor.

• First finger represents the direction of the magnetic field B, in the direction of N to S.

• Second finger represents the direction of conventional current I.

• Thumb represents the direction of the wires movement.
