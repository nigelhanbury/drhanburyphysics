The Four Energy Level Laser System

The below diagram depicts a four-level laser system. Similar to before, electrons are excited to the pumped level (P) by absorbing pumping photons. The excited electrons quickly drop down to the upper level (U), which has a comparatively long lifetime. When a population inversion is achieved, spontaneous emission from state (U) to state (L) results in the emission of a photon having energy E3 - E2. Here, state (L) has a brief lifetime, so electrons occupying this level rapidly return to the ground state. A small population of atoms in the ground state needs to be excited to (U) through (P) to achieve a population inversion, as (L) can be assumed to be devoid of electrons because of its short lifetime.

Efficiency

A four level laser requires much less energy to achieve a population inversion as compared to a three level laser. This is because there are very few atoms in state (L) and only a small number of atoms need to be excited to state (U) via (P) to achieve a population inversion. Consequently, this type of laser experiences less heating and is more efficient..

The four level laser is much more efficient compared to a three level laser as a population inversion is far easier to achieve.
