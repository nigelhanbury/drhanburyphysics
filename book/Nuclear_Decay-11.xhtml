The Link between Half Life and the Decay Constant

By using the equation A = A  0 e  − 𝜆t , we can determine the half life T    1 _ 2  , in terms of the decay constant λ.

When t = T    1 _ 2  , we know that A =   A  0  __ 2  . Substituting these into the decay equation, gives   A  0  __ 2  = A  0 e  − 𝜆 T    1 _ 2  . This simplifies to  1 __ 2  = e  − 𝜆 T    1 _ 2  .

Taking the natural log of both sides, gives − ln2 = − 𝜆 T    1 _ 2  . After re-arranging, gives T    1 _ 2  =  ln2 _ 𝜆  .

Half life

Since A ∝ N , and the activity A, decreases exponentially, it follows that the number of undecayed nuclei (parent) N, must also decrease exponentially, N = N  0 e  − 𝜆t .

The equation is of the exact same form as that for the activity. Here, N0 represents the initial number of undecayed or parent nuclei.

Now we can define the half life more generally, as the time taken for the number of radioactive nuclei, N (or the activity A) to reduce to one half of the initial value. Unit: s or any unit of time.

For the more mathematically minded,  ΔN _ Δt  = − 𝜆N or  dN _ dt  = − 𝜆N .

The negative sign indicates that the number N, is decreasing with time.

N = N  0 e  − 𝜆t is considered a solution to  dN _ dt  = − 𝜆N .

General Definition of Half Life

The half life can be defined as the time taken for the number of radioactive nuclei, N (or the activity A) to reduce to one half of the initial value.

UNIT: s or any other unit.

It is not always necessary to deal with the number of undecayed nuclei, as dealing with the number of moles or mass of a radioisotope is more useful or meaningful in many situations. It may be useful at this point, for you to recap molar mass etc from ‘Kinetic Theory’.
