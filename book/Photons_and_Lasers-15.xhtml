2.8 Lasers

In 1960, the first laser was developed as a result of research and success with the production of the MASER, which used microwaves instead of light. The Nobel Prize for Physics in 1964 was awarded to Charlie Townes, Nikolay Basov, and Alexander Prokhorov for their work on the MASER.

Theodore Maiman is credited with inventing the first functional laser device, which was successfully demonstrated on May 16th, 1960. Lasers have since become commonplace, with devices such as DVDs, CD players, games consoles, and optical disc drives all utilizing laser technology.

The term “laser” is an acronym for Light Amplification by the Stimulated Emission of Radiation. Laser light is unique in that it is coherent, meaning that all photons have the same wavelength and frequency, resulting in a monochromatic beam. Additionally, laser light is plane polarized and forms a collimated or parallel beam, setting it apart from other forms of light. These properties are possible due to stimulated emission, a phenomenon that underpins the creation of coherent light sources like lasers.
