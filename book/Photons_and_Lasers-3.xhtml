The Electron Volt (eV)

At this point, it is useful to define an alternative unit of energy, known as the electron volt, or eV for short. Let us remind ourselves about the definition of potential difference, pd.

As a reminder the potential difference, V, between two points is the energy converted from electrical potential energy to some other form per unit charge. Therefore, if one electron passes through a pd of 1V, then the amount of energy transformed is E=QV=1.6×10-19 C×1V=1.6×10-19 J. In the case of an electron passing between two points with a pd of 1V between them in a vacuum, the electron will accelerate and gain 1.6×10-19 J of kinetic energy.

Definition of Electron Volt

This is the energy transferred when an electron moves between two points with a potential difference of 1 V between them. 1eV=1.6 ×10-19 J. So for an electron being accelerated, it is the kinetic energy acquired when accelerated through a pd of 1 V.
