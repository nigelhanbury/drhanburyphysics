Potential Dividers used as Sensors

Potential dividers can be used with devices, such as thermistors and LDRs. The resistance of these devices change according to changes in temperature and light levels.

Diagram 1 represents a sensor that detects changes in temperature by making use of a thermistor. A thermistor is a semiconducting device that when the temperature increases, its resistance decreases (Known as an NTC thermistor – Negative Temperature Coefficient).

When the temperature increases, Vout increases. The increase in temperature causes the resistance of the thermistor to decrease, therefore, the resistance, R, represents a larger proportion of the total resistance, and so, Vout takes a larger proportion of the input p.d., Vin.

If Vout is connected to an electronic circuit that contains an alarm, the alarm could be triggered when Vout reaches a certain threshold p.d.

Diagram 2 represents a sensor that detects changes in light level. An LDR (Light Dependent Resistor) is a semiconducting device that when the light level increases, its resistance decreases. When the light level increases, Vout increases, with the same explanation as before. Vout could be connected to an electronic circuit within a digital camera to monitor the ambient light level in order to optimise picture quality.

Potentiometer or Variable Potential Divider

The diagram on the right, represents a potentiometer. The sliding contact, S, can be adjusted from position, A, to position, B. As the slider moves from position, A, the resistance, R1 increases and the resistance, R2 decreases. The total resistance R1+R2 remains constant. As a consequence, Vout increases from 0 to Vin.
