We will use the charge equation and graph to analyse the discharging characteristics, though the pd and current can equally be used. Using Q = Q  o e   −   t _ RC  , the index (power) is given by −  t _ CR  .

The property of any index, is that they do not contain any units. Since the time, t is in s, it follows that the constant, CR must also have units of s. Hence, 𝜏 = CR is known as the time constant.

We can determine the physical meaning of the time constant by setting t = CR .

Therefore, Q = Q  0 e   − 1 , and rewritten as Q =  1 _ e  × Q  0 . This leads us to the following definition.

The time taken for the charge to decay to 1/e of its initial value (approx. 37%) is known as the time constant. This can also be quoted in terms of pd or current.

Theoretically, it will take an infinite amount of time for a capacitor to discharge, but practically, we can consider it discharged after 5 time constants, as after 5 time constants, the charge remaining would be 0.67% of its initial value Q0.
