Multi Wavelength Astronomy

In multi-wavelength astronomy, light emitted in many different wavelengths is used to provide information about the activity, distance, chemical composition and structure of the universe.

The Earth’s atmosphere does not allow all wavelengths to reach the ground. As such, access to multi-wavelength astronomy was made possible with observatories in orbit, outside the atmosphere of the Earth.

Multi-wavelength astronomy can be at its most revealing when the same area of space is observed at different wavelengths.

Early photographs were taken in the visible spectrum, which provided only limited information. When utilising other wavelengths, much more information becomes available.

Wavelength

Detail

Radio

Reveals region of gas cool enough for carbon dioxide molecules to exist.

Infrared

Can reveal cool red stars within a galaxy.

Visible

Can reveal stars within a galaxy around the size of our sun.

Ultraviolet

Reveals hot blue stars that are less frequent within galaxies.

X-ray

X-rays are emitted from the hottest regions of gas where atoms are ionized.
