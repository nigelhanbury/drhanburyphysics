Visual Representation of Formation of Stationary Waves

The diagram on the right shows two progressive waves (red and blue) travelling to the right and left respectively. The highlighted wave in green represents the resultant wave or stationary wave.

Using the principle of super positioning, the resultant displacement at any point on the stationary wave is the vector sum of the individual displacements of both progressive waves. Look closely at the diagram to convince yourself.

At the nodal points, the displacement of both progressive waves are always equal and opposite. This demonstrates that the oscillations of the progressive waves at these points are in antiphase, resulting in destructive interference.

Again, on close inspection of the antinodal positions, it can be seen that the oscillations of the progressive waves at these points are in phase, as they reach maximum displacement and pass through the equilibrium position at the same time. This results in constructive interference.

The oscillations shown, represent half a cycle (half period), and therefore, each progressive wave has travelled half a wavelength.

Remember:

Nodes are positions where the amplitude is zero (or minimum)

Antinodes are positions where the amplitude is maximum

The distance between any two consecutive nodes (or antinodes) is equal to half the wavelength
