The Search for Life on Exoplanets

Using the intensity of the starlight at the planet’s surface, the surface temperature of the planet can be calculated using Stefan’s Law.

In reality, this can only be estimated as any cloud cover will reflect a proportion of the starlight, This is known as the albedo effect, and this proportion must be estimated. In addition, the composition of the planet’s atmosphere plays an important role in the greenhouse effect, which results in further increases in surface temperature depending on the proportion of greenhouse gases present.

In order to know if life (at least life as we understand here on Earth) is possible on an exoplanet, the planet must have an atmosphere in order to sustain life, including water along with a surface temperature around 300K. The mass of the planet must be large enough to hold down an atmosphere, but not too large as the gravitational field strength may be too high.

In an attempt to determine this, we need to know the luminosity (output power) of the star and the distance between the star and the planet.

By measuring the star’s intensity, I here on Earth and knowing its distance, r from Earth, we can calculate the star’s luminosity, L using the inverse square law. We can use L = 4π r  2 I .
