Unit 3, Section 6: Nuclear Energy

Binding Energy

Nucleus

Work/energy required to separate nucleons

Nucleus separated into its constituent nucleons

(Gain energy)

The nucleons within a nucleus are tightly bound together via the strong force. Therefore, work needs to be done in order to separate the nucleus into its constituent nucleons. Since work is being done against the strong nuclear force, the nucleons must gain potential energy. This increase in energy, therefore, must lead to an increase in mass.

We can now write ΔE = Δm c  2 , where ΔE , represents the work or energy required to separate nucleons, and Δm , represents the increase in mass. Δm is also known as the mass defect.

The total mass of the separate nucleons is greater than the mass of the nucleus. This goes against intuition, but is absolutely correct.

Likewise, when nucleons come together and form a nucleus, there is a decrease in potential energy, and energy must be released. The decrease in mass is transformed into energy.

This energy is known as the binding energy of the nucleus.

Binding energy is defined as the energy that has to be supplied in order to dissociate a nucleus into its constituent nucleons. [It is, therefore, not energy which a nucleus possesses.] Unit: J [or MeV]. Note, that the binding energy of a single nucleon is zero for obvious reasons.

Unified Atomic Mass Unit, u

Since we are dealing with extremely small masses on the nuclear scale, it makes sense to use an alternative unit of mass, known as the Unified Atomic Mass Unit.

The unified atomic mass unit is defined as exactly one twelfth of the mass of one atom of Carbon-12. Thus, one atom of Carbon-12 has a mass of exactly 12u. ( 1u = 1.66 × 10   − 27 kg )

At this point, it is useful to calculate the energy equivalence of 1u. Using E = m c  2 , and substituting

1u = 1.660538922 × 10   − 27 kg and c = 2.99792458 × 10  8 m s  −1 , gives E = 931.5MeV.

Reminder, that 1eV = 1.6 × 10   − 19 J .

So, the energy equivalent of 1u = 931.5MeV. This is extremely useful when calculated energy released when given the mass defect in units of u.

1u = 931.5MeV

If provided with the atomic mass (mass of an atom), then the mass of the electrons must be subtracted in order to determine the nuclear mass (mass of the nucleus).
