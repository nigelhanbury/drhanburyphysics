Plastic Deformation of a Ductile Material

Plastic deformation arises from an irreversible shift of particles (ions) within a lattice, facilitated by edge dislocations. These dislocations provoke a bond shift between particles in the lattice as they traverse it, causing the material to deviate from its original state. In other words, the material does not return to its original shape/length.

The diagram illustrates a rightward shift of the dislocation, with individual ions undergoing minimal movement. Ions above the edge defect (top three layers) move to a lower potential energy position in the subsequent plane, pushing the additional ½ plane to the right until reaching the grain boundary, resulting in elongation. The stress responsible for this occurrence is the yield stress. Because the dislocation does not revert to its initial location when force (stress) is eliminated, this deformation is deemed plastic.

The next stage in the process of deformation depends upon different factors:

When two edge dislocations meet in a crystal, they may entangle, thereby restricting their mobility.

The size of crystal grains influences the range of motion of edge dislocations, as greater grain size permits more extensive dislocation movement. Hence, larger grain sizes provide greater distance for dislocations to travel.

The presence of point defects:

An edge dislocation's mobility can be impeded by a foreign atom, which can increase the sample's capacity to endure stress.

Lattice voids generate numerous edge dislocations, leading to significant lattice weakening.

By controlling the grain size and the impurities, the mechanical properties are altered:

A material with larger grain sizes exhibits greater ductility, as it can sustain more plastic deformation before failure.

A material with smaller grain sizes tends to be more brittle. Edge dislocations can traverse only a limited distance before reaching the grain boundary. Subsequently, any additional stress will cause the material to fracture without further plastic deformation.
