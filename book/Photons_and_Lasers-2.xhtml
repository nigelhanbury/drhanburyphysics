Einstein’s Photoelectric Equation

The wave nature of light cannot explain the observations made of the photoelectric effect. These can only be explained if we consider light to be made up of particles or photons. The diagram on the right, represents a beam of EM radiation incident upon a metal surface. The incident beam transfers energy in discrete packets (photons) as shown. The photons interact with the surface free electrons and eject them from the surface.

Only a small proportion of the incident photons interact with the free electrons on the metal surface. The remainder are either reflected or absorbed by the metal, which results in a rise in temperature. For those photons that do interact, it is a one to one interaction only. This means that only one photon can interact with one electron. It is not permitted for more than one photon to interact with a free electron or for one photon to interact with more than one free electron. During an interaction, all of the energy of the incident photon is absorbed by the free electron. The photon then ceases to exist. Part of the absorbed energy goes to removing the free electron from the surface of the metal, with the remainder, being the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected photoelectron.

Definition of The Photoelectric Effect

The photoelectric effect occurs when light or ultraviolet radiation of short enough wavelength falls on a surface, and electrons are emitted from that surface.

Definition of The Work Function

The work function of a surface is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface.

Unit: J or eV.

It is important to note, that the photoelectrons will have a distribution of kinetic energies, and it is only the maximum kinetic energy that is relevant to the equation above. Some electrons are deeper within the surface of the metal compared to others, and therefore, require more energy to remove them. This results in the distribution of kinetic energy.

For those photons that do interact, it is a one to one interaction only. In other words, only one photon can interact with one electron.
