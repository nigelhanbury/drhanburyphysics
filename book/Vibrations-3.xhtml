Mathematical Representation of SHM

The displacement/time graph is represented mathematically as x = Acos(ωt) , where ω = 2πf .

Here, the initial conditions are: when t = 0 , x = A .

In other words, time zero is chosen when the spring has been compressed (maximum displacement), and then released.

Alternatively, the initial conditions can be chosen differently. Time zero could be chosen at the point when the mass is moving vertically upwards through the equilibrium position. In this case, x = Asin(ωt) .

Generally, time zero can be chosen at any point within a cycle, and so, the displacement can be expressed more generally as x = Acos(ωt + ε) . Here, the term, ε is known as the phase constant.

Using the initial conditions that produce x = Asin(ωt) , we can rewrite this as x = Asin(ωt) = Acos (ωt −  π _ 2  ) , where ε = −  π _ 2  .

Note, that x = Asin(ωt + ε) can also be used in some older questions and text books.

For WJEC, the general equation that describes how the displacement changes with time is given by:

x = Acos(ωt + ε)

Where ε is known as the phase constant.

Example Problem

A mass oscillates with simple harmonic motion about a central position with an amplitude of 10cm and a period of 0.50s.

The mass is at its lowest position at time t = 0.

The displacement, x of the mass at later times is given by the equation:

x = Acos (ωt + ε)

Rewrite the equation giving the correct numerical values for A, ω and ε.

Solution

The amplitude is 10cm, therefore, A = 10cm.

Using ω =  2π _ T  =  2π _ 0.50  = 12.6 rad s  −1 .

The displacement equation is given by x = − Acos (ωt) , since x = − A at t = 0.

x = − Acos (ωt) = Acos (ωt ± π) .

Therefore, x = 10cos (12.6t ± π) cm .
