When linking gas pressure, volume and temperature to molecular motion, the mean speed is not used, rather, the root of the mean square speed is used.

The diagram on the right, shows the difference between these quantities on the distribution.

Note, that in WJEC, the mean of a quantity is represented by a bar on top of the variable. Eg,  _ x  represents the mean value of x. In some text books, it is represented as < x > .

Consider a gas that contains N molecules, each with a speed c1, c2, c3, … cN. The mean speed is calculated using

 _ c  =   c  1  +  c  2  +  c  3  + ... +  c  N   ______________ N 

Similarly, the mean square speed is calculated using

_  c  2 =   c  1  2  +  c  2  2  +  c  3  2  + ... +  c  N  2    __ N 

The root mean square speed crms, is simply the square root of _  c  2 . In other words, c  rms = √ __  _  c  2   .

Linking Pressure to Molecular Motion

The mean square speed plays an important link with pressure and volume. Below, are the key formula

PV =  1 _ 3  Nm _  c  2   

and

P =  1 _ 3  ρ _  c  2

Here, m represents the mass of an atom/molecule, and ρ, the density.

It is important to note, that the mean square is not equal to the square of the mean.

‾  c  2 ≠  _ c   2   

Example Problem

A box contains just 4 molecules with speeds 300ms−1, 350ms−1, 420ms−1 and 580ms−1.

Calculate:

The mean speed of the molecules.

The root mean square speed of the molecules.

Solution

 _ c  =  300 + 350 + 420 + 580  ___________ 4  = 413m s  −1

c  rms = √ ________________     300  2  +  350  2  +  420  2  +  580  2   ________________ 4    = 426m s  −1
