Kepler’s Second Law: The Law of Equal Areas

Law 2: The line joining a planet to the centre of the sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times.

This implies that, in its orbit, as the distance between the sun and the planet decreases, the orbital speed of the planet increases.

The maximum orbital speed occurs at the perihelion, and the minimum orbital speed at the aphelion.

An ellipse has two focuses (foci) F1 and F2. The semi-major axis is the distance from the centre (halfway between the foci) and the widest part of the ellipse. The semi-minor axis goes between the centre and the narrowest part.

We can use the above diagram to understand why the orbital speed changes. For simplicity, we can consider that the planet covers a distance, x in a time interval of one month. As we can see, the area swept out is given by area x. At a later time within the orbit, the planet must sweep out the exact same area in one month, given by area y. Since area x = area y, the distance, y covered by the planet must be larger. Therefore, the planet covers a greater distance in the same time, and so, has a greater orbital speed.
