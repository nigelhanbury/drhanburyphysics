Initially, the switch is connected to A, in order to fully charge the capacitor. When the switch is then connected to B, the capacitor will discharge through the resistor R. In terms of the flow of electrons, electrons will leave the negatively charged right plate and travel in a clockwise direction through the resistor and be deposited on the positive left plate. For every electron that leaves the right plate, an electron is added to the left plate.

Therefore, the plates remain equal and oppositely charged, while the magnitude of the charge on each plate decreases, and so, the charge stored in the capacitor decreases.

The charge decreases exponentially, as shown in the diagram above. This property will be discussed in detail later.

As the capacitor discharges, current must flow through the resistor, R.

The current I, at any instant is equal to the gradient of the tangent, I =  dQ _ dt  .

On inspection of the diagram above, it can be seen that the magnitude of the gradient decreases with time, and therefore, the current must decrease with time.

Note, that the gradient of the Q/t graph is negative as the rate of change of charge on the capacitor is negative, indicating that the charge is decreasing.

We are only interested in the magnitude, as the rate at which charge is leaving the capacitor must equal the rate at which charge is passing through the resistor, R. The area under the I/t graph gives the charge that has been removed from the capacitor.
