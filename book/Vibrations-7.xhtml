The diagram on the right, represents a pendulum comprised of a spherical mass m, attached to a light inextensible rod. The pendulum is free to oscillate, and the path of the mass is constrained to the curve AB.

The arc length x, represents the distance between the centre of mass of the sphere and the equilibrium position.

Here, the component of the weight mg, acting along the tangent to the curve serves as the resultant force acting towards the equilibrium position. This is valid, as there is no component of the tension T, acting along the tangent.

Note, that the length of the pendulum L, is measured between the pivot point and centre of mass of the sphere.

Applying F = ma along the tangent, gives ma = − mgsinθ , and therefore, a = − gsinθ . Here, the negative sign indicates that the force is acting in the opposite direction to the arc length x.

Assuming the angle, θ is small, and in radians, we can make the approximation, sinθ ≈ θ .

Therefore, a = − gsinθ can be expressed as a ≈ − gθ provided the angle, θ is small.

As θ is in radians, we can write θ =  x _ L  , and so, a ≈ − (  g _ L  ) x . Assuming the angle, θ is very small, we can drop the approximation, and write a = − (  g _ L  ) x .

As before, we can compare the general SHM equation, a = − ω  2 x with a = − (  g _ L  ) x and obtain ω  2 =  g _ L  .

By inspection of the equation, it can be seen that:

The period is independent of the mass m.

Increasing the length L, will increase the period.

Increasing the acceleration of free fall g, will decrease the period.
