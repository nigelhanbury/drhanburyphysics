m1 and m2 are measured in kg and r in m.

The (−) sign is sometimes used in the above formula to indicate that it is an attractive force. This law applies to point masses (or particles) but can be extended to stars and planets. This is perfectly valid, as stars and planets are very nearly spherical and their mass is evenly distributed throughout their body, and so, the distance r, can be treated as the distance between their geometric centres as shown below.

The graph to the right shows the variation of the gravitational force of attraction with separation, and follows the inverse square law F ∝  1 __  r  2   .

In other words, if the distance between the centres r, is doubled, the force F is reduced by a factor of 4.

Some examination questions present a graph of F against r, and you are asked to determine if it obeys an inverse square relationship.

If it does obey this, then the product of F and r2 will be constant for all points.

The best method is to select two points and evaluate the product. If they are equal or very nearly equal, then the graph follows the inverse square relationship.

Definition of Newton’s Law of Gravitation

The gravitational force, F, between two particles is proportional to the product of their masses, m1 and m2, and inversely proportional to their separation squared, r  2 .

The distance r, can be treated as the distance between the geometric centres of planets and stars.
