Using the centre of mass equations, we can write

r  m =  Md _ M+m  , r  M =  md _ M+m  and   T  2  __  d  3   =  4 π  2  ___ G (M+m)   .

The above can be applied to the diagram on the right, where the star and planet have masses, M and m respectively.

The mass of the planet can be considered negligible compared to that of the star (m << M). The following approximations can be made:

r  m ≈ d and   T  2  __  d  3   ≈  4 π  2  ___ GM 

This is the Kepler’s Third Law formula derived earlier for a planet orbiting a star, where the star was located at the centre of the circular orbit of the planet.

In reality, the common centre of mass is located within the body of the star, as shown in the diagram, and the star appears to ‘wobble’.

Example Problem

Mass of sun: 2.0 × 10  30 kg

Mass of Earth: 6.0 × 10  24 kg

Radius of Sun: 696 × 10  3 km

Consider the case of the Earth-Sun system. The Earth-Sun distance is 149.6 million km.

Calculate the distance of the centre of mass of the two bodies from the centre of the Sun. Comment on your answer.

Using the appropriate Kepler’s 3rd law formula, determine the orbital period of Earth.
