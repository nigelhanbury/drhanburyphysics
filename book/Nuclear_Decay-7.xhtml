Electric field Deflection

Another method that can be used to distinguish between the 3 types of radiation, is to examine the deflection paths followed by alpha, beta, and gamma when passed through an electric field. Electric fields will be examined in detail later. The diagram on the right, represents the paths followed by the 3 types of radiation when passing between 2 charged plates.

The positively charged alpha particle will be attracted towards the negatively charged plate, and therefore, be deflected upwards. The negatively charged beta particle will be attracted towards the positively charged plate, and deflected downwards. However, the gamma photon carries zero charge, and so, will not experience any electrostatic forces and pass straight through without deflection.

On inspection of the above diagram, it can be seen that the deflection of the beta particle is significantly greater than that of the alpha particle. Even though the electrostatic force exerted on the beta particle is less than that experienced by the alpha particle, owing to the beta particle carrying a smaller charge, the mass of the beta particle is significantly smaller than that of the alpha particle.

Using a =  F _ m  , where F, represents the force acting on the particle in the vertical plane, and m represents the mass, the acceleration a, in the vertical plane will be significantly larger for the beta particle as its mass is approximately a factor of 7000 times smaller than that of the alpha particle.
