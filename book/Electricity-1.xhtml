2.1 Conduction of Electricity

Electric Charge

This section is a brief recap of electric charge and static electricity. Charge is merely one of the many properties that particles possess. Some particles are attracted towards each other and some are repelled. In order to account for this behaviour, we attribute a property to each particle called charge. It is important to note, that nobody knows exactly what charge is.

When different types of material are rubbed together, they become charged. The list below, show some examples:

Glass rubbed with a silk cloth becomes positively charged

Amber rubbed with fur becomes negatively charged.

The diagram on the right shows the force of attraction and repulsion between charged glass rods. As can be seen, if two charges are of the same type, they repel and if they are different, they attract.

Structure of the Atom

It is important to know the structure of the atom in order to understand how materials become negatively and positively charged.

Each atom is made up of a nucleus, comprised of neutrons and protons. Since the neutron has zero net charge, and the proton has a positive net charge, it follows that the nucleus has a net positive charge. The electrons orbit the nucleus in shells. The electron has a negative charge, and is equal in magnitude to that of the proton. A neutral atom has an equal number of electrons and protons. Therefore, a neutral atom has zero net charge.
