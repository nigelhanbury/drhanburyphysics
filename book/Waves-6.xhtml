The graph represents a displacement-time graph of a single particle on a wave. To the left of the graph is a phasor representation. The vertical component of the rotating phasor (vector) represents the displacement of the particle from equilibrium.

The graph represents a displacement-time graph of two particles on a wave separated by a quarter wavelength. To the left of the graph is a phasor representation. The vertical component of each rotating phasor (vector) represents the displacement of the particles from equilibrium. Here, we can see that the blue phasor is ahead of the red phasor by 90°. This means that the oscillation of the blue particle lead the oscillation of the red particle within a cycle with a phase difference of 90°.

Representation of two particles separated by one wavelength, λ. As can be seen, the two particles are travelling in the same direction at the same speed. Therefore, they occupy the same point within a cycle. These particles are said to oscillate in phase.

Representation of two particles separated by half a wavelength. These particles are travelling in opposite directions at the same speed. We say that the particles oscillate in anti-phase, and have a phase difference of 180º or π radians.
