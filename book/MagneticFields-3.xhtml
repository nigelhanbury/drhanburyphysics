Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

Experiment to Determine Magnetic Flux Density

The apparatus below, can be used to determine the magnetic flux density (Magnetic field strength, B), using a top pan balance.

With the horseshoe magnet placed on the top pan balance, the balance is zeroed by pressing the tare button. A current passes through a straight wire between the poles of a magnet, as shown to the right. Using Fleming’s Left Hand Rule, it can be seen that a force will act vertically upwards on the wire.

Due to Newton’s Third Law, an equal but opposite force must, therefore, act back on the magnet vertically downwards. This will cause a reading to be displayed on the balance.

Using this reading, the magnetic flux density B, can be calculated. The magnitude of the force acting down on the balance due to the wire will be F = BIL , and this will register as the recorded weight, mg.

We can write mg = BIL , where m represents the recorded mass in kg. Therefore, B =  mg _ IL  .

Example Problem

A student carries out an experiment using the above apparatus. The balance is set to zero when no current is flowing. The student uses a variable resistor to vary the current through the wire. For each value of the current, the student records the reading on the balance and plots the graph shown. The length of the wire within the magnetic field is 5cm.

Determine the magnetic flux density between the poles of the magnet.

Solution

We can make use of mg = BIL , and re-arrange to give m = (  BL _ g  ) I .

Here, the gradient of the graph can be seen to be  BL _ g  .

From the graph, the gradient is given by   (2.375 − 0 )  ×  10   − 3   ___________ 5.00 − 0  = 4.80 × 10   − 4 .

Therefore, 4.80 × 10   − 4 =  B ×  (5 ×  10   − 2 )    ________ 9.81  .

This gives B = 0.0942T or B = 94.2mT .

Due to Newton’s Third Law, there must be a force acting on the magnet, which is equal and opposite to the magnetic force acting on the wire.
