Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

Magnetic Field produced by a Current

When a current flows in a conductor, a magnetic field is set up. Magnetism due to the flow of current is known as Electromagnetism.

Magnetic Field Due to a Long, Straight Conductor

When current flows in a long, straight wire, the magnetic field is in the shape of a series of concentric circles around the wire as shown.

The direction of the lines of flux can be found using the Right Hand Grip Rule. Referring to the diagram on the right, position your right hand as though you are gripping the wire. The direction of the thumb represents the direction of conventional current flowing through the wire and your fingers represent the rotational sense of the lines of flux.

The magnetic flux density B, a distance r, from the wire can be calculated using B =   μ  0 I __ 2πr  , where μ0 represents the magnetic permeability of free space and is equal to 4π × 10 − 7 NA − 2.

Here, we can see that the flux density is inversely proportional to the distance from the wire, B ∝  1 _ r  .

Remember, that the distance between the field lines increases with distance from the wire, indicating that the magnetic field strength (magnetic flux density) decreases with distance from the wire.

If there are N wires, each carrying a current I, in the same direction, the total flux density B, is given by B =   Nμ  0 I __ 2πr  . This assumes that the radius of the bundle of wires is negligible compared to the distance r.
