A 2kg mass is hung at the end of a rod and pulled back 40° as shown in the diagram. The mass is released. Determine:

The speed of the mass as it passes through position A

The tension in the rod as it passes through position A

Solution

Using the conservation of energy, mgΔh =  1 _ 2  m v  2 , where Δh = 0.30 − 0.30cos40° = 0.070m

Substituting into the above, gives 2 × 9.81 × 0.070 =  1 _ 2  × 2 v  2 ⇒ v = 1.17m s  −1

The resultant force must act vertically upwards as this is the centripetal force.

T − mg =  m v  2  ___ r  . After re-arranging, T = m (g +    v  2  __ r  ) .

Substituting into the above, gives T = 2 × (9.81 +    1.17  2  __ 0.30  ) = 28.7N .

Example Problem

The setup on the diagram represents a conical pendulum. A 2kg mass is attached to the end of a rope of length 30cm. The mass is given a push so that it performs circular motion in the horizontal plane of radius, r. The angle of the rope maintains a constant 40° as shown. Determine:

The tension in the rope

The time period of the motion

Solution

Resolving vertically, Tcos40° = mg , and therefore, T =  mg _ cos40°  .

Substituting into the above, gives T =  2 × 9.81 _ cos40°  = 25.6N .

Resolving horizontally, rm ω  2 = Tsin40° , as the horizontal component of the tension provides the required centripetal force.

Substituting into the above, and realising that r = 0.30cos40° = 0.23m , gives

0.23 × 2 ω  2 = 25.6 × sin40° ⇒ ω = 5.98rad s  −1 . This gives us the angular velocity.

Using ω =  2π _ T  and re-arranging, gives T =  2π _ ω  .

After substituting into the above, gives T =  2π _ 5.98  = 1.05s .
