Nucleus

Each atom is made up of a nucleus, comprised of neutrons and protons. Since the neutron has zero net charge, and the proton has a positive net charge, it follows that the nucleus has a net positive charge.

The electrons orbit the nucleus in shells. The electron has a negative charge, and is equal in magnitude to that of the proton.

A neutral atom has an equal number of electrons and protons.

The diagram on the right, represents Oxygen.

The chemical symbol for Oxygen is  8  16  O . The bottom number represents the number of protons, and the top number represents the total number of neutrons and protons that make up the nucleus. A neutral Oxygen atom has 8 electrons in orbit.

Generally, for an element X, the chemical symbol is represented as  Z  A  X , where A, represents the mass number (total number of neutrons and protons), and Z, represents the atomic number (number of protons). The atomic number also represents the relative charge carried by the nucleus. It is common to refer to the mass number as nucleon number and the atomic number as the proton number.

It is the atomic number, which distinguishes the elements. In other words, 2 nuclei with different atomic numbers are different elements.

Isotopes of an element have the same atomic number, but different mass numbers. An example of this, are the 2 isotopes of Hydrogen 1  1  H . These are Deuterium 1  2  H , and Tritium 1  3  H . Notice that they all have the same atomic number of 1 (1 proton). This makes them the same element. However, they have differing number of neutrons, and so, different mass numbers.

The term ion, represents an atom that has had 1 or more orbital electrons removed, leaving a net positive charge. The process is known as ionisation.
