Unit 3, Section 1: Circular Motion

Circular Motion and Angular Velocity

Consider a particle moving in a circular path at a constant speed v, as shown in the diagram on the right. At any instant, the velocity acts along the tangent to the path and perpendicular to the radius of the circle.

In a time t, the particle will cover a distance d, equal to d = vt , and the angle swept out being θ. The angular velocity ω, is defined as the angle swept out per unit time, and therefore, ω =  θ _ t  . Here, θ is measured in radians and angular velocity in rads−1.

Using v =  d _ t  and d = rθ gives v =  rθ _ t  = r × (  θ _ t  ) = rω .

The speed can, therefore, be calculated using v = rω .

We know that one complete revolution (cycle) covers 2π radians in a time, T (period), it follows that we can calculate the angular velocity, using ω =  2π _ T  = 2π × (  1 _ T  ) = 2πf , where f =  1 _ T  . Here, f represents the frequency, which is defined as the number of cycles per unit time.

Since ω = 2πf , we can also express the speed as v = 2πrf .

Only circular motion involving constant speed is considered within WJEC, and this is known as uniform circular motion.

Centripetal Acceleration

A particle performing uniform circular motion, must experience an acceleration as the velocity is continuously changing. The speed is constant, but the direction of the velocity vector is changing.

With reference to the diagram to the right, consider a particle moving with constant speed between points A and B. The change in velocity Δv , is given by Δv = v − u .

Note, that the length of the 2 vectors are equal. By inspecting the vector addition diagram, the change in velocity Δv , can be seen to be acting towards the centre of the circle. Since acceleration is given by a =  Δv _ t  , where t, represents the time taken to move from point A to point B, the mean (or average acceleration) can be seen to be acting towards the centre of the circle. In fact, the acceleration at any instant can be shown to be acting towards the centre. This is known as the centripetal acceleration.

The acceleration at any instant can be calculated using a =   v  2  __ r  .

Knowing that v = rω , and substituting into a =   v  2  __ r  , gives a = r ω  2 .

Key Equations :

v = rω

v = 2πrf

a =   v  2  __ r 

a = r ω  2

ω =  2π _ T  = 2πf
