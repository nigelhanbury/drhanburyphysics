Consider an object of mass m, launched from the surface of a planet of mass, M with an initial velocity u, as shown in the diagram on the right. Here, the Earth is used to give context.

At the surface, the kinetic energy is given by E  K =  1 _ 2  m u  2 .

Also, the gravitational potential energy is given by E  P = mV  g = −  GMm _ R  , where V  g = −  GM _ R  .

The total energy is then given by E  T  = E  K + E  P . This gives E  T =  1 _ 2  m u  2 −  GMm _ R  .

Due to the conservation of energy, the total energy must remain constant during the motion of the mass.

At the highest point above the surface, h, the kinetic energy of the mass must be zero. Therefore, the total energy at the highest point must be all gravitational potential energy. We can then write  1 _ 2  m u  2 −  GMm _ R  = −  GMm ____  (R + h)   . This equation can then be solved in order to determine h.

Quite often, it is necessary to determine the minimum initial velocity at which the mass will never return, and this is known as the escape velocity, VESC.

In order for the mass to never return, the mass must effectively tend to an infinite distance from the planet, and reach zero velocity as it does so.

In this case, the gravitational potential energy will reach zero (by definition).

In addition, the kinetic energy must also be zero, and hence, the total energy must be zero.

Since the total energy is conserved, the total energy at the surface must also be zero.

Therefore, we can write  1 _ 2  m V  ESC  2 −  GMm _ R  = 0 , which leads to V  ESC = √ __   2GM _ R    .

The total energy is always conserved. Therefore, the total energy at the surface must equal the total energy at any point during the journey of the mass.
