Power Supplies and Internal Resistance

The diagrams below, represent the circuit diagram and the physical circuit used to connect a battery to a bulb.

Real batteries experience a rise in temperature when a current flows through them. This is why batteries feel warm when they have been in use for a long time. The batteries, or each cell, behaves as though there is a hidden resistance within the casing. This resistance is known as the internal resistance. The internal resistance can be thought due to the components that make up the cell, including the electrolyte.

Investigating Internal Resistance

The internal resistance will affect the applied p.d. to a circuit. The diagram on the right, represents how this can be investigated along with determining the internal resistance

This is simply a series circuit involving two resistors. The internal resistance cannot be seen directly as it is within the body of the cell. We can state E=V+Ir, where I, represents the current flowing through the circuit.

It is common to express the above formula as V=E-Ir.

The term, V is known as the terminal p.d. The voltmeter positions shown will all display the same p.d. [See note below]

The p.d., V across the terminals will therefore, be less than the EMF when a current flows through the cell.

Ir is the p.d. across the internal resistance, r, and is sometimes referred to as the lost volts.

The diagram below, represents how a cell is drawn that includes internal resistance

The red part of the circuit, represents a path of constant electrical potential energy and the blue part of the circuit represents a lower constant electrical potential energy. Therefore, it does not matter where on the red or blue wires we connect the voltmeter, as it will measure the same potential difference.
