1.7 Particles and Nuclear Structure

This topic deals with the very building blocks of matter itself (quarks and leptons). Particle physics is the most fundamental of all the subjects within physics. All the fundamental particles in the Universe are currently predicted by the Standard Model. The table below, represents all the fundamental particles within the standard model and is analogous to the periodic table.

All the normal stable matter we see in the Universe is made up of first generation quarks and leptons and the photon, and these are highlighted in bold above. As particle accelerator technology developed and machines capable of delivering ever increasing energy to particle collisions, the particles produced from collision experiments were of a larger mass and could not be accounted for using the first generation particles. This is what lead to the second and third generations. These exotic particles decay rapidly back into the first generation particles. For WJEC, only first generation will be discussed and examined.

Matter and Antimatter

Every particle has an antiparticle (eg, the positron is the antiparticle of the electron). A particle and its antiparticle have:

The same mass

Equal, but opposite charge

Oppositely directed spin (not on WJEC spec)

For example, the electron has a charge of –e or −1.6 × 10  -19 C and the positron has a charge of +e or +1.6 × 10  -19 C
