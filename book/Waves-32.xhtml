Multimode Optical Fibres

Optical fibres are very thin, glass, cylindrical fibres that are designed to transmit light or infrared radiation. An optical fibre typically consists of a core of high refractive index, n1, surrounded by cladding with a lower refractive index, n2. Light is totally internally reflected down the fibre at the boundary of the two media.

The cladding:

The cladding is made from a transparent material with a lower refractive index than the core, which is essential if total internal reflection is to occur at the cladding-core boundary, and so, keep the light rays within the core.

The cladding keeps the core away from adjacent fibre cores and so helps to prevent crossover of information (signals or data) to other fibres.

The cladding prevents surface scratches on the core which would cause light loss from the core.

There is a maximum possible angle of incidence for light entering the fibre if it is to be totally internally reflected within the core. If this angle is greater than θmax, the angle of incidence at the core-cladding boundary will be less than the critical angle, and the light will not be totally internally reflected. Rays which are parallel to the axis of the fibre take the minimum time to reach the far end of the fibre. Rays which enter at the maximum angle are reflected at the critical angle in the core, and therefore, take the longest time.
