Consider a cylindrical mass resting on a turntable that is rotating at a constant angular velocity ω, as shown below. A light source causes a shadow to be projected onto the screen.

Displacement

As can be seen from the top view, the displacement x, of the shadow from the point labelled 0, is given by x = rsin (ωt) . As the turntable rotates, the shadow will oscillate with SHM, as x = rsin (ωt) is a solution to the general SHM formula a = − ω  2 x .

It is interesting to note, that as the shadow oscillates with SHM, the shadow must experience an acceleration towards 0. This means that the cylindrical mass must also be experiencing an acceleration in the same direction. Since F = ma , there must be a force acting on the mass in that same direction, e.g., vertically down the page. This of course, must be the component of the centripetal force.

Here, we can now see a clear meaning to the angular velocity in relation to SHM. Any particle that oscillates with SHM, can be represented by a rotating vector of length r, as shown in the diagram. The angular velocity ω, is the angle swept out per unit time by the radius r.

The displacement of the particle from the equilibrium position, is merely one of the perpendicular components of r. In this case, the vertical component. If we chose the initial conditions, such that x = rcos (ωt) , it would be the horizontal component. r, represents the amplitude of the oscillation.
