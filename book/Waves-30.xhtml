Total Internal Reflection

Referring to diagram A, as light emerges from the semi-circular glass block, it speeds up, and therefore, refracts away from the normal, i.e,

θ2 > θ1. As the angle of incidence increases, so does the angle of refraction.

Referring to diagram B, as the angle of incidence continues to increase, so does the angle of refraction, until the angle of refraction equals 90º, i.e, θ2 = 90º. At this exact point, the angle of incidence is referred to as the critical angle.

The critical angle can be determined by making use of the formula n1sinθ1=n2sinθ2. Since θ2 = 90º, we can write n1sinθ1=n2 as

sin90º=1.

Therefore, sinθ1=n2/n1 or sinθC=n2/n1.

Referring to diagram C, when the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle, θ1 > θC, refraction no longer occurs, and the incident ray is completed reflected. This is known as total internal reflection.

Definition of Critical Angle

When light approaches the boundary between two media from the ‘slower’ medium, the critical angle is the largest angle of incidence for which refraction can occur. The refracted wave is then travelling at 90° to the normal.

Referring to sinθC=n2/n1, it can be seen that a necessary condition is that n2< n1.

This must be the case as otherwise, sinθC>1, which is a mathematical impossibility.

Requirements for Total Internal Reflection

For total internal reflection to occur:

The light must be travelling through a transparent medium towards the boundary with a transparent medium with a lower refractive index, i.e. the light would travel faster in the second medium. TIR can occur at a glass/air boundary, but not at an air/glass boundary.

The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle. The critical angle for a glass/air or perspex/air boundary is about 42°, given that the refractive index of Perspex/glass is about 1.5.

Uses of Total Internal Reflection:

Endoscopes. These are medical instruments, which allow doctors to view inside the body without the need for surgery.

Fibre optic cables in communications (telephone, cable TV)

Prismatic binoculars
