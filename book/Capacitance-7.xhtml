Charge on 15.0µF capacitor: The pd across BC is 12.0V, therefore, we can use Q = CV = (15.0 ×  10   − 6 ) × 12.0 = 1.80 × 10   − 4 C or 180µC.

Charge on 5.00µF and 10.0µF capacitors: The combined capacitance in series between AD is given by  1 _ C  =  1 __  C  1   +  1 __  C  2   , thus  1 _ C  =  1 _ 5.00  +  1 _ 10.0  ⇒ C = 3.33µF .

The pd across AD is 12.0V, and so, we can calculate the total charge between AD. Q = CV = (3.33 ×  10   − 6 ) × 12 = 40.0 × 10   − 6 C or 40.0µC.

Since these capacitors are connected in series, each capacitor must have the same charge and equal to the total charge of the series combination.

Therefore, the charge on both the 5.00µF and 10.0µF capacitors must be 40.0µC.

The total capacitance of the circuit is given by, C = C  1 + C  2 as the branch AD is connected in parallel with branch BC. C = 15.0 + 3.33 = 18.4µF .

So now, we can use U =  1 _ 2  C V  2 =  1 _ 2  × (18.4 ×  10   − 6 ) × 12  2 = 1.33 × 10   − 3 J or 1.33mJ.

We can use V =  Q _ C  =  40.0 ×  10   − 6  _________ 10.0 ×  10   − 6   = 4.00V .

Alternatively, we could use the potential divider formula, V = 12.0 × (  5.00 _ 10.0 + 5.00  ) = 4.00V . This formula would have been much better to use, had we not calculated the previous parts of the question.

The circuit on the right, shows two capacitors connected in series with a 12.0V supply. One of the capacitors has a capacitance of C, and the other, 3C. Determine the pds, V1 and V2.

V1

V2

Solution

We can make use of   C  1  __  C  2   =   V  2  __  V  1   for capacitors connected in series.

Therefore,  1 _ 3  =   V  2  __  V  1   . This results in V  1 = 3 V  2 .

Combining V  1 = 3 V  2 and 12.0 = V  1 + V  2 , results in V  2 = 3.00V and V  1 = 9.00V .

Alternatively, we can use the potential divider formula, V  1 = 12.0 × (  3 _ 3 + 1  ) = 9.00V .

Then we can easily see that V  2 = 3.00V .
