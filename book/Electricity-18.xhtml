Potential Difference and EMF (Electro Motive Force)

So far, we have looked at the p.d. across a single component and discussed the energy transfers involved. Here, we will look at the p.d. across multiple components in series and parallel along with the energy transfers involved with a cell or battery.

Potential Difference across Components Connected in Series

The circuit on the right, represents two components (resistors) connected in series. As before, we will discuss energy transfer, and now, apply the conservation of energy.

When one coulomb passes through resistor, R1, V1 joules of electrical potential energy is converted to other forms (thermal in this case). Likewise, when one coulomb passes through resistor, R2, V2 joules of electrical potential energy is converted to other forms.

Applying the conservation of energy, the total electrical potential energy converted to other forms when one coulomb passes through both resistors must be V1 and V2 joules.

Therefore, V=V1+V2, since the voltmeter is connected across both resistors. We can say that p.d.s in series add.

EMF (Electro Motive Force)

We have discussed at great length the transfer of electrical potential energy to other forms within a circuit. Here, we will discuss where that electrical energy comes from. In other words, what is supplying the circuit with energy. The device that does this is known as an EMF source, such as a cell or battery.

The EMF of any source of electrical energy is defined as the energy transferred from other forms into electrical energy per unit charge which passes through it and around a complete circuit.

In the above circuit, a cell of EMF, ε, is used to provide energy to the circuit. For every one coulomb that passes through the cell, ε joules of chemical energy (or another form) is converted to electrical potential energy. Therefore, each one coulomb of charge that leaves the cell now has ε joules of electrical potential energy. When each one coulomb of charge passes through both resistors, ε joules of electrical potential energy is converted to other forms, and hence, the p.d. across both resistors is ε. The applied p.d. across both resistors is equal to the EMF of the cell. Note, all the electrical potential energy must be transferred to the circuit by the time each one coulomb of charge returns to the cell.

Definition of EMF

The EMF of any source of electrical energy is defined as the energy transferred from other forms into electrical energy per unit charge which passes through it and around a complete circuit.
