The De Broglie Wavelength

In 1923 Louis de Broglie postulated that “because photons have wave and particle characteristics, perhaps all forms of matter have wave as well as particle properties”. This was a highly revolutionary idea at the time and had no supporting experimental evidence to back it up.

Electron Diffraction

In 1927 the dual nature of electrons was confirmed accidentally by Davisson and Germer who observed electron diffraction from nickel. Electrons can be diffracted by a thin graphite film in a vacuum tube, creating a series of diffraction rings. This shows that electrons have wave properties. The electrons are diffracted by the spacing between carbon atoms because the (de Broglie) wavelength of the electrons is comparable to the size of the spacing between the carbon atoms. The atoms, therefore, behave as a diffraction grating.

The diagram on the right, represents an electron gun that accelerates a stream of electrons across a high potential difference. These high speed electrons behave as particles as they accelerate. As the electrons pass through the thin graphite, the electrons behave as waves and diffract. A diffraction pattern is formed on the phosphorous coating in the form of concentric rings. The reason that rings are formed is due to the thin graphite being made up of randomly orientated crystal planes.
