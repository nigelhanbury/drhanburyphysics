Stationary (Standing) Waves

So far, we have been discussing progressive waves. Remembering that for a progressive wave, the wave profile can be seen to propagate (move forward), and so, transfers energy in the direction of wave travel. The example on the right, represents a stationary wave, sometimes referred to as a standing wave. In a stationary wave, the wave profile does not propagate, and hence, there is no net transfer of energy.

Looking closely at the movement of the rope over one cycle, it can be seen that at different points along the wave, each particle reaches a different maximum displacement. Therefore, the amplitude along a stationary wave varies. At specific points along the wave, the particles do not oscillate. These points are referred to as nodes. Nodes have zero amplitude. The points that have maximum amplitude are referred to as antinodes.

The diagram on the right shows a graphical view of the stationary wave on the rope. Below, are the properties:

Nodes(N): Positions where the amplitude is zero (or minimum).

Antinodes(A): Positions where the amplitude is maximum.

The distance between any two consecutive nodes (or antinodes) is equal to half the wavelength.

All points between two nodes oscillate in phase.

All points either side of a node oscillate in antiphase.

Formation of a Stationary Wave

Two progressive waves travelling in opposite directions with equal or almost equal amplitude and equal frequency pass through each other, superpose, forming a resultant wave made up of nodes and antinodes, where nodes are points of zero (minimum) amplitude, where the oscillations of both waves are in anti-phase, and antinodes being points of zero maximum amplitude, where the oscillations of both waves are in phase.

In the rope example above, a hand initiates a progressive wave travelling towards the wall and reflects at the fixed end. The incident and reflected wave pass through each other and produces a stationary wave. Since the rope is fixed at one end, no oscillations can occur, and therefore, a node must exist at the fixed end.
