Applications of Circular Motion - The Hump back Bridge

Consider a ball travelling over a hump back bridge with a speed v, as shown to the right.

At the instant the ball is shown, the radius of curvature is r. The resultant force must act vertically downwards, as this is the centripetal force.

Let the resultant force F, be F = mg − N . Note, that mg > N .

Applications of Circular Motion - Banked Track

The diagram below, represents a ball of mass m, rolling along a frictionless banked track with a speed v. The ball is performing uniform circular motion in the horizontal plane, and the path of the ball follows the central marked line. The inclination of the bank is θ, to the horizontal.

In this situation, the motion in both the vertical and horizontal planes have to be considered.

Vertical Plane

Since the ball remains at a fixed vertical height above the ground, the resultant force in the vertical plane must be zero. In other words, the vertical component of the normal contact force N, must balance the weight mg.

Ncosθ = mg

Horizontal Plane

Since the ball is moving with uniform circular motion of radius r, in the horizontal plane, there must be a resultant force acting towards the centre, in the horizontal plane. This centripetal force is provided by the horizontal component of the normal contact force N. The weight mg, makes no contribution as it is perpendicular to the pane.
