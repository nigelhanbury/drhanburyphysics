The diagram on the right, represents the first 5 energy levels of Hydrogen. n = 1, represents the lowest energy level of Hydrogen, and hence, is deemed the ground state. If the atom has an energy greater than the ground state, we say that the atom is in an excited state. When an atom is in an exited state, say the nth state, the atom can return to a lower energy state and emit a photon of energy equal to atoms decrease in energy.

For an atom in its nth energy level, there are multiple routes the atom can take back to ground state. For an atom in its 5th energy level, here are some of the possible transitions, etc.

Excitation

An atom can make the transition from a lower energy state to a higher energy state by absorbing a discrete amount of energy. This process is called excitation, and the resulting atom is said to be in an excited state. Such an atom may return to its ground state by the emission of one or more photons depending on the route taken. Excitation can occur when a fast-moving electron collides with an atom. This happens in sodium street lights and fluorescent tubes, as the atoms in the vapour are excited by collisions with accelerated electrons.

It is interesting to note, that all transitions down to n = 2 within Hydrogen will result in the emission of photons in the visible spectrum. The corresponding emission lines are known as the Balmer Series. This is not on the WJEC specification.

Elements in General

The diagram on the right, represents a Helium atom and its emission spectra. Here, the total energy of the atom must take into account the energy of both electrons, and again, the total energy is quantised. An atom is said to be in an excited state if one or more of its electrons is occupying an energy level which is higher than another, lower, energy level available to it. When an atom is in an excited state, at least one electron could drop down to a lower level, and in doing so, emit a photon.

Ground State

There are rules (quantum rules) governing the maximum number of electrons that are allowed at each level. An atom is said to be in its ground state when every electron is in the lowest level available to it.
