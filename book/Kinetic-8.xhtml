Molecular Motion and Gas Pressure

Vessel containing gas molecules/atom

Molecules/atoms have a distribution of speeds and directions

The properties of an ideal gas can be explained in terms of the interaction and motion of the molecules, which make up the gas. The bullet points below, represent the assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases.

The collisions between molecules and the wall of the container are elastic.

The forces between molecules are negligible except during collisions.

The duration of a collision is negligible compared to the time of flight between collisions.

Between collisions, a molecule moves with uniform velocity.

The volume occupied by the molecules themselves is negligible compared to the volume occupied by the gas.

There is a random distribution of energy among the molecules.

There is a large number of molecules in permanent motion.

The molecules that make up a gas are in constant motion, moving in random directions, and have a distribution of molecular speeds.

The distribution to the right is known as the Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution. Notice that the peak of the distribution moves to the right for increasing temperature. The area under the graphs remains constant.

Typically, gas molecules have a mean speed around several hundred meters/sec, though due to the nature of the speed distribution, a small percentage will have speeds much less than this, and a small percentage much greater than this. The pressure that a gas exerts on the walls of a container is a result of the many collisions between the molecules and the container walls.
