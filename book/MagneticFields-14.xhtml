Unit 4, Section 4: Magnetic Fields

The Cyclotron

The cyclotron is another way of accelerating charged particles to high velocities. Here, both electric and magnetic fields are used to accomplish this task. The general structure comprises two semi circular sections, known as Dees, placed perpendicular to a magnetic field. An ion source at the centre releases the charged particles into the Dees, and accelerated to high velocities by applying a high frequency AC source across the Dees.

Since the ion is injected into one of the Dees, it follows a circular trajectory as the magnetic force acting on the ion is the required centripetal force. Applying FLHR will confirm the rotational sense shown in the diagram.

At the instant shown in the diagram, the left and right Dees have a negative and positive potential respectively. The ion is then accelerated across the gap between Dees, and is said to have received a "Kick".

Since r =  mv _ BQ  , and therefore, r ∝ v , it follows that the radius of curvature increases as the velocity of the ion increases, and hence, the path of the ion spirals outwards. Half a cycle (period) later, the ion reaches the top of the Dee, and the polarity must reverse in order for the ion to be accelerated across the gap.

We can now see that the ion will receive two "kicks" every cycle. Therefore, the period, T of the AC source must match the period of the circular motion of the ion, given by T =  2πm _ BQ  . The ion will continue to gain kinetic energy over multiple cycles until it eventually exits the cyclotron. It is important to note, that as the period, T is independent of the ion's velocity, the ions will spend the same amount of time in each Dee, and hence, arrive at the gap at the same point within the cycle.

The frequency of the AC source can be determined using f =  1 _ T  =  BQ _ 2πm  . Once the AC source is set to this frequency, the ion will receive an acceleration burst twice every cycle.
