1.6 Using Radiation to Investigate Stars

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

The diagram on the right, represents the electromagnetic spectrum, where the visible region occupies a very small range. The EM spectrum is split into seven regions, where each region represents a range of wavelengths. Each region has its own properties. The longest wavelengths are radio waves, which can be kilometres in length, down to gamma rays in the region of 10  -12 m . The part of the spectrum that our eyes can perceive, is known as the visible spectrum, and ranges from 400nm to approximately 700nm.

The visible spectrum, and indeed, the entire electromagnetic spectrum plays an important role in the study of astronomy and astrophysics, particularly in determining the composition and temperature of stars.

Continuous and Emission Spectra

All solids or dense matter that have a temperature above absolute zero (0K) emit a continuous range of wavelengths. If the temperature is high enough, the full visible spectrum will be observed if the emitted light is passed through a prism or diffraction grating. The diagram on the top right, shows such a spectrum after light from a hot body is passed through a diffraction grating (More in unit2). Hot gasses, on the other hand, do not emit a continuous range of wavelengths, rather, they emit specific wavelengths as a result of atomic energy transitions (More in unit2).

The diagram to the right, represents such a spectrum in which light emitted from a cloud of hot hydrogen gas is passed through a grating. As can be seen, bright coloured lines appear on a dark background. Each line corresponds to a specific wavelength. This type of spectrum is known as an emission spectrum. Different elements have different characteristic lines.

The diagram on the right, represents the emission spectrum produced by hot helium gas. As can be seen, a different characteristic set of lines compared to that of hydrogen.

This is particularly useful, as by analysing the emission spectra of hot gases, the chemical composition can be determined as the emission spectrum produced by each element act as a signature.
