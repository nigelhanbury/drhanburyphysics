Unit 3, Section 4: Thermal Physics

Specific Heat Capacity

We can apply the First Law of Thermodynamics when supplying heat energy to a solid or liquid.

As a reminder, ΔU = Q − W . For solids and liquids, the volume can be considered unchanged after heating, so W = 0 . This results in ΔU = Q . In other words, the heat supplied is equal to the increase in the internal energy.

For a solid or liquid, we can write Q = mcΔθ , where m represents the mass in kg, c represents a constant known as the specific heat capacity (defined below), and Δθ represents the change in temperature in °C or K. The specific heat capacity is a property of a material.

Definition of Specific Heat Capacity

The specific heat capacity is defined as the heat required, per kilogram, per degree Celsius or Kelvin, to raise the temperature of a substance.

Consider 2 bodies, of differing initial temperatures, in direct thermal contact. We can use the First Law of Thermodynamics to calculate the temperature reached when in thermal equilibrium.

Let θA and θB represent the initial temperature of bodies A and B respectively. Since body A is at a higher temperature, heat will flow from body A to body B. This will cause a decrease in θA, and an increase in θB.

Assuming no heat loss to the environment, we can apply Q = mcΔθ to both bodies. It is much easier to deal with such calculations by considering the heat lost by body A equalling heat gained by body B. When equilibrium is reached, both bodies will have the same temperature, θE.

Let mA and mB, represent the mass of bodies A and B respectively, and cA, cB, the respective specific heat capacities.

Heat lost by body A is m  A c  A ( θ  A  −  θ  E )

Heat gained by body B is m  B c  B ( θ  E  −  θ  B )

Heat lost by body A = Heat gained by body B

Therefore, we can write m  A c  A ( θ  A  −  θ  E ) = m  B c  B ( θ  E  −  θ  B )

Solving the above equation leads to the equilibrium temperature reached.

When trying to find the equilibrium temperature, use

Heat lost by body A = Heat gained by body B
