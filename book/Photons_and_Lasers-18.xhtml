Population Inversion

To ensure an increase in the number of photons at every step, it is crucial to establish a scenario where the probability of stimulated emission surpasses that of absorption. This requires a higher population of atoms in a higher excited energy state in comparison to those in a lower excited or ground state, known as population inversion. If population inversion does not occur, only absorption can occur. N2 must be greater than N1 for population inversion to take place, where N2 denotes the population of atoms in a higher energy excited state, and N1 represents the population of atoms in a lower energy (or ground) excited state.

The diagrams on the right illustrate the situations for absorption and stimulated emission. In scenario A, where only a small fraction of atoms are in a lower excited state, some of the photons generated by stimulated emission may be absorbed, but the outcome will still be amplification. In contrast, in scenario B, a minor percentage of atoms will engage in stimulated emission and release photons. Nonetheless, as a large proportion of atoms are in a lower excited state, these photons will be absorbed, resulting in net absorption.

Pumping

Population inversion is contrary to the natural tendency of atoms, which favour occupying lower energy states than higher ones when there is no net input of energy (thermal equilibrium). However, a net input of energy can invert this scenario, continuously exciting atoms into a higher energy state and maintaining a population inversion. This process is referred to as pumping, which can be accomplished using light (photons with suitable energy), fast-moving electrons (current), or other methods (such as chemical methods, not mandatory for examinations).

Definition of Population Inversion

A population inversion is a situation in which a higher energy state in an atomic system is more heavily populated than a lower energy state (i.e. a less excited state or the ground state) of the system.

Definition of Pumping

Pumping is feeding energy into the amplifying medium of a laser to produce a population inversion.

Pumping is essential as it excites atoms to a higher energy state, thus maintaining a population inversion. This ensures that the population of atoms in a higher energy excited state is significantly greater than those in lower energy excited states. Stimulated emission is then significantly more probable than absorption.
