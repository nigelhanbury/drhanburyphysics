Unit 4, Section 2: Gravitational Fields of Force

Gravitational Field Lines and Equipotential Lines/Surfaces

Gravitational Field Lines

The gravitational field lines shown on the right, represent the path a small test mass would follow if allowed to move freely within a gravitational field.

Properties:

The arrows represent the direction that the force would act on a small test mass. In the case of a point mass and planet, the force will act radially towards the centre. This is known as a radial field.

The smaller the distance between the lines, the greater the gravitational field strength.

Viewing a planet, such as the Earth, at some considerable distance as shown to the right, the field lines are clearly radial.

However, at much closer distances, such as our normal viewpoint of standing on the surface, the surface appears flat, and so, the field lines will effectively be equally spaced and parallel, indicating that the gravitational field strength is uniform.

In reality, it can never be exactly flat, but appears so as the curvature of the surface at this scale is so incredibly small it’s not detectable to the inhabitant on its surface.

Field lines at the surface of the Earth are parallel and equally spaced, indicating a uniform gravitational field strength.

Gravitational Field Lines at Surface of a Planet

The gravitational field lines at the surface of a planet are:

Parallel to each other

Equally spaced
