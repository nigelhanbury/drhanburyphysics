PI Mesons

The table below, represents mesons composed of only first generation quarks (u) and (d). Pi mesons are known as pions and have the symbol, π.

As with baryons, we have mesons with identical quark structure, but different masses (due to their higher excitation levels). These are defined as different particles.

All mesons are unstable, and so, have short mean lifetimes before decaying to form other particles.

The mean lifetime of π  + is ≈ 3 × 10  -8 s .

Neutral mesons have particularly short mean lifetimes, as neutral mesons are composed of a quark and its antimatter equivalent, and so, annihilate after a very short period.

The mean lifetime of π  0 is ≈ 9 × 10  -17 s .

For both baryon and mesons, quark number is conserved. As usual, anti-quarks count as -1. Mesons, consisting of a quark and an anti-quark pair, have a total quark number = 0.

It is important to note, that the type of quark (known as quark flavour) is not always conserved during an interaction.
