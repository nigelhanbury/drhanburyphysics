We can now revisit the rectangular wire loop entering a magnetic field as shown on the right. We can make use of Fleming’s Right Hand Rule to predict the direction of the induced current.

Consider the loop entering the field. The two sides of the loop that are cutting the lines of flux are labelled A and B respectively. Using FRHR on side B, we can see that the direction of the induced current must be acting vertically upwards along side B. Therefore, the direction of the induced EMF must be acting in the same direction. The diagram has been labelled with the equivalent cell symbol to indicate the direction of the EMF. As can be seen, the current must flow in the anti - clockwise direction.

When the loop is fully within the field, we can apply FRHR to side A, and the direction of the induced EMF is shown on the diagram. The net EMF within the loop is now zero, and hence, no current flows.

Lastly, the loop exits the field, and side B is no longer within the field, and hence, no EMF is induced across side B. Side A is still within the field, and so, the direction of the induced EMF is shown. Here, the current must flow in the clockwise direction.

The diagram on the right shows a circular wire within a magnetic field acting into the page. The field is increasing in strength and results in a induced current in the direction shown. We can use FRHR to predict this direction.

Consider point P on the diagram. As the field strength increases, the lines of flux become more densely packed. In other words, the lines of flux become closer together.

This causes the lines of flux to move radially inwards across the wire loop. The blue arrow shows the direction the lines of flux move at point P.

This is the equivalent of that part of the wire at P moving to the right.

With this in mind and applying FRHR to the part of the wire at P, produces an induced current represented by the red arrow. Therefore, the induced current flows in an anti - clockwise direction.
