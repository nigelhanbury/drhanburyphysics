How to Determine Binding Energy of Nucleus

m  PROTON = mass of free proton

m  NEUTRON = mass of free neutron

m  NUCLEUS = mass of nucleus

m  CONSTITUENTS = Total mass of constituent nucleons

 Z  A  X

If provided with the atomic mass (mass of an atom), then the mass of the electrons must be subtracted in order to determine the nuclear mass (mass of the nucleus).

As stated previously, the total mass of the constituent nucleons (separated nucleons) must be greater than the mass of the nucleus as energy was needed in order to separate the nucleons, and this energy has mass.

The first step is to determine if we need to take into account the orbiting electrons. If the mass of the atom is provided, then we must subtract the total mass of the orbital electrons in order to find the mass of the nucleus.

Let m  ATOM , represent the mass of the atom represented by a general element,  Z  A  X .

Therefore, m  NUCLEUS = m  ATOM − (Z ×  m  ELECTRON ) . This is because, for a neutral atom, there are Z orbiting electrons.

Now that we have the mass of the nucleus, we can determine the mass defect (or mass difference), Δm = m  CONSTITUENTS − m  NUCLEUS .

Therefore, Δm = (Z ×  m  PROTON  +  (A − Z)  ×  m  NEUTRON ) − m  NUCLEUS .

If the individual masses are provided in kg, then the binding energy can be determined in J, using BE = Δm × c  2 .

If the individual masses are provided in u, then the binding energy can be determined in Mev, using BE = Δm × 931.5 .

How to Determine Energy Released in a Nuclear Reaction

In order for energy to be released in any nuclear reaction, the total mass of the products must be less than what you started with.
